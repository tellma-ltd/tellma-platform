- **Deletes** (`Crud/Delete/DeleteOperation.cs`, `DeleteRun.cs`, `DeleteRun.Persist.cs`).
  1. Before any round trip: ids de-duplicated in request order, `MaxIds`, the stamp list's
     length, the Delete grant's type-level check; an empty list then answers `AffectedResult(0)`
     with no round trip.
  2. Nothing validates → one guarded `Persist` batch: `DeleteSpec.ByIds` / `WithDescendants` with
     the caller's Read filter and the Delete grant's filter (spec 0012 checks both inside the
     transaction), access guards, the service's and effects' `ContributeAsync` through a
     `DeletePersistContext`, and `AfterCommitAsync`.
  3. Validated → round trip 1 loads the targets under the read filter and counts them under the
     grant (the two-stage pre-check) beside the validators' first loads; `ValidateDeleteAsync` and
     the components run their rounds; then the same persist (round trip 2).
  4. By query: blank filter and negative `ExpectedCount` are `BadRequestException`; the filter is
     discovered and compiled before any round trip (diagnostics relocated to `filter`); no
     validation hook. The persist counts the matched rows (capped one past the cap) beside
     `DeleteSpec.ByQuery(Cap, ExpectedCount)`. `PersistFailures` translates `50413` into
     `LimitExceededException(MaxDeleteByQueryRows, counted, cap)` and `50428` into
     `CountMismatchException(expected, counted)`, using the count the failed batch still completed.
  5. `Fk.InUse` from `ForeignKeyViolationException.Conflict` (never re-parsed): the referencing
     entity and property, at `Ids[0]` for one id, at `Ids` for several, at the root by query.
- **Actions** (`Crud/Actions/ActionOperation.cs`, `ActionRun.cs`, `ActionRun.Rounds.cs`,
  `ActionRun.Persist.cs`, `ActionInvoker.cs`).
  1. Resolution: the action by name from the stack's descriptor (unknown →
     `InvalidOperationException`); the overload must match the method (`ExecuteActionAsync<TResult>`
     for a value-returning method whose result type is assignable to `TResult`, the plain overload
     for a method returning none; else `InvalidOperationException`, no round trip); a
     `SingleTarget` action takes exactly one id (`InvalidOperationException`).
  2. Arguments: a JSON element is deserialised into the method's record (bad JSON, JSON `null`, or
     JSON for an action without arguments → `BadRequestException`; a record for an action without
     arguments → `InvalidOperationException`), then walked for its attributes, errors at
     `Arguments.<Property>`.
  3. Built-in `activate`/`deactivate` with nothing validating actions: one SQL-only round trip,
     `UpdateSpec.ByIds(IsActive, Stamp: true, AllOrNothing: true)` under the read filter and the
     Activate grant, then the read-back when asked. Rows already in the state are not stamped.
  4. The general path: round trip 1 loads the targets and runs the two-stage check beside the
     validators' first loads; `ValidateActionAsync`, the components, then the method run over one
     `ActionContext` with their rounds. The method's and components' appends go through a deferred
     `RecordingBatch` (no batch exists yet) composed onto the persist in order. The persist then
     holds `context.Save` rows (`ConcurrencyMode.Override`), the stamped `Update`s, the post-check
     for `PostCheck` actions over saved ids and target ids, `ContributeAsync`, the invariants and
     the read-back.
  5. `ActionContext.Persisted`: reading it marks the reader's task as leaving the rounds (an
     `AsyncLocal` gate); the task leaves once the reading flow stops running on its thread, so the
     persist is sent while the method waits; it completes after the commit (faults with the
     failure that kept the persist from running). From the read on, the task's loads, saves,
     updates and appends are refused (review 1).
  6. `PartialFailureException` from the method persists what was composed, then is rethrown.
- **Deferred recording batch** (`Data/Batches/RecordingBatch.cs`). `RecordingBatch.Deferred()`
  records without an inner batch; its first `Replay` composes the recording. Its `Tvp`,
  `DeclareIdTable` and `DeclareKeyTable` name their tables themselves (`@tm_rec<n>_…`) and every
  replay declares them under those names through `IBatchNamedTables`; an append that answers at
  once from its batch (`Save`, `Delete`, `FromCache`) is refused there (review 1).
- **Proven commit** (D-116). A delete answers the ids it was asked to delete; an action answers its
  ids, read back by a plain `Read` when asked. A read failure is logged (`ProvenCommitReadFailed`),
  a void method failing on a result the lost batch never answered is logged
  (`ActionMethodFailedAfterCommit`), and the ids answer.
  An update-only or unknown ambiguous end is `DependencyUnavailableException`.
- **Wiring**: `EntityPipeline` forwards the five methods after argument checks;
  `EntityPipelineFactory` and `CrudComposition` register `DeleteOperation` and `ActionOperation`.
- **Docs**: `src/core/Tellma.Core/README.md` gains "The delete and action pipelines" and the
  `Crud/Delete/` and `Crud/Actions/` folder rows. ARCHITECTURE.md needed no change.

### Deviations

1. **Any override moves both built-ins.** A service or component overriding
   `ValidateActionAsync` sends `activate` and `deactivate` through the general path, even when the
   override ignores built-in actions; a per-action rule cannot be read from the class.
2. **`Fk.InUse` location**: `Ids[0]` for one id, `Ids` for several (the database names no row), the
   root for a delete by query.
3. **Outcome stamp**: a delete's `PersistOutcome.Stamp` is the clock time after the commit (no
   single row stamp exists for a delete). An action's is the stamp the rows `context.Save` marked
   carry; an update-only action's is the clock time after the commit, since its update stamps
   come from SQL (narrowed in review 1).
4. **The cap message** of `LimitExceededException` is the generic one; it does not suggest running
   the delete as a background job (no such job exists yet).
5. **Stamped deletes of a long-keyed stack** are refused with `BadRequestException` before any
   round trip, since the data layer compares stamps of int ids alone (`IdStampList` carries int
   ids). No long-keyed stack has deletes with stamps today. Spec 0012 has no long-id stamp list,
   a gap for Ahmad (review 1, D-117 Q4).
6. **Id handling**: ids are de-duplicated in request order; an empty list takes no round trip on a
   delete or the fast path; a stamp list of another length than the ids is `BadRequestException`;
   a negative `ExpectedCount` is `BadRequestException`.
7. **Delete by query runs no `ValidateDeleteAsync`**: there are no target ids to give it; the cap
   and the count are the guards.
8. **A deferred action batch refuses the appends that answer at once from their batch** (`Save`,
   `Delete`, `FromCache`): the method composes before the persist batch exists, and the stack's
   rows go through `context.Save` / `context.Update`. Its table-valued parameters and declared
   tables are named by the view in the reserved recordings' namespace
   (`ReservedSqlNames.RecordingPrefix`: `@tm_rec<n>_t`, `@tm_rec<n>_ids`), not `@tb{b}_t{n}`, and
   keep those names in every batch the recording is replayed onto; a batch takes one recording's
   named tables, and a second is refused (review 1, verification V-5/V-6). Spec 0018 §invite's
   `@tb{b}_t0`/`@tb{b}_t1` read as illustrative names.
9. **`Persisted` gating**: reading `Persisted` marks the task as leaving the rounds (an
   `AsyncLocal` gate), and the task leaves once the reading flow stops running on its thread,
   detected by the gate's context switching away; the spec's condition is "parked on
   `Persisted`". From the read on, a load, a save, an update or a `Batch` append is refused.
   Raised before the flow stops running, the refusal faults the flow first and the rounds fail,
   so nothing is sent. **Residual (D-118, for Ahmad):** a plain `Task` tells no await of
   `Persisted` from an await of anything else, so a flow that awaits something else before
   composing may compose after the persist was sent; the action then commits without that write.
   Within the current contract this is made loud, not prevented: every refusal is recorded on the
   run whichever flow raised it; one recorded before the persist is sent abandons it; one recorded
   after is logged (`ActionComposedAfterPersisted`, Error) and raised once the action committed,
   for void and value actions alike, so it is never answered as success; the hook's and the
   components' released flows are awaited after the commit, as the method is, and their other
   failures are logged (`ActionTaskFailedAfterCommit`); an error reported after the rounds is a
   `ValidationException` before the send and raised after the commit. A child flow nobody awaits
   that composes after the answer is still lost silently. Closing it needs a custom awaitable for
   `ActionContext.Persisted`, a spec 0015 §7.1 type change (review 1, verification V-1).
10. **Re-entry keeps round trip 1's verdicts** (D-116 R2-2): a re-entered persist re-runs the hook
    and components in memory, never re-invokes the method, and replays the recordings; the target
    check is not redone in C#, but the persist's own spec 0012 checks run again inside the
    transaction.
11. **Read denied on a write is not found**: the Read-Denied branches in `DeleteRun.Spec` and
    `ActionRun.ComposeUpdates` answer `NotFoundException` for every id, as hidden equals missing.
    It is reached whenever the caller's Read is denied: on a cacheable stack, whose Read takes no
    filter, by a filtered write grant (covered by an integration test), and on any stack by a
    write grant holding a criterion alone (review 1, judgment 6).
12. **A method failing after the commit**: a method that answers no value and fails after its
    action committed, normally or by a proven commit, is logged (`ActionMethodFailedAfterCommit`)
    and the ids answer. A method answering a value of its own has none to give, so its failure is
    raised after the post-commit phase, though the action committed; Ahmad chooses between a
    closed-set "committed, result lost" code and requiring such methods to be idempotent on retry
    (review 1, D-117 Q5).
13. **By-query diagnostics** are relocated from the engine's composed `Filter` / user-filter
    locations to `filter`, the request's property.
14. **Action outcome**: `AffectedIds` is the bound key table of the target ids; `Captures` is
    empty (actions capture no blobs).
15. **The post-check** of a `PostCheck` action covers the saved ids and the target ids.
16. **An update-only ambiguous commit** (activate, deactivate, any action that inserts nothing) is
    `DependencyUnavailableException`, as a save's.
17. **The general path runs with empty ids**: the hook, the components and the method run and
    their persist is sent (an action may act on no target, and still save rows of its own); only
    the fast path and a delete answer an empty list without a round trip.
18. **The typed overload** accepts a method whose result type is assignable to `TResult`
    (`ExecuteActionAsync<object>` works for any value-returning action).
19. **A count mismatch whose count was not read** passes through untranslated rather than report a
    count nobody read.
20. **Assignments.Concurrent** stays local in `PersistFailures` (D-114), unchanged.

### Open risks

- **`AsyncLocal` gate**: a flow that reads `Persisted` while another flow of its task is parked at
  a load fails the rounds; one that parks after the read is refused. A flow that reads
  `Persisted`, awaits something other than it, and then composes is refused and raised after the
  commit (deviation 9), but the commit has happened without that write; only a custom awaitable
  for `Persisted` (D-118) prevents it. The gate also leaves when the reading flow synchronously
  completes a source whose continuation runs inline under another context; a refusal after that
  is recorded the same way.
- **Integration seeds** use fixed ids per test (2xx lookups, 3xx nodes, 5xx widgets, 9xxxxx
  exports). A rerun against a reused database would collide; the fixture creates fresh databases
  per run.
- **Hook detection by reflection** depends on overrides being declared on the class or a base
  below `EntityService`; a hook implemented through `new` (hiding) is not detected, which the
  compiler warns about anyway.

### What reviewers should examine hardest

1. `WriteRun.SettleTargets` and its callers: the order NotFound → Forbidden, that only hidden or
   absent ids are named, and that the persist repeats both checks inside the transaction.
2. `ActionRun.Rounds`: the `Persisted` gate, the deferred `RecordingBatch`, and that a method's
   exception, a `PartialFailureException` and a validation failure each end up where they should.
3. `ActionRun.Persist.AfterProvenCommitAsync` and `DeleteRun.Persist`'s equivalent against D-116.
4. `PersistFailures`' new rows for `50413` / `50428` and `Fk.InUse` on a delete.
5. `HookOverrides`: the reflection over services and default interface methods.

### Findings

**R1-1 (major): `Persisted` releases the flow from the rounds when it is read, not when it is
awaited. A method that reads it before its last load or write can hang the request, or commit
without its later writes and then fail after the commit.**
`ActionRun.Rounds.cs:117` (`PersistedRead`) and `:147-164` (`Gated`), `ActionContext.cs:133-139`,
and `ValidationScheduler.cs:316-326` and `:349-356`.
- **What happens.** Reading `Persisted` completes the gate. `Gated` then returns, and the scheduler
  marks the participant Completed. If the flow parked at a load in between (`ParkAsync` while it was
  still Running), `RunParticipantAsync`'s finally removes it from `_parked` without releasing its
  round. `DriveAsync` can then end the rounds with `_finished = true` and never complete that
  `_round`.
- **Scenario A: deterministic.** The natural C# shape is
  `Task persisted = context.Persisted; await context.Loader.LoadAsync(); context.Update(...); await persisted;`.
  1. Persisted is read before the first load. `WhenAny(flow, gate)` therefore finishes synchronously,
     and the parked participant is dropped at once.
  2. Round 1 still dispatches, because of `RequestRound`, and that wakes the method.
  3. `RunGeneralAsync` runs `_context.Close()` on the dispatching thread while the method's
     continuation is still queued.
  4. `context.Update` throws (`ThrowIfClosed`), or it is recorded after `Compose`'s snapshot.
     Either way RT2 commits without the method's update.
  5. `ResumeMethodAsync` then raises the method's `InvalidOperationException` as a 500 for a
     committed action.
- **Scenario B: racy.** `await LoadAsync(); var p = context.Persisted; await LoadAsync(); await p;`
  1. The second park races the wrapper's finally. If the finally wins, no round is requested, so
     `DriveAsync` finishes.
  2. The method waits forever on a `_round.Task` nobody releases.
  3. RT2 commits, and `ResumeMethodAsync` then awaits the hung method forever, so the request never
     answers.
- **Contract gap.** The remarks promise "a load awaited after it is refused". No test exercises
  that, and the code does not reliably do it.
- **Suggested fix.**
  1. Release under the scheduler's lock, synchronously, at the read. Add a scheduler method that
     moves the current participant from Running to Completed and calls `Leave`, so it is never
     Parked-then-dropped. A later `ParkAsync` from that flow then fails deterministically with the
     stray error.
  2. Refuse `Save`, `Update` and `Batch` appends from a released flow, for example by checking
     `Waiting.Value is { Task.IsCompleted: true }`.
  3. Before RT2 is sent, abandon the action if a released flow has already faulted. That matches
     §11.2: "every other exception from the method abandons the batch unsent".
  4. Add unit tests for "read, then load" (refused, no hang) and "read, then Update" (refused, nothing
     sent).
  - Alternatively, release only when the flow suspends on `Persisted`. That needs a custom awaitable
    in place of `Task`, which changes the contract and would go to Ahmad.

**R1-2 (major): the deferred action batch's refusal of `Tvp`/`DeclareIdTable` (deviation 8)
breaks `ActionContext.Batch`'s contract, and with it spec 0018's `invite` as specified.**
`RecordingBatch.cs` (`EnsureBatch` in `Save`, `Delete`, `RecordName`, `FromCache`) and
`ActionRun.Rounds.cs:67`.
- **The contract.** Spec 0015 §7.1 types `ActionContext.Batch` as "the persist batch, through §5.1's
  recording view", a full `IDataBatch`.
- **The broken caller.** Spec 0018's `invite` (an `[EntityAction]` answering its own result) appends
  its write-back through `context.Batch.Sql` with `SqlOptions(UserIds = @tb{b}_t1 : IdList)`.
  `UserIds` must be a name the batch declared, from `Tvp` or `DeclareIdTable`
  (`DataBatch.cs:314`). On the deferred view that `Tvp` throws, so the invite statement as specified
  cannot be composed.
- **Other callers.** Spec 0014 §3.4's preference-bag writers (`preferences/set`/`delete` are
  `[EntityAction]`s) bind `@tb{b}_t0 : UserPreferenceList` the same way when they need a user-tag
  bump.
- **Why this matters now.** Deviation 8 presents this as a local implementation choice. It
  silently narrows a contract two later checkpoints depend on.
- **Suggested fix.** Make the deferred view able to answer names. Either:
  - replay the deferred recording at a fixed position at the head of the persist body, before the
    emitter, the target key table and the updates, and allocate its batch-local names from the same
    deterministic counter the persist batch will use (a seam on `DataBatch` to reserve the next
    names). `EnsureSame` then checks them on replay; or
  - give `SqlOptions.UserIds` a list-of-ids form that needs no declared name.
- **If neither is done.** Record it as a cross-spec conflict for Ahmad (spec 0018 §invite, spec 0014
  §3.4), not as a PC-076 deviation.

**R1-3 (major): the delete and action re-entry path is untested.** That covers deviation 10, the
§5.1 replay, and the §18.2 stale-context pin for `WidgetService`'s `[EntityAction]`.
`DeleteRun.Persist.cs:99-131, 186-245`, `ActionRun.Rounds.cs:210-243` and
`ActionRun.Persist.cs:132-195`.
- **The untested code.** No unit or integration test drives a second `ComposePersistAsync` entry
  for a delete or an action. Untested on re-entry:
  - `RevalidateAsync` for both;
  - the deferred `_recording.Replay` onto a second batch;
  - `EnsureSameNames`;
  - the participants' replay;
  - "the method is never invoked again".
- **The spec pin.** §18.2 "Stale context" names exactly this: on the recompose, `WidgetService`'s
  validators re-run in memory, and "its `[EntityAction]` method and `ContributeAsync` run once, and
  their statements commit once". `WidgetService` and its `rename` action are in this checkpoint's
  fixture.
- **Why a test is needed.** PC-074's save re-entry test does not cover these paths. A regression
  here (say, a replayed deferred recording composing twice, or a re-run hook's `Gated` flow
  completing the real context's gate) would ship unnoticed.
- **Suggested fix.**
  - Unit, over the scripted runner: force one recompose of a hooked delete and of the `Close`
    action, then assert the method ran once, the hook ran twice, the recorded `Sql` appears once per
    batch, and the participants were called once.
  - Integration: copy `SavePersistTests`'s "permissions moved since the scope cached its caller"
    pattern for `rename`.

**R1-4 (minor): a method failure after a normal commit is raised for a void action, which goes
against D-116 and departs from the proven-commit branch.** `ActionRun.Persist.cs:117-119`.
- **The inconsistency.** On a committed (non-ambiguous) RT2, `ResumeMethodAsync`'s failure is
  re-thrown for every method, so the caller gets a 500 for a committed action and is invited to
  retry it. The proven-commit branch (`:291-295`) logs the same failure for a void method and answers
  the ids. Deviation 12 justifies the proven-commit split, but not raising on the normal path.
- **Suggested fix.** On the normal path:
  - a void method's failure after commit: log it, for example as `ProvenCommitReadFailed` or a
    sibling event, and answer the ids;
  - a value method's failure: raise it, since there is nothing else to answer.
- **For Ahmad.** List this under deviation 12. It is the same question as D-117 Q5.

**R1-5 (minor): a stack whose `MaxDeleteByQueryRows` is `int.MaxValue` makes every delete by
query fail with a 500.** `DeleteRun.Persist.cs:265`, and `PersistFailures.cs:132`'s `counts.Cap + 1`.
- **What happens.** `StackLimits` accepts any positive value. The special case passes
  `cap = int.MaxValue` to `Count`, whose `CountTake` refuses it with `ArgumentOutOfRangeException`
  ("less than the largest integer"). The translation's `Cap + 1` would also overflow.
- **Suggested fix.** Count with `Math.Min(cap, int.MaxValue - 1) + 1`, or something equivalent, and
  use checked arithmetic in the translation. Or refuse `int.MaxValue` in `StackLimits`'
  validation, which the ceilings do not need.

**R1-6 (minor): a stamped delete on a long-keyed stack is refused only at compose time, as an
`ArgumentException`, so a 500, and after RT1 on a hooked stack (deviation 5, D-117 Q4).**

### What holds

- **No hang from the shapes Review 1 named.** "Read, then load" fails at once.
  `ValidationScheduler.BeginLeave` sets `Leaving` under the scheduler's lock, so the later
  `ParkAsync` is refused deterministically (`ValidationScheduler.cs:259`), whether the load comes
  before the first suspension or after a round. I also probed "start a load, then read Persisted
  while it is parked". `BeginLeave` sees `Parked`, fails the rounds and throws from the getter. The
  request ends in under a second with no persist.
- **The quiet-point count stays balanced on every path.** `Release` decrements only from
  `Running` (`ValidationScheduler.cs:329-339`). `RunParticipantAsync`'s finally decrements only from
  `Running` (`:377`). So a task that leaves, then completes, is counted once. The AsyncLocal handler
  calls `Left` at most once per gate, because `_leave` is nulled under the gate's lock.
- **The method's continuation never runs twice.** `PersistedSource` completes or faults once. The
  method is invoked only on the first run (`Start(withMethod: true)`). `RevalidateAsync` never starts
  it. `ResumeMethodAsync` awaits it and never re-runs it. Only one of `PersistAsync`'s normal-commit
  and proven-commit branches runs.
- **An accepted write is never dropped.** Within the persist, `Close` and `Compose`'s snapshot run
  under the context's lock, and the recording is sealed before `Replay`. A `Save`, an `Update` or an
  append that was accepted is therefore always in RT2. A write can only be refused, never accepted and
  then lost.

### The R1-1 leftover, established

The leftover is reproduced deterministically: a probe whose method awaits a task completed by the
scripted Persist batch's execution. The answer depends on who composed late.

| Who composes after reading `Persisted` and awaiting something else | What the caller sees | Server-side trace |
|---|---|---|
| A method that returns no value (`Task`) | **Success**: `EntitiesResult` with the ids. RT2 committed without the write. | `ActionMethodFailedAfterCommit` at Error, carrying the refusal |
| A method that returns a value (`Task<T>`) | **Error after commit**: the refusal as `InvalidOperationException` (a 500), with RT2 and the post-commit phase already run. The message is the sealed recording's ("The persist batch takes a participant's statements…") or the compose guard's. | none beyond the 500 |
| The service's `ValidateActionAsync`, a component, or a child flow of the method (`Task.Run`) | **Success**. RT2 committed without the write. | **none**: `Gated` only `Observe`s a released flow, and `ResumeMethodAsync` awaits only the method |

So the plain answer is that the leftover is a **silent write loss** for void actions, hooks and
components: the caller gets success. It is an error after the commit only for a method that returns
a value of its own. The implementer's note ("refused, but the persist may already have been sent")
is accurate but leaves the outcome unstated. R1-4's fix (log a void method's failure after the
commit, then answer the ids) is what turns the void case into a success answer. The same holds for an
`Errors.Add` after the read and a later await: `ValidationErrors` never closes, so the error is
dropped once RT2 committed.

**Can it be closed within the current contract? No.** The implementer is right that closing it fully
needs a custom awaitable.
- `Persisted` is a plain `Task` (spec 0015 §7.1: `public Task Persisted { get; }`). Awaiting a
  `Task` goes through the non-virtual `TaskAwaiter` and registers on the task's private continuation
  slot, so nothing public tells "suspended on `Persisted`" from "suspended on anything else".
  `Left` fires at the first suspension of any kind.
- Holding RT2 "until the flow awaits `Persisted` or completes" therefore cannot be told apart from
  holding it for an HTTP call that the flow awaits before awaiting `Persisted`. The only signal left
  is the flow resuming before `Persisted` completed, and that can come after RT2 committed.
- Within the contract, the loss can only be made loud, not prevented. A custom awaitable, whose
  `UnsafeOnCompleted` releases the gate, needs §7.1's type to change. Code that stores `Persisted` as
  a `Task` or passes it to `Task.WhenAny` would break. That is a decision for Ahmad.

### Findings

**V-1 (major): the leftover loses the late write silently for void actions, hooks and components.**
`ActionRun.Persist.cs:135-148` (`RaiseAfterCommit`), `ActionRun.Rounds.cs:185-191` (a released flow
is only observed), `ActionRun.Persist.cs:292-310` (only the method is awaited), and
`ActionContext.cs:129-136`, whose remarks document only the "abandoned unsent" branch.
- **Scenario.** `var p = context.Persisted; await externalCall; context.Update(...); await p;`
  Against a slow dependency in production RT2 commits without the update and the caller gets
  success. Against a fast fake in tests, the same code is usually abandoned unsent
  (`ThrowIfReleasedFlowFailed`), so a test suite does not reveal it. In a hook or a component, the
  refusal is not even logged.
- **Fix within the contract.**
  1. Record every refusal from the leaving gate on the run (`PersistGate.EnsureComposing`, the
     scheduler's "after it left the validation rounds" stray), whatever flow raised it, for example
     as `_composedLate ??= exception`.
  2. Check that record beside `ThrowIfReleasedFlowFailed` before RT2 is sent. This also covers
     child flows the method does not await.
  3. After the commit, raise it for every action, void or not, under a distinct log event at Error.
     It is an authoring bug, not a transient failure, so D-116's "do not invite a retry" rule should
     not hide it.
  4. Log the failure of a released hook or component flow after the commit, instead of only
     observing it.
  5. Optionally, abandon earlier: the AsyncLocal handler can flag a leaving flow that resumes before
     `PersistedSource` completed. This needs the gate set inside a wrapper around `body()`, so that
     `Gated`'s own continuation does not carry the gate.
- **Also.** State the committed branch in `ActionContext.Persisted`'s remarks, and put the custom
  awaitable to Ahmad as the full close.

**V-2 (minor): the gate can release before the reading flow stops running.**
`ActionRun.Rounds.cs:44-51`.
- **Scenario.** `ThreadContextChanged` also fires when the reading flow, still on its stack,
  synchronously completes a `TaskCompletionSource` created without `RunContinuationsAsynchronously`.
  That source's continuation belongs to another task and runs inline under its own context, so
  `Left` fires while the reader is still running. A refusal the reader raises right after that races
  RT2 instead of failing the rounds.
- **Why minor.** It needs a synchronous cross-task completion after the read, and it widens V-1's
  class rather than adding a new outcome.
- **Fix.** None needed beyond V-1. A comment on the handler should say that "the flow stopped
  running" really means "the thread left the flow's context".

**V-3 (minor): a released flow whose fault carries more than one inner exception does not stop the
persist.** `ActionRun.Rounds.cs:203`.
- **Scenario.** The pattern `InnerExceptions: [Exception failure]` matches only a single inner
  exception. A non-async hook that returns `Task.WhenAll(a, b)` with both faulted passes
  `ThrowIfReleasedFlowFailed`, and RT2 is sent.
- **Fix.** Throw `flow.Exception.InnerExceptions[0]`, or the flattened aggregate, whenever
  `flow.IsFaulted`.

**V-4 (minor): a read of `Persisted` from a flow that does not carry the gate is never seen.**
`ActionRun.Rounds.cs:138`.
- **Scenario.** A read under `ExecutionContext.SuppressFlow()`, or from `UnsafeQueueUserWorkItem` or

  `ValidationScheduler.Fail` does not complete `_quiet`, so not even cancellation ends `DriveAsync`.
  The request hangs.
- **Why minor.** It is very unlikely: every ordinary `await` and `Task.Run` flows the context.
- **Fix.** Make `PersistedRead` throw when `Waiting.Value` is null and the run is in its rounds.

**V-5 (minor): the deferred view's table counter is per view, not per batch.**
