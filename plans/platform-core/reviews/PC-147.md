# PC-147: TellmaHub, SignalR publisher and hosting modes

## Implementer notes

### What was built, and where

- **Hub** (`src/core/Tellma.Core.AspNetCore/Realtime/TellmaHub.cs`): internal, no invocable
  method. `OnConnectedAsync` reads the claims from `Context.User` alone (`TellmaHubClaims.TryRead`:
  `sub`, `tellma_tid`, `tellma_uid`, `tellma_sh`, each exactly once, ids positive with no sign),
  adds the connection to the tracker under the memo lock, then joins `HubGroups.Of(claims)`. A
  connection without the claims or under a remembered close is counted `connects{refused}`,
  logged `HubConnectionRefused` and aborted. Invocations and streams are refused by the protocol
  decorator (`TellmaJsonHubProtocol`, the one `json` protocol), counted
  `connects{invocation-refused}`.
- **Claims** (`TellmaHubClaims.cs`): `Create(RequestContext)` mints the `Tellma.Hub` identity from
  a bound context; `Select(principal)` is Azure SignalR Service's `ClaimsProvider`, the subject and
  the three hub claims and nothing else.
- **Front door** (`HubFrontDoor.cs`, `Tenancy/TenantMiddleware.cs`): on the Hub surface the
  middleware runs the guard, then one prologue-only Read through `IGuardedBatchRunner` (always,
  not only on a cold cache), then adds the hub identity. Every refusal from tenant parse to guard
  to prologue is counted and logged (`Refused`), unless the client went away.
- **Groups** (`HubGroups.cs`): `t{tid}`, `t{tid}.u{uid}`, `t{tid}.s{subject}`, `t{tid}.x{handle}`.
- **Tracker** (`HubConnectionTracker.cs`): singleton, `ISessionTerminationListener` and
  `ITenantStateListener`. Session end: the session group in every tenant of the local connections
  and of the subject's membership hints. Revoke: the subject group. Suspended, Retired and
  removed: the tenant group. Each close remembers its key for 5 minutes, tells `session.ended`,
  then aborts the local connections, counting `closes{reason}` and logging `HubConnectionsClosed`.
  Any other state sends `cache.changed {tag:'tenant'}` and closes nothing.
- **Publisher** (`SignalRClientEventPublisher.cs`): the tenant group, or the distinct user groups
  in the scope's tenant; a tenantless scope is refused before anything is sent.
- **Options and naming** (`RealtimeOptions.cs`, `RealtimeApplicationName.cs`): `Tellma:Realtime`
  (`Enabled`, 15 s keep-alive, 150 s client timeout at least twice the keep-alive,
  `ExpectedInstances`); the Azure application name is the deployment id with `_` for `-`.
- **Composition** (`TellmaRealtimeServiceCollectionExtensions.cs`): `AddTellmaRealtime(configure)`,
  idempotent, called by `AddTellma`; registers SignalR, the hub's options through
  `AddHubOptions<TellmaHub>` (so the 4 KB frame limit, detailed errors off and filters take
  effect), the intervals read unvalidated from configuration (the handler is built at `MapTellma`,
  before the options gate), `TellmaUserIdProvider`, the tracker, the publisher and status
  factories honouring `Enabled`, and the hosting check. Every call strips the framework's plain
  `JsonHubProtocol` the builder delegate may add.
- **Mapping** (`TellmaEndpointRouteBuilderExtensions.cs`): `MapHub<TellmaHub>` on the Hub group
  with `CloseOnAuthenticationExpiration`, `NoActivityStampMetadata`, `AllowMember()`, skipped when
  `Enabled` is false.
- **Hosting** (`RealtimeHosting.cs`, `RealtimeHostingCheck.cs`): the mode from the registered
  lifetime manager (in-process, Azure, Redis, unknown); `host.realtime-hosting` fails an unknown
  manager, a connection string without Azure, another application name, and an in-process hub with
  Jobs off; warns for App Service or `ExpectedInstances > 1` without a backplane.
- **Azure line** (`src/defaults/Tellma.Defaults.Azure.AspNetCore/AzureWebDefaultsTellmaBuilderExtensions.cs`):
  `AddAzureSignalR` through `AddTellmaRealtime`'s delegate, `ClaimsProvider = Select`, a 5-minute
  token, the application name from `DeploymentIdentity`; `Microsoft.Azure.SignalR` 1.33.1 pinned.
- **Telemetry**: `RealtimeMetrics.cs`, `RealtimeLog.cs`; KQL alerts in `infra/monitoring/realtime/`
  (refused connects per instance, access-revoked closes untold).
- **Core**: `SessionEndedEvent` (name, reasons, payload) registered as a client event in
  `CoreFeature`.
- **Live suite**: `test/core/Tellma.Core.AspNetCore.Tests/Realtime/AzureSignalRLiveTests.cs`
  (`Category=Integration`, `Live=true`), skipped unless `TELLMA_SIGNALR_TEST_CONNECTIONSTRING` is
  set; the `signalr-live` job in `.github/workflows/nightly.yml` (environment
  `live-realtime-tests`, secret `SIGNALR_TEST_CONNECTIONSTRING`, a require step so a missing
  secret fails rather than skips). Each test runs under a random environment name, so its
  application name on the shared instance is its own.
- **Docs**: READMEs of `Tellma.Core.AspNetCore`, `Tellma.Defaults.Azure.AspNetCore` and both test
  projects; `ARCHITECTURE.md` (the Azure bundle's hub line, the hosting check under Real-time and
  Redis, the nightly workflow's comment).

### Deviations

1. The session group is `t{tid}.x{handle}`, not `x{handle}`: the client drops envelopes whose
   `tenantId` is not its own, so an untenanted group cannot carry a correct envelope to tabs in
   two tenants. A session's end is therefore sent per tenant: the tenants of the local connections
   plus the subject's membership hints (a hint read failure is logged, `HubSessionTenantsUnread`).
2. `session.ended` goes to the instance's own connections one by one and to the rest of the group
   with `GroupExcept`, rather than one group send. On Azure SignalR Service a direct send to a
   local connection is written on that connection's own server connection, the one its abort's
   close follows (verified in the SDK 1.33.1 source); a group send may take another server
   connection and be overtaken by the close. It also tells a connection that is admitted but still
   joining its groups.
3. The check is `host.realtime-hosting` (the host checks' prefix), not `realtime-hosting`.
4. `OnRemovedAsync` is treated as tenant-unavailable (reason `tenant-unavailable`).
5. A 5-minute memo of ended sessions, revoked (tenant, subject) pairs and unavailable tenants
   refuses late joiners whose negotiate passed before the close; a user reactivated within the
   window is refused for up to 5 minutes. A ReadOnly, Frozen or Active change clears the tenant's
   memo.
6. The hosting check passes everything when `Enabled` is false, and fails an unknown lifetime
   manager.
7. `RealtimeApplicationName.From` throws when the deployment id does not start with a letter (the
   service's rule).
8. The Azure application name is set with `AddOptions<ServiceOptions>().Configure<DeploymentIdentity>`
   rather than inside the `AddAzureSignalR` lambda, which has no service provider.
9. `WEBSITE_INSTANCE_ID` is read through `IConfiguration` (environment variables are a source), so
   tests set it without touching the process environment.
10. `TellmaHub` is internal (SignalR activates it with `ActivatorUtilities`).
11. `CoreFeature`'s client-event literals became constants (`SessionEndedEvent.Name`).
12. Refusals before the tenant middleware (the authorization's 401, the CSRF control's 403) are not
    counted in `connects{refused}`; they have their own instruments.
13. The hub's intervals are read from configuration unvalidated for `HubOptions<TellmaHub>`, so a
    bad value fails at the options gate as a composition problem rather than as an
    `OptionsValidationException` when `MapTellma` builds the handler.
14. The live suite lives in `Tellma.Core.AspNetCore.Tests` (reusing its test host), not in a
    separate integration project; that project now references `Tellma.Defaults.Azure.AspNetCore`
    so the Azure line is composed exactly as a distribution composes it.

### Open risks

- On Azure a service token minted before a revocation can open a connection for up to its
  5-minute lifetime; the memo that refuses it is per instance, so a connect routed to another
  instance is refused only by the closes that instance itself ran (its own listener calls).
- Long polling runs the prologue on every poll request.
- The live suite has never run: it needs the `live-realtime-tests` environment and its secret.
  Until they exist, the nightly's `signalr-live` job fails at its require step, by design.
- Ordering between two separate sends is not guaranteed on Azure; the live suite asserts absence
  after a barrier plus a 3-second settle, not by order.

### What reviewers should examine hardest

- Hub authentication: that no path reaches `OnConnectedAsync` with claims the front door did not
  mint (`HubFrontDoor.EnterAsync` throws if the principal already carries hub claims; `TryRead`'s
  exactly-once rule; the `/bypass` test).
- Tenant isolation of groups: every group name is tenant-qualified; the publisher's groups come
  from the scope's tenant only; envelopes name the connection's own tenant.
- What a client can subscribe to: nothing (no invocable method, invocations refused, groups only
  from claims).
- The tracker's memo and snapshot under one lock, and the direct-send-then-abort order.
- `RealtimeHosting.From`'s type-name matching of the Azure and Redis lifetime managers.
