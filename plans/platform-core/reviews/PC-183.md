# PC-183: Audience resolver

## Implementer notes

### What was built

- **The contract** (`src/core/Tellma.Core.Abstractions/Sharing`): `IAudienceResolver`,
  `AudienceQuery`, `ShareAudience`, `AudienceMember`, `AudienceReason`, `AudienceGrant`,
  `AudienceReasonKind`, `AudienceAccess`, and `SharingTelemetryNames`
  (`tellma.sharing.audience.members`, tag `resource`). The shapes follow spec 0033 §6.1. The
  resolver also takes a `CancellationToken`.
- **`AudienceResolver<TUser, TRole>`** (`src/core/Tellma.Core/Sharing/AudienceResolver.cs`) is
  scoped and closed over the leaves in `SharingComposition`. `ShareableShapes` (a singleton map
  from resource to shape) and `SharingMetrics` are registered beside it.
  - Round trip 1 is one guarded Read batch. It holds:
    - the row under the caller's `Share` decision filter, as a `Rows` query over a key-set;
    - per group (the row itself, and the rows seen through): a statement with the rows and their
      owners, the grants (with the grantee's audience/active/public flags), and the stored
      permissions of active roles on the resource, its owner chain or its aliases, or on `*` with
      the registered actions. It also fills batch-local id tables for people, candidates, roles
      and the "reaches everyone" marker;
    - a common statement with the current audience memberships of the named non-public roles,
      the audience size, and `TOP (MaxAudienceMembers)` users by display name in the reader's
      content language (only when a grant may reach everyone and the members are visible);
    - per data securable (skipped when a bespoke `Read`/`*`/own-action criterion exists on any
      level): the reader roles, the public-role Everyone bit, the exact `COUNT(DISTINCT)` of
      audience readers, and which named candidates read it;
    - the `core.User` (plus `Kind`) and `core.Role` related projections, and the projection of
      the rows seen through under the caller's `Read` filter.
  - Permissions are classified with `PermissionClassifier` and matched with
    `AccessComposition.Matches` against the shareable's `Read` securable, as the permission-set
    build does. Edit comes from `Save` or `*`; every other matching action gives View.
  - Round trip 2 runs only when a matched permission is filtered. It is one `Rows` query per
    distinct (group, filter text, version), `FilterTree.Leaf(text, version)` over a key-set of
    the group's ids. A filter beyond the compile ceilings counts as unsatisfied.
- **`AudienceComposer`** (`src/core/Tellma.Core/Sharing/AudienceComposer.cs`) is the pure merge:
  - one grant per owner, user share, role share, Everyone share and stored permission that
    reaches the row, plus one `Through` grant per row seen through (the union of its own grants);
  - `Total` and `CannotRead` are exact. A grant reaching everyone counts from the audience size
    and the readers' count, and is never expanded into ids;
  - members carry every reason and the highest access, are sorted by display name in the reader's
    culture, and are cut at the cap. `Members` is null and `Capped` false without
    `core.Role` × `Read`.
- **`ReportService.AudienceAsync`** (`[ApiAction("audience", Action = "Share", Mutation = false,
  Idempotent = true, Long = true)]`):
  - reads the report's `Root` through its own pipeline query (not found when invisible);
  - asks `IDashboardReadThrough.DashboardsUsing([id])` and takes the distinct ids, the first 20
    by id;
  - resolves `("core.Report", id, [(Root, Read)], "core.Dashboard", ids)`.
  The route is in the route and stack goldens, the client entity fixture, and the Acme
  `web.json`/`contracts.json` snapshots. It is driven end to end in `ReportOperationTests`
  and listed in `ProjectedRouteTests`. Its label `Core_Report_Action_Audience` is in both
  `Strings` resources.
- **Tests:**
  - `AudienceComposerTests` (9 unit tests).
  - `ReportAudienceTests` (9 integration tests on real SQL): CannotRead per grant and per member;
    the report route with the bespoke self criterion on `core.User`; a service account via a
    shared role, a public role and a permission role; a deactivated grantee with `Members: 0`;
    hidden members plus a 404 for a grantee without `Share`; the cap with an exact `Total`;
    600 permission holders counted exactly, plus the metric; a filtered permission counted only
    when the row satisfies it; Through.
  - `ReportOperationTests` now reads the audience over HTTP and checks §6.2's example grant shape.

### Deviations

1. **Round trips.** §6.2 puts the per-filter counts in the first round trip. A permission's filter
   is Queryex text that only C# compiles, so it cannot be compiled before the permissions are
   read. Round trip 1 therefore carries everything else, the Through grants included (their ids
   are known up front). Round trip 2 runs only when a matched permission is filtered. The result
   is 1 trip without filtered permissions and 2 with them, against the spec's 1, or 2 with
   Through.
2. **`ReportService.AudienceAsync` reads the root first** (one more round trip, through the
   pipeline under the caller's `Read`). The data securable has to be known before the resolver
   composes. So a caller who may share but not read the report gets a 404.
3. **The rows seen through are projected only when the caller's `Read` on `ThroughResource`
   admits them** (a privacy choice). The spec names the projection but no authorization. Their
   grants count regardless.
4. **Bespoke criteria count for everyone on the actions `Read`, `*` or the data securable's own
   action.** The spec says `Read` or `*`. A wildcard-resource permission counts only for the
   actions the levels register, plus `Read` and `*`, as the holders resolver does. A public role's
   wildcard-resource permission is not an Everyone reader.
5. **Unsatisfied filtered permissions are left out of `Grants`.** A permission whose filter drifted
   (a classifier problem), or that is filtered on a securable with no filter root, is skipped.
6. **`ThroughIds` are de-duplicated, non-positive ids and the row itself are dropped**, and an
   unregistered `ThroughResource` is accepted when there are no ids. `core.Dashboard` is not
   shareable yet, and `NullDashboardReadThrough` answers nothing.
7. **The users' projection in `Related` is not narrowed by the caller's `core.User` × `Read`**
   (the details-page principle). `Kind` is added to the projection.
8. **The membership predicate is written literally** in the statements, in the place of
   `AccessFilters.CurrentMembershipSql("m")`. A text hole binds as a parameter, as in
   `AccessHoldersResolver`.
9. **`IAudienceResolver.ResolveAsync` takes an optional `CancellationToken`**, and
   `AudienceAsync` takes one as well.

### Open risks

- **`me()` in a permission filter evaluates as the caller** in round trip 2, not as each holder.
  A permission like `OwnerId = me()` therefore counts its role's members for any row the
  caller owns. The spec's "one count per distinct filter text" implies this. A per-holder
  evaluation would need one statement per holder.
- The members listed when a grant reaches everyone are the first `MaxAudienceMembers` by the
  database collation's order (`COALESCE(NULLIF(<twin>, ''), Name), Id`). They are then sorted in
  C# by culture compare. The two orders can disagree at the cut-off.
- The class-level integration database is shared by the tests of the class. They avoid
  cross-talk by granting roles through SQL (a `Publish` permission would reach every report)
  and by filtering every seeded report permission to its one report.

### What reviewers should examine hardest

- Whether the round-trip-1 SQL counts exactly: audience users only (active, `Kind <> 'System'`),
  current memberships only, inactive roles excluded, public roles reaching everyone. The
  `candidates` table must hold every user a reach can name before the per-securable reader
  statements run (statement order in the batch).
- The permission matching (`Classify` plus `Matches` on `Read`), the Edit mapping, and the
  wildcard handling against spec 0014 §5.2 and §5.5.
- `CannotRead` for an everyone reach (`Audience − Readers.Count`) against the explicit-reach path.
- The authorization: the `Share` filter on the row, the `core.Role` × `Read` gate on `Members`,
  and the `Read` gate on the rows seen through.

## Review 1

### Findings

Counts: 0 blocking, 2 major, 1 minor.

What I checked and accept (not findings):

- **Deviation 1 (round trips).** The spec's count cannot be met as written. A permission's filter
  is Queryex text that only C# compiles, and the rows that hold the filters are read in the same
  trip that would need them compiled. Meeting "one trip" would need a tenant-wide copy of the
  stored permissions cached in C# under the `permissions` tag, which the platform does not have
  (it caches each caller's set only). The result (1 trip, or 2 with a filtered permission, Through
  folded into trip 1) is never worse than the spec's 2-with-Through. Spec 0033 §6.2 step 2 should
  be corrected.
- **Deviation 2 (root read first).** The extra trip is inherent: `AudienceQuery.Data` must be known
  before the resolver composes its reader statements. The 404 for "Share but no Read" cannot
  happen. Spec 0014 §5.2 step 2 makes a role grant of `Share` match `Read` with the same filter,
  and the bespoke `Share` criterion (`OwnerId = me()`) is inside the bespoke `Read` one (owner),
  so `Share` always implies `Read` on the same rows.
- **Deviation 3 (rows seen through projected under the caller's `Read`).** Consistent with spec
  0033 §5.7, where `DashboardsUsing` gives a null title for a dashboard the caller cannot read. The
  grants still count. The owners and grantees of a row seen through never reach `Related` unless
  they are listed members (only the `Through` entry carries ids), so nothing leaks.
- **Deviations 4–9.** The wildcard-resource narrowing mirrors `AccessComposition.Matches` and
  `AccessHoldersResolver` (spec 0014 §5.2 with the action-resource map). Adding the data
  securable's own action to the bespoke-criterion test is a no-op for `Read` data. The other
  deviations are justified.
- **`Core_Report_Action_Audience`.** Required by the action metadata (the client fixture names it),
  and it follows the `Core_Report_Action_*` naming of the other actions. Both resources carry it.
- **SQL counts.** Checked statement by statement: audience users only (active, `Kind <> 'System'`),
  current memberships only, inactive roles excluded, public roles reaching everyone, and
  `candidates` filled by both group statements and the common statement before any reader
  statement reads it. Everyone-reach `CannotRead` (`Audience − Readers.Count`) is exact, because
  `Readers` counts only non-public roles' current audience members and a public reader role sets
  `Everyone`.
- **Tests listed by the checkpoint.** Each one exists and asserts what the checkpoint names. The
  600-holder test asserts the exact `Members`, `CannotRead` and `Total` and the instrument's one
  measurement with its `resource` tag. The cap test asserts `Total` 7 exact with 3 members.
- Build: `dotnet build Tellma.slnx` succeeded with 0 warnings on 08720b8c. The pc/PC-183 CI run
  (46108640607) was skipped, so it is no evidence.
- Tests I re-ran, all green:
  - `ReportAudienceTests`: 9 of 9.
  - `AudienceComposerTests` and `ClientEntityFixturesTests`: 14.
  - `RouteContractsTests` and `StackDescriptorGoldenTests`: 11.

  The commit's test list matches what the change touches. I did not re-run `ReportOperationTests`

**R1-1 (major): a permission filter that calls `me()` is evaluated as the caller, so the
share dialog overstates the audience of the caller's own reports.**
`src/core/Tellma.Core/Sharing/AudienceResolver.cs:751-760` (round trip 2). The filter is compiled
as `FilterTree.Leaf(text, version)` inside the caller's guarded batch, so `me()` binds to the
caller, and one result is applied to every holder of the role (`Finish`, line 807).
- **Failure scenario.** This is spec 0033 §5.2's own example. A "Report authors" role holds
  `core.Report × Publish` filtered by `OwnerId = me()`. By spec 0014 §5.2 step 2 that grant
  matches `Read` with the same filter. An author opens the share dialog of a report they own. The
  filter is true for the caller, so every member of "Report authors" is counted in `Total` and
  listed in `Members` with a `Permission` reason. The grant row also shows all of them as
  `Members`. In fact only the owner sees the report through that permission. Ownership is the
  normal case for reading an audience, since only owners hold the bespoke `Share`. The reverse
  also happens. An administrator who reads someone else's report sees that grant dropped from
  `Grants`, which deviation 5 does for an unsatisfied filter.
- **Risk.** This is a correctness issue, not a disclosure or access risk. Nothing is granted, and
  the caller learns only how the filter evaluates for themselves.
- **Spec gap.** "One count per distinct filter text" is wrong for filters that depend on the
  caller. Spec 0014 §5.1 lists `me()` among the context functions a filter may use.
- **No test covers it.** The filtered-permission test uses `Title = 'Alpha eight'`.
- **Suggested fix.** Detect filters that reference a caller-context function (`me()`; `now()` is
  fine because it is the same for every holder). Two options:
  - Evaluate each such filter per holder, with `me()` bound to each holder: one statement per
    (filter, holder) over the row, bounded by the role's current members, and capped so a large
    role falls back to the second option.
  - At least, leave such permissions out of every reach and mark the grant so the dialog does not
    claim its members.

  Add an integration test with `OwnerId = me()` on a role of several members: owner as caller
  gives 1, a non-owner caller gives 1, never the role's size. Raise the spec gap with Ahmad.

**R1-2 (major): `reports/audience` does not take the `tellma-reports` rate policy.**
`src/core/Tellma.Core/Reports/ReportService.cs:388`.
- **What the spec says.** Spec 0033 §2.2 says the long routes `run`, `export` and `audience` "also
  take the `tellma-reports` policy". Spec 0016 §8.2 says `tellma-reports` covers
  `reports/audience` and `dashboards/audience`.
- **What the code does.** The action carries `[ApiAction(..., Long = true)]` but no
  `[RatePolicy(TellmaRatePolicies.Reports)]`, as the export actions do
  (`src/core/Tellma.Core/Excel/ExcelOperations.cs:76`). The endpoint therefore gets only
  `tellma-user` and `tellma-tenant`.
- **Failure scenario.** A user, or a script with a session, can run any number of concurrent
  audience resolutions, each up to two guarded batches with `COUNT(DISTINCT)` over memberships and
  up to 2,000 users projected. That bypasses the per-user concurrency of 4 (queue 8) the spec puts
  on report-class work.
- **Suggested fix.** Add `[RatePolicy(TellmaRatePolicies.Reports)]` to `AudienceAsync`, with the
  interactive lane and no priority. Add an assertion beside `WebApiProjectionTests`' export case
  (`test/core/Tellma.Core.AspNetCore.Tests/Api/WebApiProjectionTests.cs:101`) that the audience
  endpoint's chain is `tellma-reports`, then user, then tenant. Regenerate any golden or snapshot
  that records policies.

**R1-3 (minor): the SQL behind the everyone reach and the membership window has no
integration test, so a regression in the hand-written statements would pass.**
`src/core/Tellma.Core/Sharing/AudienceResolver.cs:319-341` and `:359-375`, against
`test/core/Tellma.Core.IntegrationTests/Sharing/ReportAudienceTests.cs`. No integration test:
- shares a report with Everyone (the `Everyone` reason kind is absent from the suite);
- asks a data securable together with an everyone reach (the public-role test passes
  `Data: []`), so the `Readers` `COUNT(DISTINCT)` column is never read;
- has a public role hold the data permission (the `Everyone` reader bit);
- puts an expired or a future membership into any count;
- shares with an inactive role.

The window predicate is a literal copy of `CurrentMembershipSql` in five places (deviation 8).
A wrong comparison in one copy, or a public role leaking into `readerRoles`, would make
`CannotRead` or `Members` wrong with every test green. The composer's unit tests cover the C# side
only.
- **Failure scenario.** Change `[m].[EndsAt] > SYSUTCDATETIME()` to `>=` or drop it in the
  `Readers` count, and nothing fails.
- **Suggested fix.** Add one integration test:
  - an Everyone grant with `Data [core.Role × Read]`;
  - readers through a non-public role, one with an expired and one with a future membership on
    that role;
  - assert the Everyone grant's `Members` = audience users, `CannotRead[0]` = audience users − the
    current readers, and a listed reader's member `CannotRead` empty;
  - then make a public role hold the data permission and assert `CannotRead` 0;
  - add an inactive role share, asserting `Members` 0.

### Responses

- **R1-1 (fixed, per the ruling).**
  - Detection: `ViewerFilters.ReadsCurrentUser` scans a classified permission's filter text
    lexically. It skips string literals, bracketed identifiers and named parameters, and ignores
    a `me` that is a path member (`x.me`). A call to `me()` anywhere else marks the permission
    as depending on the viewer. `now()` and `today()` are not marked, because they are the same
    for every holder.
  - What a marked permission does: it never goes to round trip 2 and is never evaluated, per
    holder or as the caller. In `AudienceComposer` its reach is empty, so it adds nothing to
    `Total`, `CannotRead`, `Members` or any member's reasons. It is still listed in `Grants`,
    for every row of its group, with `Members: 0`.
  - Contract change: `AudienceGrant` gains `bool DependsOnViewer`. The existing shape could not
    carry the mark: `Filtered` sits on `AudienceReason`, and the grant would have no reason.
    This is new deviation 10.
  - Tests:
    - `AudienceComposerTests`: one case for a marked permission (a non-public and a public role
      both reach no one) and a theory over the detector.
    - Integration: `A_permission_filtered_by_the_current_user_reaches_no_holder_whether_the_owner_or_an_administrator_reads_the_audience`
      uses spec 0033 §5.2's example. The "Report authors" role holds `Publish` filtered by
      `OwnerId = me()` and has two members, the owner and another author. The owner and an
      administrator (`Share` on the report and `core.Role` × `Read`) each get the same audience:
      the authors' grant marked, `Members: 0`, and only the owner and the administrator as
      members.
- **R1-2 (fixed).**
  - `AudienceAsync` now carries `[RatePolicy(TellmaRatePolicies.Reports)]`, in the interactive
    lane with no priority.
  - `WebApiProjectionTests` asserts the `reports/audience` chain (`tellma-reports`, user across
    tenants, user, tenant) and `HonorsPriority` false, next to the export case.
- **R1-3 (fixed).** One integration test,
  `An_everyone_grant_counts_the_readers_of_current_memberships_alone_and_a_public_reader_role_leaves_no_one_lacking`.
  - Setup: an Everyone grant with `Data [core.Role × Read]`, and a reader role with a current,
    an expired and a future membership. It also adds a grant to an inactive role, whose one
    member holds a current membership.
  - The Everyone grant's `Members` and `Total` equal the audience users, every audience user is
    listed, and `CannotRead[0]` (both the grant's and the total) equals the number of members
    the reader statement marked. This checks the `COUNT(DISTINCT)` copy of the window against
    the per-member copy.
  - The current reader lacks nothing, the expired and the future members lack the roles, and
    the inactive role's grant has `Members: 0`.
  - A public role holding the data permission then leaves `CannotRead` at 0 for the audience,
    for every grant and for every member.
- **Deviation 10 (new).** `AudienceGrant.DependsOnViewer` (see R1-1). For Ahmad: spec 0033 §6.1/§6.2
  should say that a permission whose filter reads the current user is listed but not counted,
  and add the field.
