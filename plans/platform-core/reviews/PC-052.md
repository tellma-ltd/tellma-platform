# PC-052: IAccessEvaluator, deny re-check, witness, EvaluateFor and the holders resolver

## Implementer notes

### What was built

The evaluation surface of spec 0014 §5.1, with the deny re-check of §6.4, the owner chain of §5.5, the witness of §4.6 layer 4 and §7.8, the step-up bar of §4.3, the holders and administrators of §10 and §7.5, the FilterTooComplex path of §12.2, and the §13 decision and witness instruments.

- **Contracts** (`Tellma.Core.Abstractions/Access`):
  - `IAccessEvaluator`: `EvaluateAsync` (one pair, or a list), `RequireAsync`, `EvaluateForAsync`, `EvaluateAllAsync`, `GetAccessAsync` and `Invalidate`.
  - `IAccessHoldersResolver`, with the records `AccessHolders` and `AccessHolder`. Both copy their lists and refuse null parts, an undefined outcome and a negative total.
  - `IAdministratorDirectory` and `IAccessGuards` (the §8.3 seat).
  - `AccessDecision.Deny`'s doc now says "an owner pair", because an unknown pair asked directly now carries its problem.
- **`AccessEvaluator`** (Core/Access, scoped):
  - A scope with no caller is short-circuited to the empty set before `Decide`, because bespoke criteria grant on their own (carried from PC-043). `DecideFrom` also denies a set with no user before it reads any criterion or owner.
  - Criteria are read from every provider of the resource, ignoring case, on every evaluation. The owner chain is decided recursively through `registry.Find(owner, action)`.
  - **The deny re-check**: a registered pair denied from a set whose `ValidatedAt` is older than `FastDenyWindow` triggers one `IScopeConnection.RevalidateAsync` per scope, guarded by `Interlocked.Exchange`. Every pair of that call is then decided again from the returned set.
    - The system set, a set with no user, and an unknown pair's denial never trigger the re-check.
    - `EvaluateAllAsync` never re-checks, because the summary's own prologue has just validated the set.
  - **`RequireAsync`**:
    - A denial raises `ForbiddenException("forbidden", registered pair, NoGrant)`.
    - A sensitive pair is then held to `StepUpBar` through `AssuranceCheck.Ensure`, except in the system scope.
    - The denial is checked before the step-up bar, so the refusal reveals nothing about sensitivity.
  - **The witness and counter**: `EvaluateAsync` and `RequireAsync` record each pair, as registered (or as asked when the registry does not hold it), in `AccessWitness`. They also count `tellma.access.decisions` by `outcome`, once per pair, after any re-check.
  - **`EvaluateForAsync`**:
    - An unknown pair answers as for the caller, with no load.
    - The caller's own id answers from the caller's set.
    - Another user is loaded through `IScopeConnection.LoadDetachedAsync`, once per scope and user. The load is a `Lazy<Task>` under a lock; a faulted or cancelled load is not shared.
    - An unknown, inactive or system user, a `userId <= 0` and a tenantless scope are all denied before any criterion is read.
    - No witness, no counter.
  - `Invalidate` removes the `PermissionsCache` entry and, for the scope's tenant, the scope's load of that user.
- **`UserConnector`**: two new `IScopeConnection` members.
  - `RevalidateAsync`: the scope's warm premises with `ReloadPermissions = false`, in one prologue-only round trip. A pass advances `ValidatedAt` on the cached set itself; a moved tag brings fresh rows, which are applied as usual.
  - `LoadDetachedAsync`:
    - one `ForUser` round trip expecting nothing cached, so the rows always come back;
    - `Contribute(..., detached: true)`, so none of the target's tags reach the batch outcome or the response header;
    - the set is built and returned uncached;
    - an unknown, inactive or system target counts `unknown_subject` or `deactivated`.
- **`AccessWitness`** (scoped):
  - Holds the pairs evaluated (ignoring case) and the member witness, which `ConnectInitializer` records when a user or service account connects.
  - `HasEvaluated` resolves an endpoint's pair through the registry, aliases included.
  - `Confirm(securable?, endpoint)` is the filter's check. A miss counts `tellma.access.witness.missing` and logs `MissingWitness` (3100, Error) with the tenant, user, endpoint and expectation (`resource:action`, or `member`).
- **`HoldersComposer`** (singleton, pure):
  - Classifies the matching rows with the set build's `PermissionClassifier`, then composes each user's grants through `AccessComposition.Compose` along the same owner chain.
  - Computes `Everyone` from the public grants plus the criteria, and `Grants` and `Problems` from the all-roles decision (windowless).
  - `MembersByRole`: a public role counts the audience.
  - Holders are composed once per distinct set of granting roles.
- **`AccessHoldersResolver<TUser, TRole>`** (scoped, closed over the leaves at composition):
  - Runs one guarded read through `IGuardedBatchRunner`. The single `batch.Sql` statement fills three result sets:
    - the matching permissions of active roles, across every level of the owner chain, their aliases, the action or `*`, any action for Read, and on `*` the actions the chain registers;
    - the current memberships of non-public roles' active, non-system members;
    - the public audience.
  - It also queries the members the caller may read, under the caller's `core.User × Read` decision and filter, and the involved roles, each with its related projection.
  - Holders the caller may read are sorted by name in the content language, then by id. They are capped at `MaxHolders`, with `Total` and `Capped` reported.
  - A users' filter that the `PipelineLimits` compile refuses (an `ArgumentException` from `batch.Query`) logs `FilterTooComplex` (3107, Error, with the roles behind it) and throws `InvalidOperationException`.
- **`AdministratorDirectory`** (scoped): one guarded `TOP (20)` read of active individuals, invited or joined, with a current administrator membership, ordered by id.
- **`NullAccessGuards`**: the default `IAccessGuards` (no lock, no assertion), per the planner note.
- **`StepUpBar`** (singleton):
  - Core's default is `urn:tellma:acr:mfa` within 15 minutes.
  - The web host replaces it with `Tellma:Session:StepUp` (`TellmaWebApplicationBuilderExtensions`).
  - `StepUpOptions` defaults now read `StepUpBar.DefaultAcr` and `DefaultMaxAge`, so the two cannot drift.
- **`AssuranceCheck`** (Core/Tenancy): the class and age rules extracted from `TenantAccessGuard`, so the endpoint bar and the evaluator's bar are one rule. `TenantAccessGuard` now calls it.
- **Composition**:
  - `AccessRuntimeComposition.Register(services, record, options)` registers the bar, the witness, the evaluator (behind `IAccessEvaluator` and as itself), the composer, the resolver closed over the record's user and role leaves, the directory and `NullAccessGuards`.
  - `CompositionPipeline` passes the record.
  - `AccessLog` gains 3100 and 3107; `AccessMetrics` gains the two counters and `OutcomeValue`.
- **Tests**:
  - Unit, in `Tellma.Core.Tests/Access`:
    - `AccessEvaluatorTests`, 37 tests (about 50 cases) over a fake connection and a `FakeTimeProvider`: the re-check window (0, 4, 5, 6 and 3600 seconds, plus a configured window), once per scope under concurrency, never from the summary, `ValidatedAt` advancing, Require's Forbidden and step-up theory, the witness and counter tags via `MetricCollector`, EvaluateFor loads, and Invalidate.
    - `AccessWitnessTests` (3100 via `FakeLogCollector` and `MetricCollector`).
    - `HoldersComposerTests`: conformance over every securable of a seeded registry, so a holder is exactly a user whose own-membership decision allows the securable, with the same outcome and permission ids. Doc Read is pinned in full.
    - `AccessHoldersResolverTests` over `BatchHarness`: the membership predicate pinned to `AccessFilters.CurrentMembershipSql("M")`, an unknown pair making no round trip, and 3107.
    - Additions to `AccessRuntimeCompositionTests` and `ConnectInitializerTests`.
  - Unit, in `Tellma.Core.Abstractions.Tests`: the holder contracts, in `AccessContractTests`.
  - Unit, in `Tellma.Core.AspNetCore.Tests`: `HostCompositionTests` checks that the web host's `StepUpBar` comes from `Tellma:Session:StepUp`, and falls back to its defaults.
  - Integration (SQL), in `Tellma.Core.IntegrationTests/Access`:
    - `AccessEvaluationTests`:
      - EvaluateFor shares one load per scope across concurrent calls and never feeds the caller's snapshot, the target's snapshot or the permissions cache;
      - a deactivated, unknown or system target;
      - the re-check once per scope, with `ValidatedAt` advancing on the same cached set;
      - a re-check that admits a new grant;
      - the witness;
      - holders skip an ended, a future and an inactive member, for full, self-only and blind readers.
    - `AdministratorDirectoryTests`: an ended, a future, an inactive and a service-account administrator are skipped, and the cap of 20 holds.
    - `ConnectDatabase` gains `SeedRoleAsync`, `SeedMembershipAsync`, `BumpPermissionsAsync` and a `configure` hook on `CreateInstance`.
- READMEs are updated (Core, Core.Abstractions, Core.AspNetCore, Core.Tests, Core.Abstractions.Tests, Core.AspNetCore.Tests, Core.IntegrationTests). ARCHITECTURE.md needed no change, because it says nothing about the evaluator, the re-check, the witness or the holders.

### Deviations

1. **The current-membership predicate is written literally** in the holders and directory SQL, as `AccessFilters.CurrentMembershipSql("M")`'s text. An interpolated string hole binds as a parameter (TELLMA0003). Unit tests pin both reads to the helper's output, so the two cannot drift.
2. **The connector never calls `Invalidate`.** `Invalidate` lives on the evaluator, and the connector sits below it, so the dependency would point the wrong way. The connector already replaces a stale set when a prologue returns rows; `Invalidate` is for writers such as the role-save path.
3. **`EvaluateForAsync` and `EvaluateAllAsync` neither witness nor count.** The witness proves the caller's own endpoint check, and the §13 counter counts the caller's decisions. Counting another user's decisions, or the summary's hundreds, would swamp both.
4. **Aliases for the holders' match come from the concrete `SecurableRegistry.Aliases`.** `ISecurableRegistry` exposes no alias enumeration, so a substituted registry matches the registered names only.
5. **A system, tenantless or `userId <= 0` EvaluateFor target is Denied** without a round trip. The spec names only the "unknown or inactive" target.
6. **The resolver reports no drift logs.** Its rows are classified by the same `PermissionClassifier`, and problems surface in `AccessHolders.Problems`, but `PermissionDrift` (3101) stays the set build's, once per version, so that a holders read does not log each problem again.
7. **FilterTooComplex is detected from the `ArgumentException` that `batch.Query` raises** when compiling the users' filter under `QueryexProfiles.PipelineLimits`. There is no dedicated exception type to catch.
8. **`AccessHolders.Related` includes the roles behind `Problems` as well as `Grants`.** The UI can then name a role whose permission drifted.
9. **The detached load counts its connect result but does not log `ConnectRejected`** for an unknown or inactive target. That log is for a refused caller, and another user's absence is not a refusal.
10. **Step-up is judged on the host's clock** (`TimeProvider`), not `context.Now`, because the evaluator can run long after the context was stamped. `TenantAccessGuard` keeps `context.Now`.
11. **`AssuranceCheck` was extracted** from `TenantAccessGuard` into Core/Tenancy, so the endpoint bar and the evaluator's bar share one rule.
12. **`StepUpBar` is a Core singleton that the web host replaces** from `Tellma:Session:StepUp`. Core cannot read the web host's options, and the migrator and job hosts need a bar too.
13. **`AccessWitness.Confirm` ships now, unused.** The HTTP witness filter that calls it is spec 0016's endpoint work (CHECKPOINTS: "wired with 0016 (later)").
14. **`AccessHolders.Grants` lists the role grants of the all-roles decision**, not literally "every matching row". The decision prunes a filtered wildcard-action row on an action without a filter root. Such a row grants nothing there, and listing it would contradict every holder's own decision.
15. **The §10 `AccessSummary` type (spec 0016) is not built.** `EvaluateAllAsync` returns the decisions it needs, per the planner note.
16. **A Require that is refused is still witnessed.** The witness records that the check ran; the refusal is the endpoint's answer.

### Open risks

- **A job scope has no `Assurance`, so `RequireAsync` refuses it on a sensitive pair** with `StepUpRequiredException`, unless it runs as the system. `RequestContextSnapshot` carries no assurance. This follows §4.3 and §5.1: the refusal reaches "every caller, request or not", including "a caller no endpoint guard saw". Even so, a user-run background job that saves a user, a role or a schedule will fail. Whether such jobs should run as the system or carry the requester's assurance is for the jobs work to settle.
- **The holders read fetches every visible current member** of the matching roles before the `MaxHolders` cap, because the sort is by display name in the reader's language, computed in C#. A role with tens of thousands of members reads them all. A SQL-side cap would need a collation per content language.
- **The evaluator is per scope.** Its re-check flag and target loads live as long as the scope. A long-lived job scope keeps another user's set beyond `PermissionsMaxAge`, unless `Invalidate` is called.

### What reviewers should examine hardest

1. **The re-check concurrency** in `AccessEvaluator.EvaluateCallerAsync`. A racing second call that loses the `Interlocked.Exchange` decides from the set it already had, without awaiting the winner's round trip. That is what §6.4 says ("a second stale denial in the same scope answers from the set as it is"), but a concurrent pair of checks in one request could split. Awaiting the winner instead would mean sharing its cancellation.
2. **`UserConnector.RevalidateAsync` and `LoadDetachedAsync`**: that the warm premises without the age reload never fetch rows needlessly, and that `detached: true` keeps every one of the target's tags out of the outcome (`AccessEvaluationTests` asserts the snapshots and the cache).
3. **The conformance of `HoldersComposer`** with the per-user decision: the owner chain, the implied read through other actions, public roles and criteria (`HoldersComposerTests` asserts it over every securable).
4. **The holders SQL's matching predicate** (aliases, `*`, the implied-read actions), compared with `UserAccess`'s matching rules.
5. **The order in `RequireAsync`** (denial before step-up), and the system bypass.
