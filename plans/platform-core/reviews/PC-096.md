# PC-096: client/sync service, TmCacheRefresher and TmLaunchCache

## Implementer notes

### What was built, and where

- **`client/sync`** (`src/core/Tellma.Core/Client/ClientSyncService.cs`): internal routed service,
  `[ApiAction("sync", MemberOnly, Idempotent, Mutation = false, Mcp = Hidden)]`, registered in
  `CoreFeature`. One Read batch through `IGuardedBatchRunner`: the connect prologue, then the
  caller's preference rows (read always; whether the envelope is needed is known only after the
  prologue). Tags compare ordinally; a held name the server does not know is ignored. Settings come
  from `ITenantSettingsCache.GetForClientAsync` (the cache's bytes, under the wire tag they were
  loaded with); the envelope is composed by `MeEnvelope` (`src/core/Tellma.Core/Users/MeEnvelope.cs`,
  extracted from `UserService.Me`/`Preferences`, which now use it); every `entity:<Name>` tag is
  added when the held composite differs or is absent; `StaleEntities` in ordinal name order;
  `TenantFacts` carries `Realtime` from `IRealtimeStatus`. The polling-routes set already held
  `client/sync`, so it never stamps activity.
- **Records** (`src/core/Tellma.Core.Abstractions/Api/`): `ClientSyncRequest`, `ClientSyncResult`,
  `TenantFacts`, all in the source-generated context, and `PreSerializedJson<T>` (internal
  constructor and `Of`, written with `WriteRawValue`, described by `ContractDescriber` as `T`).
- **Client store additions** (`client/projects/core/tellma-core/src/state/store/tm-client-store.ts`),
  all internal: `ɵwrite` (several documents of one partition in one readwrite transaction, one
  `store.changed` naming the written ones, mirrors set together), `ɵshow` (a result shown in the
  mirror ahead of its write, held against re-reads until released), `ɵreread`, `ɵreadOnce` and
  `ɵnames`. `ɵstart` now registers its read for each boot partition before awaiting the database,
  so an entry during boot joins it. `#write` was generalised to several names per commit.
- **`TmLocks`** (`src/state/sync/tm-locks.ts`): Web Locks behind an abstract class; a page without
  `navigator.locks` queues within the tab. `TmFakeLocks` in `@tellma/core-testing`, installed by
  `provideTellmaStateTesting()`.
- **`TmCacheRefresher`** (`src/state/sync/tm-cache-refresher.ts`): `observe`, `refresh`, `adopt`,
  `lastSyncAt`, internal `ɵcacheChanged` (the leader's handler) and `ɵsignal`. Runs coalesce per
  tenant (cold wins), queue on `tm:<slug>:<sub>:<t>:sync`, re-read and end at the check, wait out
  the 2 s throttle (abortable), send one read (`background` unless cold), and store the answer in
  one `ɵwrite`. The boundary timer arms at `validUntil` + 0-2 s while visible, backs off 30 s to
  10 min while the server keeps the boundary. `provideTellma` binds `TM_API_CACHE` to it.
- **`TmLaunchCache`** (`src/state/launch/tm-launch-cache.ts`) and the launch-document types
  (`tm-launch-docs.ts`): `docs`, `warm`, `tenants`, `distribution`, `entitySet`, `revalidateUser`,
  internal `ɵenter`.

### Deviations

1. `ClientSyncResult.Settings` and `.Me` are `PreSerializedJson<T>`; the wire is unchanged.
   Settings: the cache's bytes are written as they are. Me: the web OpenAPI document writes every
   schema under the HTTP JSON options, whose `MaxDepth` is 16 (spec 0016 §3.1), and the envelope's
   schema is 15 deep on its own, 17 under the answer, so `/openapi/web.json` failed with a 500.
   Pre-serialized, it reads as "any value" in the schema; `users/me` still describes it in full,
   and the contract fingerprint describes it as `MeResult`.
2. `ClientSyncService` is internal and hidden from MCP (agents hold no client cache).
3. `MeEnvelope` refactor shared by `me`, the preference writes and `client/sync`.
4. A new trigger, `list`, and `refresh(t, ['entities'])` or `refresh(t, ['entity:<Name>'])` holds
   no `entities` composite, so the answer reports every list's tag: this is what makes §3.3's
   "sync first, which reports every list's tag" true when the composite is held but a list's tag
   is not.
5. `TmLaunchDocs` holds `settings`, `me` and `tenant` only: `inbox` and `assignments` wait for the
   leader-documents registry.
6. The TypeScript names carry the `Tm` prefix (`TmTenantSettingsForClient`, `TmMeResult`, ...).
7. A list's staleness is derived (stored tag ≠ `sync.entityTags[name]`) rather than a stored flag;
   a stale report the held tags contradict deletes the list's tag and sets the composite to null.
8. `TM_SYNC_ERRORS` is a seam standing in for the error presenter (a later checkpoint); until it
   is provided, `tenant-not-found` and `tenant-unavailable` are dropped and nothing is swept
   (WATCHLIST hand-off to PC-110, added in review 1).
9. `TmLocks` with an in-tab fallback when Web Locks are absent.
10. `TmLaunchCache.ɵenter` exists for the tenant route (shell checkpoint); meanwhile an effect enters
    the routed tenant by itself.
11. A list document stores the `entities` array alone.
12. The e2e pins (leaving the tenant aborts; the multi-tab scenarios) are covered by unit tests
    here and left to the shell checkpoints.
13. `ɵstart` registers its reads up front.
14. An `access` refresh is needless only while the held `validUntil` is ahead (not also "a sync
    sent after the call"), because a sync that held the permissions tag did not re-decide grants.
15. (Review 1) A stored `sync.at` later than now, left by a clock set back since, is taken as
    unknown: a forced call is needless only when `requested < sync.at <= now`, and the throttle
    waits `min(2 s, sync.at + 2 s - now)`, never §5.3 step 4's whole remainder.
16. (Review 1) A `cold` call is needless only once `settings`, `me` and `tenant` are all present,
    not once a sync was sent after it (§5.3 step 3): another build's sync may hold tags this
    build cannot read (§2.4), and bring nothing this tab lacks.
17. (Review 1) A list's load calls `refresh(t, ['entity:<Name>'], 'list')`, which is also needless
    once `sync.entityTags[Name]` is known, so a list read while a sync is in flight costs no second
    sync.

### Open risks

- **The OpenAPI depth limit is structural.** Any response type whose wire depth reaches about 8
  has a schema deeper than 16 and breaks `/openapi/web.json`. Spec 0016 §3.1 (MaxDepth 16) and
  §12.1 (schemas are the real types) conflict for deep types; client/sync works around it here.
- **Size.** The `@tellma/core` primary grew from 16.51 to 20.19 KB on the linked measure (+3.68 KB);
  its ceiling rises from 16.59 to 20.28 under D-031, with a budget note. Spec 0025's primary services
  now weigh 7.98 of their 9 KB share, leaving about 1 KB for the rest of the spec's primary work.
  Please check the measurement. Review 1's fixes add 0.16 KB (20.35 measured, ceiling 20.44; 8.14 of
  the 9 KB share).
- **No consumer yet.** Nothing in the shell injects `TmLaunchCache` or calls `ɵstart`; the
  refresher is live through `TM_API_CACHE` only.

### What reviewers should examine hardest

- The run in `TmCacheRefresher.#run`/`#sync`: the needless checks inside the lock, the throttle's
  abort path, `close()` timing (calls joining during the wait, a later call starting a new run),
  and the cold-only rejection.
- `adopt` ordering: `ɵshow` now, `ɵwrite` under the lock later, with a sync that holds the lock
  storing an older envelope in between. Since review 1, `ɵwrite` makes a held show's update again
  on each incoming copy (never the write's own update), so the mirror keeps the shown copy.
- The boundary timer's `due`/`delay` state across an effect re-run and the refresh's `finally`.
- `ɵwrite`'s memory-mode path and its interplay with sweeps and `#hold`.
- `entitySet`'s side effect inside a `computed` (a queued `#load`), and the skip tests that stop a
  refused or uncached list from looping.

**R1-1 (major) — a `sync.at` from the future stalls the tenant's syncs inside the lock and makes
every forced refresh needless.**
`client/projects/core/tellma-core/src/state/sync/tm-cache-refresher.ts:548-561` (the throttle) and
`:322` (the forced check), over `:473-475`. `sync.at` is the writer's `Date.now()` and is persisted.
When the device clock moves back by Δ after a sync (NTP correcting a dual-boot or VM-resume skew,
a manual fix), `sync.at` is Δ ahead, on disk, across launches:
- the next run computes `wait = sync.at + 2 s − now = Δ + 2 s` and waits it out holding
  `tm:<slug>:<sub>:<t>:sync`, so for up to Δ (hours, for a time-zone-sized skew) every other tab's
  sync, every cold entry, every adoption's write and every list-tag write in this browser queues
  behind it, and the leader's election sync and the boundary refresh wait with them;
- every forced call (`event`, `leader`, `poll`, `terminal`, the read-only `header`, `cold`,
  `list`) passes `requested < sync.at` and ends at the check without a sync until the clock
  catches up, so a role edit's `{ tag: 'permissions' }` is never applied, and a cold entry whose
  documents are absent (a format bump, spec 0025 §2.4's example) resolves with them still absent.
The spec's device-clock-ahead backoff shows clock skew is in scope; the code follows the spec's
"wait the remainder" literally, which never anticipated a negative `now − sync.at`.
Fix: treat a `sync.at` later than now as unknown — throttle with
`Math.min(THROTTLE_MS, sync.at + THROTTLE_MS − now)` and make a forced call needless only when
`requested < sync.at && sync.at <= now`; state it under Deviations. Test: store `sync.at` an hour
ahead; a forced refresh syncs within 2 s, and a cold refresh with `settings` absent stores it.

**R1-2 (minor) — `ɵenter` can resolve with the launch documents still absent, and nothing then
syncs them.**
`client/projects/core/tellma-core/src/state/launch/tm-launch-cache.ts:197-205`, with
`tm-cache-refresher.ts:322`. Two paths:
1. A cold run is needless once any sync was sent after it was requested, whether or not that sync
   wrote what this tab lacks. Spec 0025 §2.4 (Decision 17) runs two builds side by side for days:
   a new build's tab finds `settings@2` absent while `sync@1` is shared with an old build's tab. If
   the old tab's run sends its sync after the new tab's cold call (it was waiting out its throttle
   inside the lock), the new tab's run ends at the check; the old tab held its own `settings@1`
   tag, so the answer carried no settings, and `settings@2` stays absent. The page's data request
   waits for the documents (spec 0024 §4.4), so no header comes to repair it.
2. The `#entry` memo survives leaving the tenant: 17 → `/tenants` → 17 returns the old resolved
   promise without re-checking, so documents that went absent meanwhile (the `{ tenant: 17 }`
   sweep, the idle rule) never get their cold sync.
Fix: make a `cold` call needless only when `settings`, `me` and `tenant` are present (a needless
closure over the three documents instead of the time test), and memoise only the in-flight entry
(or re-check the three documents before returning a settled one). Tests: a second refresher that
sends a sync after the cold call while the cold tab lacks `settings`; a re-entry after a sweep.

**R1-3 (minor) — a list read while a sync is in flight costs a second sync and at least 2 s.**
`client/projects/core/tellma-core/src/state/launch/tm-launch-cache.ts:285-287`, with
`tm-cache-refresher.ts:322`. `#load` calls `refresh(t, ['entities'], 'list')` when it holds no tag
for the list. If a sync of the tenant has already been sent (the cold entry's, the leader's on
election, a header's), the pending run is closed, so the list's call starts a new run; it queues
behind the in-flight sync, which stores every list's tag, then finds `requested < sync.at` false,
waits out the 2 s throttle and sends a second `client/sync`. That breaks "at most one `client/sync`
per change" and delays the list. Fix: let the call name the list (`docs: ['entity:<Name>']`) and
make it needless once `sync.entityTags[Name]` is known, besides the time test. Test: read
`entitySet` while a cold sync is gated, then release it; one `client/sync` in total.

**R1-4 (minor) — an adoption can visibly revert while a sync that holds the lock lands.**
`client/projects/core/tellma-core/src/state/store/tm-client-store.ts:688-698` (`ɵwrite`'s
`mirror` sets every copy, ignoring the hold that `ɵshow` placed), with `tm-cache-refresher.ts:376-382`.
Farah pins a page while the leader's sync is in flight: `adopt` shows the new bag; the sync then
stores its older `me` and the mirror shows the old pins again; the adoption's own write lands one
lock hand-off (several IndexedDB tasks) later and restores them. The pinned bar flickers back and
forth. Spec 0025 §5.3 accepts an out-of-order bag on disk, not a reverted screen.
`tm-cache-refresher.spec.ts:504-520` asserts only the end state. Fix: keep the shown copy over
`ɵwrite` while a show is held (re-apply the shown update to the incoming copy before setting the
mirror); assert the mirror between `sync.resolve` and the adoption's write.

**R1-5 (minor) — the re-read inside the lock is never exercised.**
`client/projects/core/tellma-core/src/state/sync/tm-cache-refresher.ts:542`. Every refresher spec
runs over `provideTellmaStateTesting()`'s memory store, where `ɵreread` returns at once
(`tm-client-store.ts:946-948`), and `another()` (`tm-cache-refresher.spec.ts:207`) shares the same
store and mirror, so the "second tab" sees the first tab's write without any re-read. Deleting
line 542 leaves every test green. In a browser the second tab's mirror learns of the first tab's
commit only through `store.changed` over BroadcastChannel, which can arrive after the lock is
granted. Then it syncs again, the duplicate the spec's "lock serialization across two instances;
the re-check skips" pin exists to catch. Fix: one refresher test over two IndexedDB-backed stores
(browser mode, as `tm-client-store.spec.ts` already runs) with the bus delivery withheld, asserting
the second run ends at the check.

**R1-6 (minor) — OpenAPI depth: the claim holds and the workaround is acceptable here, but the
platform needs a fix item.**
`src/core/Tellma.Core.Abstractions/Api/ClientSyncResult.cs:60-61`. I checked it with
`JsonSchemaExporter` under `TellmaJson.Options`: `MeResult`'s schema nests 15 deep, and one record
holding it nests 17. Serializing that node under `MaxDepth = 16` throws, and two levels further
the exporter itself throws. So the whole `/openapi/web.json` fails (500), not just one route, for
any response whose schema passes 16. Roughly, that is any type of wire depth 8 or more. The
workaround keeps the wire and the contract fingerprint (`ContractDescriber` describes the member
as `T`), and `users/me` still documents the shape, so it is fine for this checkpoint. But it trades
spec 0016 §12.1 ("schemas are the real types") for §3.1's limit, a request-reading bound that the
document writer happens to share. `WebOpenApiTests` covers only the core fixtures' routes, so a
distribution's deep type (an `EntitiesResult` over a parent → child → grandchild stack with related
entities) would break its Development document unseen. Suggest a platform fix item: write the
OpenAPI document (exporter and serializer) under options with a larger `MaxDepth`, or have Ahmad
amend 0016 §3.1/§12.1. Then return `ClientSyncResult.Me` to `MeResult`. `Settings` may stay
pre-serialized, since spec 0025 §5.1 asks for the cache's bytes.

**R1-7 (minor) — `TM_SYNC_ERRORS`: consistent with spec 0025, but with no named owner, and silent
in the interim.**
`client/projects/core/tellma-core/src/state/sync/tm-cache-refresher.ts:88-91`, `:649-650`. Spec 0025
§5.3 step 7 hands the two refusals to `TmErrorPresenter.present`, and §2.6 makes the `{ tenant: t }`
sweep the presenter's (spec 0024 §11), so the refresher owes no sweep, and a seam with a no-op
default matches the pattern of `TM_API_CACHE`/`TM_API_UPDATES` (D-054). Two gaps:
- no WATCHLIST hand-off names PC-110 as the owner that must provide `ɵTM_SYNC_ERRORS`, as D-054
  required for the other seams;
- until then a background sync's `tenant-not-found` or `tenant-unavailable` vanishes without even
  `tm.sync.failed`, so a deleted or suspended tenant is invisible in telemetry. Cold entries still
  reject to `ɵenter`'s caller.
Fix: the orchestrator adds the WATCHLIST line (PC-096 → PC-110: provide `ɵTM_SYNC_ERRORS` from
`TmErrorPresenter`, which also runs the `{ tenant: t }` sweep on `tenant-not-found`); optionally
the default seam emits `tm.sync.failed` with the kind.

**R1-8 (minor) — budget: the raise is within D-031, but spec 0025's 9 KB share is effectively
spent.**
`client/projects/core/tellma-core/package.json:30`. My measurement matches the note: 20.19 KB on the
linked measure; +3.68 KB rounds up to 4 KB, so D-031 allows up to 20.59, and 20.28 is within it.
The weight follows the sources (about 1,000 lines of refresher and launch cache plus 250 of store),
with no new dependency. The savings named are real (one write helper and pending count; private
fields). With the store and bus, 0025's primary services now weigh 7.98 of 9 KB. That leaves about
1 KB for TmLeader, `TM_LEADER_DOCUMENTS`, TmPreferences, `tmPreferenceKey`, TmPageState, TmDrafts,
TmTreeExpansion and TmRecents (WATCHLIST line 1092 forecast 4.7 KB for them plus this checkpoint).
They cannot fit. This is also the fifth raise of "." (D-031's re-split clause), although the
primary grows towards its sum of shares by design. Fix: none in this checkpoint. The orchestrator
should record now that 0025's primary share will be exceeded, and decide whether some of that work
moves out of the primary (for example TmLeader's election logic behind the leader-only
`/realtime` import) or the share rises.
