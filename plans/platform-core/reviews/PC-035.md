# PC-035: Notification and realtime contracts, registries and null publisher

## Implementer notes

### What was built, and where

**Contracts** (`src/core/Tellma.Core.Abstractions/`):

- `Notifications/`: the three seeded descriptors are kept unchanged. New beside them:
  - the registries `INotificationTypeRegistry` and `INotificationChannelRegistry`;
  - `INotificationRenderer`;
  - `INotifier`, `NotificationRequest`, `DuplicatePolicy` and `WithdrawRequest` (§3.1);
  - `NotificationsOptions` (§9.1), whose data annotations carry the §9.1 bounds. `IValidatableObject`
    adds the two retention periods: at least 1 s, and at most `int.MaxValue` seconds, the width of
    the retention statement's `DATEADD(second, …)`;
  - `NotificationPreference` (§4.3);
  - the channel seam `INotificationChannel` (`[CollectionService]`), `NotificationRecipient` and
    `TestNotificationResult` (§4.4);
  - `NotificationsTelemetryNames` (§10.1).
- `Realtime/`: `ClientEvent` (with `MaxPayloadBytes = 4096`), `ClientEventEnvelope`,
  `IClientEventRegistry`, `IClientEventPublisher`, `IRealtimeStatus` and `RealtimeTelemetryNames`.
- `Localization/`: `LocalizedText` and `LocalizedTextJsonConverter` (§2.4).
  - The converter writes `[text, text2|null, text3|null]` under any options. It refuses every other
    shape with one `JsonException` message.
  - `In(slot)` takes slots 1 to 3 and falls back to `Text` when the slot is null or empty.
  - `Names(entity, property = "Name")` copies a `[Multilingual]` anchor and its `2`/`3` twins. It
    reads them by reflection, cached per (type, anchor). It refuses a missing, non-string or
    non-multilingual property and an anchor that holds null.
  - `Text` is get-only, following the Abstractions README rule, so a `with` expression cannot
    bypass the null check.
- `TableTypes/`: `NotificationRowList`, `NotificationSliceList` and `NotificationPreferenceList`
  (§3.2, §4.3).
- `Composition/`: the three item records `NotificationTypeContributionItem`,
  `NotificationChannelContributionItem` and `ClientEventContributionItem`. `FeatureContribution`
  gains the sugar `NotificationType(descriptor)`, `NotificationChannel(descriptor)` and
  `ClientEvent(name)`, each refusing null.
- `Crud/` and `Validation/`: contract-first seeds of spec 0015's write frame, which
  `INotificationChannel.DeliverAsync` names. These are `IWriteHost`, `WriteHostKind`,
  `IOpenWriteHost` (`PersistAsync`), `EnlistmentOutcome` and `IConfirmations`. Each is shaped as
  spec 0015 §13.3/§7.5 sketch it; see "Decisions for the orchestrator".

**Runtime** (`src/core/Tellma.Core/`):

- `Notifications/`:
  - `NotificationTypeRegistry`, `NotificationChannelRegistry` and `ClientEventRegistry`. Each keeps
    realisation order and the first item of a key, skips null items and keyless ones, and does
    ordinal lookups. `Categories` lists each category once, in the order of its first type. A null
    key passed to `Find` or `IsRegistered` gives null or false.
  - `NotificationsClientAssetContributor` writes `notificationTypes`, `notificationChannels` and
    `clientEvents`, in that order, into the Core chunk, with `TellmaJson.Options`.
  - `ClientEventPublisher` is a public abstract base, and `NullClientEventPublisher` derives from
    it. The base holds:
    - the refusals: null event (`ArgumentNullException`), null `UserIds` (`ArgumentException`),
      an unregistered name, and a payload over 4,096 UTF-8 bytes measured as
      `JsonSerializer.SerializeToUtf8Bytes(payload, TellmaJson.Options)`. Both refusals are
      `InvalidOperationException`, thrown synchronously by `Publish` and `PublishAsync` before
      anything is registered.
    - a `protected virtual ValidateScope(ClientEvent)` hook. The SignalR publisher will refuse a
      tenantless scope through it.
    - the coalescing. A `ConditionalWeakTable<IDataBatch, …>` under a `Lock` gives one `OnCommitted`
      registration per batch. That one callback sends each distinct
      (name, sorted distinct user ids, payload JSON) once, in first-publish order.
    - failure handling: a failed delivery, thrown or faulted, is logged as `ClientEventFailed` at
      warning and never thrown.
    - Without a batch, an event is sent fire-and-forget.
  - `NullRealtimeStatus`, which is internal.
  - `NotificationNames`, the four grammars.
  - `NotificationsLog`.
  - `NotificationsComposition`, called from `CompositionPipeline.Realise` after `DataComposition`.
    It binds `NotificationsOptions` through `OptionsRegistrar`, and registers `TryAddScoped` of the
    null publisher and `TryAddSingleton` of the null status.
- `Composition/Realizers/`: three realizers, added to `ContributionRealizerSet.CreateBuiltIn()`.
  - Each reports a null descriptor or name as a composition problem.
  - Each records `(item, owner)` on `TellmaCompositionRecord`, in the new lists `NotificationTypes`,
    `NotificationChannels` and `ClientEvents`.
  - Each registers the descriptor, or the event item, as a singleton. The registries take these
    as `IEnumerable<…>`, which is the value-set pattern.
- `ServiceContributionRealizer` now also records every registration it makes, in the new
  `TellmaCompositionRecord.Services`. The channels check uses this to name a sender's feature.
- `Composition/Checks/`: `NotificationTypesCheck`, `NotificationChannelsCheck` and
  `ClientEventsCheck`, named `core.notification-types`, `core.notification-channels` and
  `core.client-events`. All three have `RequiresInfrastructure = false` and are registered in
  `RegisterBuiltInChecks`. Every problem names the owning feature.
  - Types: key and category grammars and lengths; unique keys; a defined severity; the
    `Inbox = false` rule; and a target that is a top-level entity of the model with an `int` or
    `long` key. A model that fails is the entity-metadata check's to report, so targets are then
    skipped.
  - Channels: the grammar; unique keys; `inbox` and `email` present; and the senders, resolved in
    the gate's scope. Each sender must name a registered channel other than `inbox`, with at most
    one sender per channel. A sender that cannot be created is reported.
  - Events: the grammar and uniqueness.
- `CoreFeature.Contribute`:
  - the three registries and the asset contributor;
  - `NotificationType(core.user.added, users, Mutable: true, TargetResource: null)`;
  - `NotificationChannel(inbox, true)` and `NotificationChannel(email, false)`, with the keys
    exposed as `CoreFeature.InboxChannel` and `CoreFeature.EmailChannel`;
  - `ClientEvent("inbox.changed")` and `ClientEvent("session.ended")`.

### Deviations

1. The three realizers live in `Tellma.Core.Composition.Realizers`, beside every other realizer.
   §1.1 lists them under the `Tellma.Core.Notifications` row.
2. The check names carry the `core.` prefix of every built-in check (`core.notification-types`,
   `core.notification-channels`, `core.client-events`). §11 names them without it.
3. §11's `notification-options` check is not a separate check. `NotificationsOptions` binds through
   `OptionsRegistrar`, and `core.options` validates it with every other options type. This is the
   D-016 pattern.
4. A `TargetResource` must name a top-level entity of the model, which is a stack resource. §11 also
   accepts "a registered securable resource". No securable registry exists yet, so a non-entity
   securable target is refused today. The seam is commented in `NotificationTypesCheck.CheckTargets`.
5. The three table types are classes only. They are not contributed to the model (`TableType<…>()`
   in `CoreFeature`), because that would regenerate the Acme and fixture migrations and change the
   model fingerprints. The checkpoint asks only for the classes, and §11's "the three standalone
   table types on the model" belongs with the statements that bind them (0021-b).
6. `ClientEventPublisher` is a public abstract base that is not in the spec. It keeps the refusals,
   the coalescing and the failure logging in one place for the null publisher and the future SignalR
   one. The tenantless-scope refusal of §6.4 goes into its `ValidateScope` hook. The null publisher
   applies only the §6.3 refusals, as §6.4 says.
7. A `ClientEvent` with null `UserIds` is refused with `ArgumentException`. The spec says nothing;
   an empty list means "every connected user", so null cannot.
8. `NotificationTypesCheck` also refuses an undefined `Severity`, which the spec does not list. A
   cast value has no name to store in `Severity varchar(8)`, and `StrictEnumJsonConverter` refuses
   it in the client metadata.
9. `NotificationsOptions` validates the two retention periods (at least 1 s, at most
   `int.MaxValue` s). §9.1 bounds only the counts.
10. Spec 0015 frame types are seeded here as contracts: `IWriteHost`, `WriteHostKind`,
    `IOpenWriteHost`, `EnlistmentOutcome` and `IConfirmations`. §4.4's `INotificationChannel`
    names `IOpenWriteHost`, which did not exist.
11. `TellmaCompositionRecord.Services` records every service registration with its feature, so
    `core.notification-channels` can name a sender's feature, as §11 requires.

### Open risks

- **The senders are resolved in the gate's tenantless scope.** This is the only way to read
  `INotificationChannel.Key`. A future sender whose constructor needs a tenant (or anything
  request-bound) fails the gate with "could not be created". `EmailNotificationChannel` (spec 0018)
  must keep its constructor tenant-free, or resolve tenant-bound dependencies lazily. The fix text
  says so.
- **The Guid-key rule is unreachable through composition.** `Entity<TKey>` constrains the key to
  `IBinaryInteger<TKey>`, and `SequenceConvention` refuses anything but `int` and `long`. So a
  target with a Guid key is tested on hand-built metadata. Through composition, such an entity
  fails `core.entity-metadata` first, and the target check is skipped.
- **The `PublishAsync` delivery of the SignalR publisher must still count `tellma.realtime.events`**
  and resolve the tenant. Both belong in its `DeliverAsync` and `ValidateScope`. The base does
  neither.
- **The coalescing table is per publisher instance, which is scoped.** A batch published on from two
  scopes would get two callbacks. Batches are scope-bound, so this cannot happen today.

### What reviewers should examine hardest

- `ClientEventPublisher.Publish`: the callback is registered before the batch is remembered, and the
  flush removes the entry under the lock. Is there any path where an event is silently held on an
  executed batch? There is one known case: a batch that failed keeps its pending entry until it is
  collected, and further publishes on it are silently dropped. Those events would not be sent
  anyway, but they are not refused either.
- The dedup key: user ids as a sorted distinct set, and the payload as its serialised JSON.
  Anonymous objects with the same members in another order are different payloads.
- `NotificationChannelsCheck.SenderOwners`: how a sender's feature is attributed when it was
  registered through a factory (it matches no implementation type).
- The `LocalizedText.Names` cache key (type, anchor name). `Attribute.GetCustomAttribute(…,
  inherit: true)` walks overridden properties, and the tests cover an override that does not
  restate `[Multilingual]`.
