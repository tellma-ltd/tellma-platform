# PC-077: IApiActionInvoker, write frames and enlisted writes

## Implementer notes

### What was built

Spec 0015 §11.3, §13.3, §14.2 (group attribution), §2.6 / §16.1 (the enlistments counter) and the
§18.2 enlistment and API action pins, over PC-076's write runs. Before this checkpoint the
pipeline's write host took no enlistment (`EnlistSaveAsync` and `EnlistDelete` refused every host),
and neither `IApiActionInvoker` nor `IOpenWriteHost` was registered.

- **Frames** (`Crud/Enlistment/`).
  - `EnlistmentFrame` is the shared base: the pair check against `IStackRegistry.Enlistments`, the
    participant check, the phase check, the self-pair rule, the duplicate-root-id refusal, the
    groups in call order. Every refusal is `InvalidOperationException` (`Admit`).
  - `PipelineFrame` is the `IWriteHost` of every save, delete and action (it replaces
    `PipelineWriteHost`): preprocessing refuses, id assignment to the last validation round
    composes, the persist hooks and post-commit refuse. Its participants are the host's service,
    companions and components (`StackRegistry.ParticipantsOf`) plus each group's own.
  - `OpenWriteFrame` is the open frame of an `[ApiAction]`, a job or a provisioning step. It owns a
    validation scheduler, a context loader and the host's error list; the work runs as one task of
    those rounds, so an enlistment that waits for a round (a cold id buffer, a before image) is
    served while the work awaits it. `PersistAsync` (`OpenWriteFrame.Persist.cs`) leaves the rounds,
    waits for them to end (the `_ended` completion carries their failure), raises the groups'
    errors and then their warnings, and sends one persist batch with every group; nothing enlisted
    is no round trip. A second `PersistAsync`, a persist while a nested pipeline frame composes, and
    work that returns with unpersisted groups are `InvalidOperationException`.
  - `FrameFlow` is the flow's frame chain (async-local): the innermost frame is what
    `IOpenWriteHost` (the scoped `OpenWriteHostProxy`) forwards to, and what the front door reads
    to refuse `SaveAsync`, both deletes, both `ExecuteActionAsync` overloads, activate, deactivate
    and `DeleteWithDescendantsAsync` while a pipeline frame of the flow composes.
  - `GroupFrame` / `RerunHost` are the host a group's own contexts see, so a group's enlistments
    nest under it (B ahead of A with C after B composes B, C, A) and `MapPath` composes along the
    nesting. On a re-entry the host's validators re-run in memory against a `RerunHost`, which
    answers the n-th enlistment from the first run's n-th group (same target, kind and row count,
    the first run's ids on the rows; anything else is `StaleContextException`); the first run's
    groups are recomposed and replayed at their positions.
- **Groups** (`Save/SaveRun.Enlisted.cs`, `Delete/DeleteRun.Enlisted.cs`, `EnlistmentRounds.cs`,
  `IEnlistedGroup.cs`). An enlisted save runs the target's shape checks, platform preprocessing and
  `PreprocessAsync` at once, reserves its ids (warm buffer at once, else parked at the host's next
  round), loads its update rows' before images unfiltered on the host's loader (a missing row is
  `Entity.NotFound` at the mapped path), re-applies the `ServerOwned` members the writer names, runs
  the target's validators and components as tasks of the host's scheduler, then composes its
  statements live on the host's batch under its target's authority and runs the target's post-commit
  after the host's. No grant and no reference visibility is asked of it. The default concurrency is
  `Override`; under `Check` a default stamp is `Concurrency.StampRequired` at the mapped path. An
  enlisted delete by ids loads its rows only when something validates the delete.
- **Error re-homing view** (`Tellma.Core.Abstractions/Validation/ValidationErrors.cs`). An internal
  constructor over a parent list, a path map and the enlisted resource: each participant writes its
  own payload's paths, the parent receives each error at the mapped path with `enlistedResource`
  added; views over views compose innermost first. A map returning null is refused and leaves both
  lists untouched.
- **Attribution** (`Data/Batches/IBatchOrdinals.cs`, `DataBatch.NextOrdinal`). Each group marks the
  ordinal range of its statements; a persist failure whose `StatementOrdinal` falls in a group is
  translated by that group's failure context and re-rooted onto the host's paths.
- **`ApiActionInvoker`** (`Crud/Actions/ApiActionInvoker.cs`): the registry's own descriptor by
  reference (`StackRegistry.MethodOf`), the handler resolved per request, `RequireAsync` or the
  connected-member check (System passes), the body's annotations walked at its own members, the
  scope's operation named after the action, the method run inside `OpenWriteFrame.ForApiAction`
  through every `IApiActionInterceptor`, the first registered outermost.
- **Provisioning and jobs.** `ProvisioningFrameOpener` runs each step inside
  `OpenWriteFrame.ForProvisioning` (the step's class is the frame's participant).
  `OpenWriteFrame.ForJob` is the seam spec 0020's worker opens: its `PersistAsync` appends the groups
  to the worker's completion batch, after the outcome statement, and `AfterCompletionAsync` runs
  their post-commit once the worker reports the commit. Open frames enter `WriteFrames`, so a
  read-only tenant admits the persists of a read-only-safe action, a `RunsWhenReadOnly` job and a
  provisioning step.
- **Stack table ownership** (`Crud/StackTableOwners.cs`). `CrudComposition` replaces
  `NoStackTableOwners` with the registry-backed owner map (every root and child table of every
  stack), so a public-member append whose declared writes include another stack's table is refused
  by `DataBatch.CheckAuthority`. The platform's composers keep the internal channel.
- **Telemetry** (`CrudMetrics`, `CrudOperationNames`). `tellma.crud.enlistments`, once per enlisted
  write, tagged with the host's operation, the write's kind and the host's outcome; the
  `api_action`, `job` and `provisioning` operation names.
- **Docs.** A "Write frames and enlisted writes" section in `src/core/Tellma.Core/README.md`; the
  ARCHITECTURE.md "Cross-stack posting orchestration" row now names the C# enlisted-writes model.

### Deviations

1. **`IProvisioningFrameOpener.Open` became `RunAsync(scope, stepName, stepType, step, ct)`.** An
   open frame drives its own rounds while its work runs, so the step must run as a task of those
   rounds; a frame opened before the step and left open could not serve an enlistment that waits
   for a round. The step runner hands the opener the step's creation and run.
2. **The job frame is a seam only.** No worker exists in this tree (spec 0020), so nothing opens
   `OpenWriteFrame.ForJob` yet. Its `PersistAsync` appends to the completion batch the worker
   passes and answers a provisional outcome; the commit is the worker's.
3. **`EmailOutbox`'s frame-wide admission is not built.** Spec 0015 §13.3 admits `EmailOutbox` as a
   writer in every frame; no such type exists yet. `ActionContext.Host` is a full pipeline frame,
   so an action's validators and method enlist through it today; the outbox's admission belongs in
   `EnlistmentFrame.Admit` beside the participant check, and its pair in the registry, with spec
   0022.
4. **The checkpoint's "FKs in each direction" are check constraints.** The fixture's EF model is
   unchanged: the serialized fixture migration is not claimed here. `EnlistmentDatabase` adds, on
   tenant A only, `CK_Widgets_ReferencedShipment` and `CK_Shipments_ReferencedWidget` over two
   scalar functions: a widget coded `ref:<x>` needs a shipment with payload `ref:<x>`, a shipment
   with payload `of:<x>` needs a widget coded `<x>`. They fail inside the offending statement as an
   FK would, so the ordering each way is proven; the failure surfaces as `DataAccessException`
   (a CHECK, not an FK, so not `ForeignKeyViolationException`).
5. **The self-pair API action lives on the shipment stack.** The fixture's Widget does not declare
   `Enlist`, and a self-pair needs the target stack to accept enlisted writes, so the `restamp`
   action is the shipment service's (`EnlistedShipmentService`); the fixture's `Shipment` now
   declares `StackOperations.All | StackOperations.Enlist`. The widget side is the test instance's
   `EnlistingWidgetService`, not a shared fixture service.
6. **`FixtureTenant` maps no table to a stack.** The data-layer suites append every statement
   through `IDataBatch`'s public members the way a stack's own pipeline does, so they keep
   `NoStackTableOwners`; the append refusal is exercised by the suites that run the full
   composition (`ConnectDatabase` instances use the real owner map).
7. **Tests moved off `core.Jobs`.** `SavePersistTests` and `WidgetSaveProbe` wrote `core.Jobs` as
   the caller's own table; it is the `core.Job` stack's now, so those appends are correctly refused.
   They write a test table `dbo.CallerRows` that no stack owns. `GuardedPersistTests` sets the
   caller authority explicitly before its raw appends.
8. **The invoker checks the body before it opens the frame.** Spec §11.3 lists the frame's
   registration before the walk; walking first means a refused body opens no frame and runs no
   interceptor. Nothing else observes the order.
9. **Enlisted saves record no save metrics of their own.** `RecordSaveEntities` and
   `CountOverride` count front-door saves; an enlisted write is counted once by
   `tellma.crud.enlistments` under its host.
10. **An open frame needs the tenant's settings in its scope** (the loader's schema map is built
    over the tenant's multilingual shape); a scope without them is `InvalidOperationException` at
    the frame's open. Every tenant scope of the full composition carries them.

### Where this meets PC-078

PC-078 (built in parallel, `origin/pc/PC-078` at 5b0b6328) implements `IAccessGuards` and leaves
the union over enlisted groups to this checkpoint.

- **The guards' union.** Every persist path here (`SaveRun.Persist.cs`, `DeleteRun.Persist.cs`,
  `ActionRun.Persist.cs`, `OpenWriteFrame.Persist.cs`) decides `guarded` over the host's own tables
  plus every group's (`EnlistmentRounds.GroupTables`, which nested groups join, since a group frame
  shares its host's rounds). The host contributes the lock once, ahead of the first group, and the
  invariants once, after the last group and after `OnPersist`; a group's `ComposeAsync` never calls
  the guards. PC-078's `AccessGuards` places the lock in the transaction head and is idempotent per
  batch, so the two merge with no change and the invariants follow every enlisted write. A nested
  front-door pipeline frame inside an open frame has its own batch and its own guards.
- **`DataBatch`.** This checkpoint adds `IBatchOrdinals` and `NextOrdinal`; PC-078 adds
  `OnServerErrors` and its observer list. Different members, different hunks. Group attribution
  reads the failure's `StatementOrdinal` after translation and never registers an observer.
- **`UserService<TUser> : IEnlists<TUser>`.** The self-pair is admitted only from the open frame of
  one of `core.User`'s own `[ApiAction]` methods (`OpenWriteFrame.AdmitsSelfPair`); from a pipeline
  frame (a user validator, a user action's hooks) it is refused. An enlisted user group runs the
  stack's components as a front-door save would: `UserAccessRules` as tasks of the host's
  scheduler, `UserStampsCompanion` and `BootstrapSubjectEffect` composed under `core.User`'s
  authority with the group's unfiltered before images on the persist context.
- **Table ownership.** `StackTableOwners` maps stack root and child tables only, so
  `core.UserStamps` and `core.UserPreferences` belong to no stack and the companion's and the
  preference statements' public `Sql` appends pass the append check. PC-078's
  `AdministratorGuardTests` appends through the internal channel, and its fixture's raw seeding goes
  through `ConnectDatabase.ExecuteAsync`, outside any batch.
- **Goldens.** `fixture-stacks.txt` changes here only in the `fixture.Shipment` operations line;
  PC-078 changes the user and role services and appends its enlistment line.
- **Merge.** `git merge-tree --write-tree` of this commit with `origin/pc/PC-078` (5b0b6328) and
  with `origin/platform-core` reports no conflict. The merged tree was not built or tested here.

### Open risks

- On a re-entry the groups' own validators are not re-run; the host's re-run validators are matched
  to the first run's groups by position (`RerunHost`), so a validator that enlists the same writes
  in another order reads as stale rather than being reconciled.
- Groups share the host's `MaxValidationRounds`; a deep enlistment chain can exhaust it sooner than
  the host alone would.
- On an ambiguous commit (the proven-commit read failing) the groups' rows get no stamps back.
- An enlisted `Check` stamp is compared by the emitter only (no C# pre-check against the before
  image), so the refusal for a stale stamp is the statement's.
- Sequentially awaited enlistments on cold id buffers each cost a round; the 0 RT promise holds for
  warm buffers or enlistments made before the writer's last dispatch.

### What reviewers should examine hardest

- `OpenWriteFrame.RunAsync` / `PersistAsync`: the interplay of the work task, the scheduler's
  `BeginLeave`, the `_ended` completion and the failure paths (a failing round, a failing work, an
  unpersisted return).
- `EnlistmentFrame.Admit` and the phase gates of `PipelineFrame`: every way to enlist from a stage
  the spec refuses.
- Group composition and attribution in `SaveRun.Persist.cs`, `DeleteRun.Persist.cs` and
  `ActionRun.Persist.cs`: BeforeHost/AfterHost placement, nesting, the guards over the union of
  writes, and the ordinal ranges.
- `StackTableOwners` and the `DataBatch.CheckAuthority` interplay: every platform composer must
  stay on the internal channel.
