### Deviations

1. **Period styles are date-only in `tmFormatDateTime`.** `month`, `quarter` and `year` ignore
   `timeStyle`: "Q4 2026, 10:40 AM" names no moment. The spec does not say.
2. **`quarterPattern` is an option of the pure formats** (`TmDateFormatOptions`,
   `TmDateTimeFormatOptions`), default `Q{quarter} {year}`. The pure layer cannot reach the message
   catalog; the facade passes the `l10n.quarter` message.
3. **An unknown IANA zone formats as UTC**, with a dev-mode warning, instead of throwing a
   `RangeError` out of a template.
4. **`TM_TIME_ZONE` defaults to the `TM_DEVICE_TIME_ZONE` signal**, so an app that provides
   neither never labels.
5. **Relative days are calendar days in the display zone, literally.** On the one 25-hour day a
   year, an instant 24 h 30 min back can share today's date and then reads "today" rather than
   "yesterday" or "24 hr. ago".
6. **The era-separator rule changed** in `ɵtmFormatWithoutEra` (see below). Pre-existing shapes are
   unchanged over a 6 300-row sweep; only the new `weekday` style in `lt-LT` under a non-Gregorian
   calendar differs from what the old rule would have written (it keeps the comma: "1447 Ram. 16,
   ketvirtadienis").
7. **Golden shape.** The spec lists the columns loosely; the file has one array per formatter
   (`monthName` and `weekdayName` as their own arrays), `header` as an ISO instant or null on
   zone-label rows, a `grouping` column on every number row, and `quarterPattern` on quarter rows.
8. **Only instants warn on malformed input** (dev mode, once per value, the remembered set capped
   at 100) and show the text as-is. Dates keep their existing behaviour, with no warning.
9. **"messageformat biDiSupport off" has nothing to turn off**: the in-house interpreter never
   wrote isolates. The strip on the resolved message covers the Transloco path too.
10. **`formatMonthName` defaults to the ambient calendar**, with an optional override.
11. **`TmL10n` resolves `TM_UI_TRANSLATE` lazily**, on the first quarter format. An argument
    formatter may inject `TmL10n`, which would otherwise be a cycle.
12. **The narrow no-break space before a day period becomes a plain space** in the instant
    formats. Node's `formatToParts` writes U+202F where Chromium writes a space, so without this
    the golden generated in Node cannot hold in the browser, and a copied time would differ by
    engine.
13. **`./grid` ceiling 38 -> 38.5** for one attribute binding. Nothing in the touched lines could
    be folded to pay for it.
14. **The calendar packs' golden-row tests were replaced** by direct assertions (month names,
    period styles, the Ethiopic fourth-quarter cap, instants); the rows themselves are asserted in
    the root golden spec.
15. **"Primary within 7"** in the checkpoint's done-when predates the ceiling of 8. The primary
    measures 7.78.
16. A literal U+200E in a pre-existing comment of `tm-number-codec.ts` became an escape.

### Open risks

- **Primary headroom is about 0.2 KB.** The next primary-entry feature needs a ceiling raise.
- **`hour: 'numeric'` renders "7:42" and "0:30" in en-GB.** It is the spec's skeleton; a two-digit
  hour would need `hour: '2-digit'` per locale and is not what §D.4 asks for.
- **Other direct `Intl` uses are not stripped**: the date popup's weekday and month headers and
  day digits, and the file components' sizes. §D.14 lists formatters, and those texts are not
  copied or exported. I did not sweep them for control characters.
- **Node/V8 24 aborts in `formatToParts`** for `my-MM` under `islamic-umalqura` and `te-IN` under
  a non-Gregorian calendar with `{ year, month: 'long' }`. It is an engine bug in a path that
  existed before; Chromium formats them. The sweeps skip those combinations.
- **Zone names** (`tmFormatZoneName`) are the engine's CLDR text and differ by engine version
  (Chromium says "Arabian Standard Time" for Asia/Riyadh). They are not in the golden.

### What reviewers should examine hardest

1. **The era-separator rule** (`ɵtmFormatWithoutEra` in `l10n/tm-date-engine.ts`). When the era
   sits mid-pattern, exactly one of its two neighbouring literals is kept: the one before, unless
   that is plain spacing and the one after says more. Check it against patterns I may not have
   swept (era first, era last, era between two literals with no field on one side).
2. **Zone arithmetic** (`ɵtmZoneOffset` and the calendar-day difference in `l10n/tm-instant.ts`):
   daylight-saving days of 23 and 25 hours, half-hour and 45-minute offsets, instants before 1970,
   and the `hourCycle: 'h23'` reading of midnight.
3. **The label decision in column mode** (`ɵtmShowsZoneLabel`): a cell is bare when the header
   already carries its offset, labels on its own when its offset differs from the header's, and
   falls back to the per-instant comparison when the header is bare.
4. **Relative thresholds**: truncation (never rounding), the future side, the switch to an
   absolute date at seven days, and the fallback when `Intl.RelativeTimeFormat` gives no numeric
   word (Arabic duals).
5. **Reactivity of `zoneMismatch` and the facade's clock reads**: the `visibilitychange` revisit,
   and that a format called inside a `computed` re-runs when the locale, calendar, zone or clock
   changes.
6. **The golden diff.** Every pre-existing number and date row is unchanged except six number and
   nine date rows that lost a direction mark. `pnpm run golden:approve` regenerates it from the
   built `dist`.
7. **The bidi claim in the browser**: the grid number cell and `input[tmNumber]` geometry tests in
   `e2e/specs/bidi-free-text.spec.ts`, including Arabic-Indic digits.

## Review 1

### Findings

Paths are under `client/` unless they start with another root. "Golden" is
`projects/core/tellma-core-ui/l10n/format-golden.json`.

**R1-1 (major) — The zone label rewrites the hour: "1:30" becomes "01:30 GMT+3" in every
24-hour locale.**
`projects/core/tellma-core-ui/l10n/tm-instant.ts:425-442` (date-time) and `:461-470` (time).

- **What happens.** The label is not appended to the text: the whole instant is formatted again
  with `timeZoneName: 'shortOffset'`. With a zone field in the skeleton the engine resolves a
  different pattern and no longer narrows the hour to `hour: 'numeric'`. So the label changes
  the time it labels.
- **The golden pins it.** Same locale, zone and instant (en-GB, Asia/Riyadh,
  `2026-03-28T22:30:15.1234567+00:00`): line 7429 expects `29/03/2026, 1:30`, line 12192 expects
  `29/03/2026, 01:30 GMT+3`. For `time`: line 13469 `1:30`, line 14598 `01:30 GMT+3`.
- **How wide.** I compared the hour, minute and day-period parts with and without the label over
  49 locales. In Node 24 (ICU 78), Chromium 149 and WebKit 26.5 they differ in the same 25:
  en-GB, en-IE, en-ZA, de-DE, fr-FR, fr-CA, it, nl, pt-BR, pt-PT, tr-TR, ru, he, sv, da, nb, pl,
  zh-CN, id, vi, hu, ro, uk, bg and sw. Firefox 151 differs in fa, he, hu and bg only: elsewhere
  it writes the two-digit hour in both forms (`01:30` bare in en-GB and de). 12-hour locales
  (en-US, ar-SA, ar-EG, hi-IN) are not affected.
- **Failure scenario.**
  - §D.4's own example locale: a user in en-GB reads `7:42` on a device in the display zone and
    `07:42 GMT+3` on a device in another one.
  - In `'column'` mode (spec 0029 §12.1) one column mixes both forms: bare cells read `7:42`, and
    a cell across a DST change reads `07:42 GMT+2`.
- **No test would catch it.** Nothing asserts that the labelled text is the bare text plus the
  label.
- **Suggested fix.** Do what §D.4 says ("appends the zone's `shortOffset` name"): format once
  without `timeZoneName` and append a space and `zonePartOf(at, locale, timeZone, 'shortOffset')`.
  That removes the two `z` formatter variants (bytes back for `./l10n`) and makes the label
  additive in every engine. Add a unit test over the golden locales that the labelled text equals
  the bare text followed by the label, then re-approve the golden.
  - If the locale's own placement of the zone matters more (zh-CN writes `GMT+3 01:30`), the
    alternative is to always format with `timeZoneName` and drop that part with one adjoining
    literal when no label is wanted. That changes every bare 24-hour time in the golden to two
    digits.
- **Related, for Ahmad (a spec question, not a defect of this commit).** Under §D.4's skeleton
  (`hour: 'numeric'`) the bare hour is engine-dependent in 24-hour locales: Chromium 149 and
  WebKit 26.5 write `1:30`, Firefox 151 writes `01:30` (measured with the workspace's Playwright
  browsers). CLDR's own en-GB and de pattern is `HH:mm`. The implementer lists `7:42` and `0:30`
  under open risks; the second fix above would settle it at two digits everywhere.

**R1-2 (minor) — The plain-space rule (deviation 12) covers the instant formats only, and no test
fails if it breaks.**
`projects/core/tellma-core-ui/l10n/tm-instant.ts:376-378` (applied at `:427` and `:463` only);
`projects/core/tellma-core-ui/l10n/tm-date-engine.ts:192-233` (not applied);
`projects/core/tellma-core-ui/l10n/tm-instant.spec.ts:277-290`.

- **The rule itself is sound.** Measured: Node 24's `formatToParts` writes U+202F before AM/PM
  and its `format` does not; Chromium 149, Firefox 151 and WebKit 26.5 write a plain space in
  both. Replacing it makes the Node-generated golden hold in the browser. English and Arabic are
  consistent: Arabic never has U+202F, English has it before the day period only.
- **Where it is inconsistent.** Browsers replace U+202F in every `Intl.DateTimeFormat` text, not
  only before a day period. Node keeps it in date patterns: `tmFormatDate('2026-03-05', 'bg')`
  gives `5.03.2026<U+202F>г.` in Node and `5.03.2026 г.` in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit. The
  same holds for mk in every style and for be, kk and mn in some of the named-month styles
  (swept over 147 locales in Node).
  - Within Node, `tmFormatDateTime(at, 'bg', …)` writes the plain space while
    `tmFormatDateTime(at, 'bg', { timeStyle: 'none' })` and `tmFormatDate` write U+202F for the
    same date.
  - A date golden row for any of those locales would be generated with U+202F and fail in
    Chromium, which is the failure the deviation was meant to remove. No golden locale is
    affected today.
- **Test gap.** The unit suite runs in Chromium, which never emits U+202F, so the test at
  `tm-instant.spec.ts:277-290` passes with `withPlainSpaces` deleted.
- **Suggested fix.** Move the replacement into the shared path (`ɵtmFormatWithoutEra`, which
  `tmFormatDate`, `tmFormatDateTime` and the date popup all use) and keep it in `tmFormatTime`, so
  the rule is "every `Intl.DateTimeFormat` text", as the browsers have it. Make the golden
  generator throw on U+202F in a `date`, `dateTime` or `time` row, as it already does for bidi
  controls: that check runs in Node, where the character really occurs. Say the rule in
  `tmFormatDate`'s TSDoc and in ARCHITECTURE.md's l10n paragraph, since it is a text contract.

**R1-3 (minor) — In `'column'` mode the header and its cells can read the clock at different
times.**
`projects/core/tellma-core-ui/l10n-facade/tm-l10n.ts:100-102`, `:144-151`, `:246-249`, `:252-265`.

- **What happens.** `zoneMismatch` is a `computed`, so it holds the clock reading of its last
  evaluation. `zoneLabel()` and `labels(…, 'column')` call `this.now()` afresh on every call.
  Nothing ties the three to one reading.
- **Failure scenario.** Display zone Asia/Beirut, device Asia/Riyadh, the page stays visible
  across Beirut's change to summer time (no `visibilitychange`). The header was computed in
  winter and still reads `GMT+2`. A cell rendered afterwards compares against a header at the new
  "now" (+3, a bare header), so a summer cell at +3 stays bare under a header that says `GMT+2`:
  the reader is an hour off.
- **Suggested fix.** One memoized reading:
  `private readonly revisitedAt = computed(() => { this.revisit(); return this.now(); })`, used
  by `zoneMismatch`, by `zoneLabel()` with no argument and by `labels()` for the header instant.
  The header and the cells then always agree, and all move together on the next visit. Extend
  the test "recomputes zoneMismatch when the page is shown again" with a cell first rendered
  after the clock moved and before the visit.

**R1-4 (minor) — Instants at the edge of the range give unlocalized text or throw.**
`projects/core/tellma-core-ui/l10n/tm-instant.ts:418-424`, `:529-539` (the round trip through an
ISO date string), `:286` and `:353` (`Number.isFinite` as the only range guard).

- **`DateTimeOffset.MaxValue`** (`9999-12-31T23:59:59.9999999+00:00`, a common "never" value) in
  any zone east of UTC falls on 1 January 10000. `zoneDateOf` writes `10000-01-01`, which
  `tmFormatDate` cannot parse and returns as-is. Measured in Asia/Riyadh:
  `tmFormatDateTime(…, { timeStyle: 'none' })` and `tmFormatRelativeTime(…)` both give
  `10000-01-01`, with Latin digits in ar-SA too, while the form with a time gives
  `01/01/10000, 2:59`.
- **A finite number outside the `Date` range** (`1e20`) passes `Number.isFinite`, and
  `tmFormatZoneOffset`, `tmFormatZoneName`, `ɵtmShowsZoneLabel` and `tmFormatRelativeTime` (as
  `now`) then throw `RangeError: Invalid time value`. The file already refuses to throw out of a
  template for an unknown zone id (`:187-193`) and for `NaN`; this is the same case.
- **Suggested fix.** Guard with `Math.abs(at) <= 8.64e15` where `Number.isFinite` is used now.
  For the date-only and the relative fallback, either format the instant through Intl in the
  zone with the date skeleton (no ISO round trip; `quarter` still needs the calendar parts), or
  clamp the zone date to `9999-12-31`. Add both values to the unit tests.
