# PC-108: Leader election, leader documents and degraded polling

## Implementer notes

### What was built, and where

All client-side, in `client/projects/core/tellma-core/` unless noted. No C# changes.

- **Primary (`src/state/leader/`, spec 0025 §7.1, §8.6)**:
  - `tm-hub-types.ts`: `TmHubState` (unchanged), `TmHubStatus`, `TmClientEvent`, and the seam to
    the realtime connection that PC-153 will provide: `TmHubSink` (event, state, connected,
    refused, ended) and `TmHubConnection` (start, stop, nudge).
  - `tm-leader.ts`: `TmLeader` (`isLeader`, `hubStatus`, `events(name)`), a small facade whose
    internal `ɵisLeader`, `ɵhubStatus` and `ɵdeliver` the election writes. `hubStatus` starts as
    `{ connecting, now, true }`.
  - `tm-leader-documents.ts`: `TmLeaderDocument` and the multi token `TM_LEADER_DOCUMENTS`
    (default `[]`). `store/tm-doc-formats.ts`: `TM_STORE_EXTRA_FORMATS` is now built from it
    (name to `formatVersion`), as the PC-075 watchlist item asked.
  - `app/tm-app-init.ts`: `TM_LEADER_LOAD` (internal) loads `@tellma/core/leader` after the first
    render, as the recent pages are loaded, through one shared `start` helper; a failed load is
    reported as `tm.error.captured { source: 'tm-leader' }`.
  - `tm-state-dump.ts`: the dump's `leader` member, `{ isLeader, hubStatus }` (carried from D-216).
- **Lazy entry point `@tellma/core/leader` (`leader/`)**, loaded after the first render:
  - `src/tm-leader-election.ts`: `TmLeaderElection` (§7.2-§7.4). It follows
    `TmCacheRefresher.ɵpartition` and `TmSession.state`. While a tab shows a tenant to an active
    session, it queues on `tm:<slug>:<sub>:<t>:hub` with an `AbortSignal`, posts `leader.probe`,
    and notes the holder's `clientId` from `navigator.locks.query()`. The grant reports
    `leader-acquired { how }`. The release reasons are tenant, pagehide, freeze, session and stolen
    (`leader-released { why }`). `pageshow` and `resume` queue again. A stolen or released
    leadership awaits the connection's `stop()` before it queues again. The steal rules:
    - hung: no `hub.status` within 5 s while the lock is still held;
    - hidden and disconnected: `leaderVisible: false` and not connected for over 30 s, re-judged
      when the 30 s are up; never against a visible or a `disabled` leader;
    - only while visible, after a 0-2 s jitter, and only if the holder is the one the probe noted;
      a changed holder means a new probe.

    A visible follower probes every 60 s while the mirrored state is not `connected` or a leader
    document's `pollWhile` holds. Followers mirror `hub.status` and deliver relayed `hub.event`s
    that their own `clientEvents` list. `ɵTM_LEADER_LOCKS` is the browser's `LockManager`, or
    `null`, in which case every tab leads alone.
  - `src/tm-leadership.ts`: `TmLeadership`, the leader's work from grant to release:
    - On election: `hub.status { connecting }`, `refresh(t, ['tenant'], 'leader')` (see deviation
      3), and a read of every leader document.
    - It follows `tenant.realtime`: when true, it creates the connection through
      `ɵTM_HUB_FACTORY` and starts it; when false, it stops the connection and enters `disabled`.
      With no tenant facts yet, it waits.
    - Every connection state is reported to all tabs. Each successful (re)connect catches up:
      `refresh 'leader'` and every document.
    - Relay and dispatch (§8.4): events of other tenants are dropped; every other event is relayed;
      events this build knows are delivered and acted on. `cache.changed` goes to
      `TmCacheRefresher.ɵcacheChanged`, which applies §4.4's actions and jitter, after a shape
      check. A document's events trigger a read coalesced over 250 ms, with at most one read in
      flight and one queued.
    - Reads use `TmApi.read(route, {}, { signal, background: true, tenantId })`, and the `put`'s
      `savedAt` is the time the read was sent.
    - Degraded polling (§8.7) runs while realtime is `disabled`, or while the hub has not been
      connected for 60 s (`polling { cause }`, emitted on each change of cause). It does a
      `refresh 'poll'` and reads every document, at most once per 60 s, on the leader's own timer
      while the leader is visible, and on any probe. While connected, documents whose `pollWhile`
      holds are re-read on the same triggers.
    - `refused` and `ended` lead to a `terminal` refresh, and the connection is restarted once a
      sync lands (the seam for §8.5, which PC-153 owns).
  - `src/tm-leader.testing.util.ts`: the two-tab rig. Each tab gets its own injector, its own iframe
    and the iframe's real `navigator.locks` (so each tab is a client of its own to the lock
    manager, and removing the iframe is a crash), a linked tab bus, a scripted page, a recording
    refresher and a fake hub. The rig also provides `tmUntil`, `tmPause` and `tmAdvance` (real
    waits for lock grants, faked timers for the election's own waits).
  - `package.json` exports, budget, `staticGraphExcludes`, `tsconfig.lazy-entry-points.json`, the
    `tm/core-import-boundary` entry list (`tellma-core-tools`), and the API golden
    `client/api/core.leader.api.md`, which is empty because every export is internal (`ɵ`).
- **Testing (`tellma-core-testing`)**: `provideTellmaStateTesting()` replaces `ɵTM_LEADER_LOAD`
  with a loader that loads nothing, so a test app runs no election and every tab reports itself as
  a follower.
- **Docs**: the core README (entry-point table, a "The leader tab" section, the dump, the lazy
  import paragraph) and ARCHITECTURE.md's `@tellma/core` row.

### Deviations

1. **Most of the leader code is in the lazy `@tellma/core/leader`, against spec 0025 §1, which
   places the election in the primary.** The election, the leader's work and the polling go there;
   only `TmLeader`, the documents token and the types stay in the primary. This is the pattern
   D-216 accepted for memory and recents. As a result, `isLeader` is false and `hubStatus` is
   `connecting` until the entry point loads after the first render.
2. **The hub lock bypasses `TmLocks`.** It uses the browser's `LockManager` directly, through
   `ɵTM_LEADER_LOCKS`: the election needs `steal`, an `AbortSignal`, a held promise and `query()`,
   which `TmLocks` (a run-to-completion helper with an in-tab fallback) does not offer. Without
   Web Locks, each tab leads alone and no steal happens.
3. **The election's `refresh(t, ['tenant'], 'leader')` is skipped when the page has already seen
   a sync of the tenant** (the stored `sync.at` is at or after `performance.timeOrigin`). The spec
   calls this refresh "the warm launch's revalidation". After a cold entry, the cold sync has just
   run, and the refresher would send a second `client/sync` once the 2 s throttle passes. Warm
   launches and tenant switches into warm documents still revalidate. Catch-up on connect is
   unchanged.
4. **No realtime connection yet.** `ɵTM_HUB_FACTORY` defaults to `null` until PC-153's
   `/realtime` provides it. Until then a leader of a realtime tenant stays `connecting` and polls
   as `hub-down` after 60 s, which matches the spec for a hub that never connects.
5. **The sign-out on `session.ended { session-terminated }` (§8.5) is not wired.** A
   `session.ended` reaching the sink stops the connection and runs a `terminal` refresh; a
   terminated session's 401 then takes the expiry path. The `session-terminated` sign-out and the
   followers' own `terminal` refresh on `access-revoked` and `tenant-unavailable` are §8.5, which
   belongs to the `TmHub` checkpoint.
6. **`provideTellmaStateTesting()` disables the election in test apps.** Otherwise every component
   test would load `/leader` and run the election.
7. **No size check on relayed events.** Spec 0021 caps a client event's payload at 4 KB on the
   server, so the client relays events as they are (the watchlist's 6.2 item).

### Open risks

- **Real-time tests.** The election specs use real Web Locks across iframes, and lock grants cross
  to the browser in real time. `tmUntil` waits up to 3 s, so a very slow or heavily loaded CI agent
  could time out. 39 tests take about 60 s locally.
- **`performance.timeOrigin` (deviation 3)** stands in for "this page saw a sync". A tab restored
  from the back-forward cache keeps its original origin, which is correct there too.
- **Polling after a failover.** A follower that becomes leader after another tab's recent sync
  skips the election refresh (deviation 3). It revalidates on its first poll, 60 s later, or when
  it connects.
- **The holder re-check** relies on `query()` reporting the same `clientId` for one tab across
  calls, which Chromium does; WebKit's cross-engine e2e is a later checkpoint.

### What reviewers should examine hardest

- In `tm-leader-election.ts`:
  - The race between a steal and the queued request: the queued request is aborted before the
    steal, and the lock callback guards against `term.ended` and against `term !== this.#term`.
  - The `.catch` that tells "stolen while holding" apart from "aborted while queued".
  - `#reconcile`'s choice of release reason when several conditions hold at once.
- In `tm-leadership.ts`:
  - `#poll`'s next-due arithmetic: a read still in flight from over a minute ago must not spin the
    timer.
  - The `downSince` reset when leaving `disabled`.
  - The generation guards that ignore a stopped connection's late reports.
- The first-frame justification for the 0.28 KB the primary gains.
