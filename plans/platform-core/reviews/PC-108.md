# PC-108: Leader election, leader documents and degraded polling

## Implementer notes

### What was built, and where

All client-side, in `client/projects/core/tellma-core/` unless noted. No C# changes.

- **Primary (`src/state/leader/`, spec 0025 §7.1, §8.6)**:
  - `tm-hub-types.ts`: `TmHubState` (unchanged), `TmHubStatus`, `TmClientEvent`, and the seam to
    the realtime connection that PC-153 will provide: `TmHubSink` (event, state, connected,
    refused, ended) and `TmHubConnection` (start, stop, nudge).
  - `tm-leader.ts`: `TmLeader` (`isLeader`, `hubStatus`, `events(name)`), a small facade whose
    internal `ɵisLeader`, `ɵhubStatus` and `ɵdeliver` the election writes. `hubStatus` starts as
    `{ connecting, now, true }`.
  - `tm-leader-documents.ts`: `TmLeaderDocument` and the multi token `TM_LEADER_DOCUMENTS`
    (default `[]`). `store/tm-doc-formats.ts`: `TM_STORE_EXTRA_FORMATS` is now built from it
    (name to `formatVersion`), as the PC-075 watchlist item asked.
  - `app/tm-app-init.ts`: `TM_LEADER_LOAD` (internal) loads `@tellma/core/leader` after the first
    render, as the recent pages are loaded, through one shared `start` helper; a failed load is
    reported as `tm.error.captured { source: 'tm-leader' }`.
  - `tm-state-dump.ts`: the dump's `leader` member, `{ isLeader, hubStatus }` (carried from D-216).
- **Lazy entry point `@tellma/core/leader` (`leader/`)**, loaded after the first render:
  - `src/tm-leader-election.ts`: `TmLeaderElection` (§7.2-§7.4). It follows
    `TmCacheRefresher.ɵpartition` and `TmSession.state`. While a tab shows a tenant to an active
    session, it queues on `tm:<slug>:<sub>:<t>:hub` with an `AbortSignal`, posts `leader.probe`,
    and notes the holder's `clientId` from `navigator.locks.query()`. The grant reports
    `leader-acquired { how }`. The release reasons are tenant, pagehide, freeze, session and stolen
    (`leader-released { why }`). `pageshow` and `resume` queue again. A stolen or released
    leadership awaits the connection's `stop()` before it queues again. The steal rules:
    - hung: no `hub.status` within 5 s while the lock is still held;
    - hidden and disconnected: `leaderVisible: false` and not connected for over 30 s, re-judged
      when the 30 s are up; never against a visible or a `disabled` leader;
    - only while visible, after a 0-2 s jitter, and only if the holder is the one the probe noted;
      a changed holder means a new probe.

    A visible follower probes every 60 s while the mirrored state is not `connected` or a leader
    document's `pollWhile` holds. Followers mirror `hub.status` and deliver relayed `hub.event`s
    that their own `clientEvents` list. `ɵTM_LEADER_LOCKS` is the browser's `LockManager`, or
    `null`, in which case every tab leads alone.
  - `src/tm-leadership.ts`: `TmLeadership`, the leader's work from grant to release:
    - On election: `hub.status { connecting }`, `refresh(t, ['tenant'], 'leader')` (see deviation
      3), and a read of every leader document.
    - It follows `tenant.realtime`: when true, it creates the connection through
      `ɵTM_HUB_FACTORY` and starts it; when false, it stops the connection and enters `disabled`.
      With no tenant facts yet, it waits.
    - Every connection state is reported to all tabs. Each successful (re)connect catches up:
      `refresh 'leader'` and every document.
    - Relay and dispatch (§8.4): events of other tenants are dropped; every other event is relayed;
      events this build knows are delivered and acted on. `cache.changed` goes to
      `TmCacheRefresher.ɵcacheChanged`, which applies §4.4's actions and jitter, after a shape
      check. A document's events trigger a read coalesced over 250 ms, with at most one read in
      flight and one queued.
    - Reads use `TmApi.read(route, {}, { signal, background: true, tenantId })`, and the `put`'s
      `savedAt` is the time the read was sent.
    - Degraded polling (§8.7) runs while realtime is `disabled`, or while the hub has not been
      connected for 60 s (`polling { cause }`, emitted on each change of cause). It does a
      `refresh 'poll'` and reads every document, at most once per 60 s, on the leader's own timer
      while the leader is visible, and on any probe. While connected, documents whose `pollWhile`
      holds are re-read on the same triggers.
    - `refused` and `ended` lead to a `terminal` refresh, and the connection is restarted once a
      sync lands (the seam for §8.5, which PC-153 owns).
  - `src/tm-leader.testing.util.ts`: the two-tab rig. Each tab gets its own injector, its own iframe
    and the iframe's real `navigator.locks` (so each tab is a client of its own to the lock
    manager, and removing the iframe is a crash), a linked tab bus, a scripted page, a recording
    refresher and a fake hub. The rig also provides `tmUntil`, `tmPause` and `tmAdvance` (real
    waits for lock grants, faked timers for the election's own waits).
  - `package.json` exports, budget, `staticGraphExcludes`, `tsconfig.lazy-entry-points.json`, the
    `tm/core-import-boundary` entry list (`tellma-core-tools`), and the API golden
    `client/api/core.leader.api.md`, which is empty because every export is internal (`ɵ`).
- **Testing (`tellma-core-testing`)**: `provideTellmaStateTesting()` replaces `ɵTM_LEADER_LOAD`
  with a loader that loads nothing, so a test app runs no election and every tab reports itself as
  a follower.
- **Docs**: the core README (entry-point table, a "The leader tab" section, the dump, the lazy
  import paragraph) and ARCHITECTURE.md's `@tellma/core` row.

### Bundle

- `"."`: 38.49 KB before, 38.77 KB after on the linked measure (+0.28 KB). The ceiling moves from
  38.5 to 39 (rounded up to the next 0.5 KB, D-031). The added weight is `TmLeader`, the documents
  token with the store's formats, the startup loader and the dump member.
- `"./leader"`: born at 3.73 KB, ceiling 3.8. It is excluded from `"."`'s static graph, and the
  check passes.
- Spec 0025's primary services were already over their 9 KB share before this checkpoint. This
  checkpoint adds 0.28 KB to the primary; the other 3.73 KB stays off the first frame.

### Deviations

1. **Most of the leader code is in the lazy `@tellma/core/leader`, against spec 0025 §1, which
   places the election in the primary.** The election, the leader's work and the polling go there;
   only `TmLeader`, the documents token and the types stay in the primary. This is the pattern
   D-216 accepted for memory and recents. As a result, `isLeader` is false and `hubStatus` is
   `connecting` until the entry point loads after the first render.
2. **The hub lock bypasses `TmLocks`.** It uses the browser's `LockManager` directly, through
   `ɵTM_LEADER_LOCKS`: the election needs `steal`, an `AbortSignal`, a held promise and `query()`,
   which `TmLocks` (a run-to-completion helper with an in-tab fallback) does not offer. Without
   Web Locks, each tab leads alone and no steal happens.
3. **The election's `refresh(t, ['tenant'], 'leader')` is skipped when the page has already seen
   a sync of the tenant.** The spec calls this refresh "the warm launch's revalidation". After a
   cold entry, the cold sync has just run, and the refresher would send a second `client/sync`
   once the 2 s throttle passes. Warm launches and tenant switches into warm documents still
   revalidate. Catch-up on connect is unchanged.
   - *Amended in review 1 (R1-4):* "seen" now means the stored `sync.at` is at or after the later
     of `performance.timeOrigin` and the page's last return from the back-forward cache or a
     freeze, and not ahead of the clock. A restored or resumed page and a stamp left by a clock
     set back therefore revalidate.
4. **No realtime connection yet.** `ɵTM_HUB_FACTORY` defaults to `null` until PC-153's
   `/realtime` provides it. Until then a leader of a realtime tenant stays `connecting` and polls
   as `hub-down` after 60 s, which matches the spec for a hub that never connects.
5. **The sign-out on `session.ended { session-terminated }` (§8.5) is not wired.** A
   `session.ended` reaching the sink stops the connection and runs a `terminal` refresh; a
   terminated session's 401 then takes the expiry path. The `session-terminated` sign-out and the
   followers' own `terminal` refresh on `access-revoked` and `tenant-unavailable` are §8.5, which
   belongs to the `TmHub` checkpoint.
6. **`provideTellmaStateTesting()` disables the election in test apps.** Otherwise every component
   test would load `/leader` and run the election.
7. **No size check on relayed events.** Spec 0021 caps a client event's payload at 4 KB on the
   server, so the client relays events as they are (the watchlist's 6.2 item).

### Open risks

- **Real-time tests.** The election specs use real Web Locks across iframes, and lock grants cross
  to the browser in real time. `tmUntil` waits up to 3 s, so a very slow or heavily loaded CI agent
  could time out. 39 tests take about 60 s locally.
- **The "page saw a sync" test (deviation 3)** combines `performance.timeOrigin` with the last
  `pageshow` or `resume` that undid a `pagehide` or `freeze`. The original note claimed that a
  restored page needed no more than the origin. It did, as R1-4 found, and that note is
  superseded.
- **Polling after a failover.** A follower that becomes leader after another tab's recent sync
  skips the election refresh (deviation 3). It revalidates on its first poll, 60 s later, or when
  it connects.
- **The holder re-check** relies on `query()` reporting the same `clientId` for one tab across
  calls, which Chromium does; WebKit's cross-engine e2e is a later checkpoint.

### What reviewers should examine hardest

- In `tm-leader-election.ts`:
  - The race between a steal and the queued request: the queued request is aborted before the
    steal, and the lock callback guards against `term.ended` and against `term !== this.#term`.
  - The `.catch` that tells "stolen while holding" apart from "aborted while queued".
  - `#reconcile`'s choice of release reason when several conditions hold at once.
- In `tm-leadership.ts`:
  - `#poll`'s next-due arithmetic: a read still in flight from over a minute ago must not spin the
    timer.
  - The `downSince` reset when leaving `disabled`.
  - The generation guards that ignore a stopped connection's late reports.
- The first-frame justification for the 0.28 KB the primary gains.

## Review 1

Reviewed commit 241b37fd against 0025 §7.1–§7.4, §8.4, §8.6, §8.7 and §15's leader rows, §14's
Leader row, PLAN.md §6–§7, WATCHLIST 0025 and c07, and D-216. Run locally on that commit:
`node scripts/build.mjs` (exit 0); `size:check` ("." 38.77 of 39, `./leader` 3.73 of 3.8, and
"'.' statically reaches none of ./shell, ./memory, ./recents, ./leader"); `ng test core` on
`leader/**`, `provide-tellma.spec` and `tm-state-dump.spec` (4 files, 60 tests, all green, real
Web Locks across iframes). CI has not run on this commit.

The deviations, judged:
- **1 (lazy `/leader`).** Accepted. The only path from the primary is
  `import('@tellma/core/leader' as string)` (`src/app/tm-app-init.ts:78`), and the gate's static
  graph check confirms it. The +0.28 KB is honest: 38.77 measured. What stays (TmLeader, read
  before the election loads; the documents token, which the store's formats read at boot; the
  loader; the dump member) has a first-frame reason. It is the D-216 pattern.
- **2 (LockManager, no TmLocks).** Accepted. `steal`, `signal` and `query()` are needed. Without
  Web Locks every tab leads and polls, which breaks §8.7's cost bound, but only in insecure
  contexts.
- **3 (skip the election's refresh).** The cold-entry case is right: the refresher's step 3
  re-check would not absorb it, because the leader's trigger is newer than the cold sync. The
  rule is too broad, though: see R1-4.
- **4 and 5 (no connection; §8.5 deferred).** Allowed. PC-108's scope is "§8.4 (relay and act,
  without a connection)" with "the TmHub connection left as a seam". PC-153 lists §8.1–§8.5,
  including the followers' terminal sync and the session-terminated sign-out. The seam works, but
  its hand-off is not written down: see R1-5.
- **6 and 7.** Accepted. BroadcastChannel has no 4 KB limit, so an envelope slightly over 4 KB
  is harmless.

Web Locks, item by item:
- **Close and crash.** Correct, and tested (`crash()` removes the iframe).
- **Freeze.** Correct: it releases the lock or aborts the queue, and `resume` queues again.
  Tested.
- **Steal and split brain.** A stolen tab awaits `stop()` and then queues again. Duplicates
  during a steal are allowed by §8.4. One narrow race leaves a leadership orphaned: R1-2.
- **Handover mid-write.** Safe. `stop()` aborts the read (`TmApiError cancelled`, which is
  silent), and a put that is already running carries its send time, so the new leader's later
  read wins.
- **The holder re-check and the no-locks fallback.** Behave as described.

### Findings

**R1-1 (major): a connected, visible leader never arms its poll timer when a leader document
starts to satisfy `pollWhile` after the last `#arm()`. The document is then stranded on a lost
event, which §8.6 rules out.**
`client/projects/core/tellma-core/leader/src/tm-leadership.ts:440` (the put),
`:525-539` (`#arm`).
- **Cause.** `#arm()` runs only from the constructor, `visibility()`, `#set()` and its own timer.
  While the state is `connected`, `#poll()` returns `Infinity` unless some stored copy already
  passes `pollWhile`. The `downSince` clause is off, so no timer is set. Storing a document
  (`#read`'s `.then`) never re-arms.
- **Failure.** A single tab, or a visible leader with hidden followers, is connected. Inbox is
  `{ running: false }`. A job starts, `job.changed` arrives, the coalesced read stores
  `{ running: true }`, and `pollWhile` now holds. Then the job's later `job.changed` events are
  dropped. No timer exists and no follower probes, so the bell's progress freezes until the hub
  changes state or the tab's visibility flips. This is §14's multi-tab scenario ("job.changed
  events are dropped → the bell's ring and progress still move within 75 s"), and it fails in
  the common layout where the visible tab is the leader.
- **Why the test passes.** "re-reads a leader document every minute while its stored copy asks
  for it" (`tm-leadership.spec.ts:323`) stores `running: true` before `sink.state('connected')`,
  and that call is what arms the timer.
- **Fix.** Re-arm after each leader-document read that stores a copy, or after any change to a
  leader document's stored copy, since a panel's own put should count too (for example, an
  effect over the stored documents that calls `#arm()`).
- **Test.** Connect with `running: false`. Then deliver `inbox.changed` with the API answering
  `running: true`. Advance 60 s and expect a second read, with no state change and no follower.

**R1-2 (minor): a steal that races a grant of the same tab's queued request leaves a second
`TmLeadership` that is never stopped.**
`client/projects/core/tellma-core/leader/src/tm-leader-election.ts:286-296` and `:448-451`.
- **Cause.** `#candidate` checks the holder (unchanged) in the `query()` continuation, then calls
  `term.request?.abort()` and `#request(term, how)`. The old leader may release between the
  query's snapshot and the abort, for example the hidden disconnected leader closing. The lock
  manager then grants the queued request first. Abort after a grant is a no-op, and the steal
  then takes the lock from this same tab.
- **What happens next.** Both callbacks still run, since a granted callback is invoked even when
  the lock is stolen before it runs.
  - The first callback sets `term.release = release1` and creates leadership 1.
  - The second sets `term.release = release2` and creates leadership 2, overwriting
    `term.leadership` without stopping leadership 1.
  - Request 1's `AbortError` lands in `.catch` with `term.release !== release1` and is swallowed.
- **Failure.** Leadership 1's effect, poll timer and connection run for the life of the app. It
  keeps polling and relaying in a tab that may later follow, and `leader-acquired` is reported
  twice. The window is a few milliseconds, but nothing ever repairs it.
- **Fix.** In the lock callback, return `undefined` at once when `term.leadership !== null`. That
  releases the stolen lock. Request 1's rejection then takes the existing `stolen` path, which
  re-queues. Alternatively, `#granted` can stop any leadership already on the term.

**R1-3 (minor): leaving the tenant for no tenant is reported as `leader-released { why:
'session' }`.**
`client/projects/core/tellma-core/leader/src/tm-leader-election.ts:196`.
- **Cause.** With `partition === null` and an active session, `partition?.sub !==
  term.partition.sub` is `undefined !== sub`, which is true, so the code picks `'session'`.
- **Failure.** A leader that navigates to the tenant picker or a tenantless page mislabels a
  closed §15 value. The test (`tm-leader-election.spec.ts:77`) switches to T18 only, so this case
  is untested.
- **Fix.** Use `!active || (partition !== null && partition.sub !== term.partition.sub) ?
  'session' : 'tenant'`, and add a test that sets the partition to `null` and expects `{ why:
  'tenant' }`.

**R1-4 (minor): deviation 3's `performance.timeOrigin` rule skips revalidation beyond the cold
entry it was written for.**
`client/projects/core/tellma-core/leader/src/tm-leadership.ts:179-182`.
1. **Back-forward cache and resume.** §7.2 step 4 reruns the election on `pageshow` and
   `resume`, so step 2's revalidation runs too. A restored or resumed page keeps its
   `timeOrigin`, so any sync from before it was hidden (hours earlier) still counts as "seen by
   this page", and the leader skips. The tenant facts (state, realtime), which carry no header
   tag, stay stale until the first poll, 60 s later with realtime off, or until a hub connect.
   The implementer notes say this case is correct. It is not what the spec describes.
2. **A stamp ahead of the clock.** A stamp left after the device clock was set back is ≥
   `timeOrigin`, so a warm launch skips. The refresher treats such a stamp as unknown
   (`#sentSince`, `tm-cache-refresher.ts:512`, the PC-096 fix).
- **Fix.** Compare against the later of `timeOrigin` and the last `pageshow`-persisted or
  `resume` time, which the election can hand to `TmLeadership`. Also require `at <= now`, as
  `#sentSince` does.
- **Test.** A pageshow after a pagehide, with an old (but post-origin) `sync.at`, should
  refresh `'leader'`.

**R1-5 (minor): the §8.5 and connection seam's hand-off to PC-153 is recorded only in the
implementer notes, and one of its constraints is easy to get wrong.**
`client/projects/core/tellma-core/leader/src/tm-leadership.ts:54-57`, `:321-345`;
`tm-leader-election.ts:476-489`. PC-153 needs to know three things:
- (a) `ɵTM_HUB_FACTORY` lives in the lazy `/leader`. An app-level or `provideTellma` provider for
  it would statically import `/leader` into the primary and fail the static-graph check. The
  default factory in `/leader` itself must become the `import('@tellma/core/realtime')` call.
- (b) The followers' terminal refresh on a relayed `session.ended { access-revoked |
  tenant-unavailable }` belongs in `TmLeaderElection.#received`'s `hub.event` branch.
- (c) `ended` calls `hub.stop()` without awaiting it, and `#terminal` later calls `hub.start()`
  on the same connection. `TmHubConnection` must accept a `start()` while a `stop()` is still
  pending, or `#terminal` must await the stop.
- **Failure.** Without a note, PC-153 could wire the factory from the primary, or miss the
  follower side.
- **Fix.** Add a WATCHLIST 0025 entry "PC-108 → PC-153" with (a)–(c), as the PC-096 → PC-110
  entry did.

### Responses

- R1-1: fixed in the "PC-108: review 1 fixes" commit (child of 241b37fd).
  - `TmLeadership` has a second effect, `#asking`. It tracks the stored copy of every leader
    document that has a `pollWhile`, and calls `#arm()` when any `pollWhile` result changes, so
    the leader's own reads, another tab's put and a panel's put all count. Its first run only
    records the starting state, and `stop()` destroys it.
  - New tests: "re-reads a leader document whose stored copy starts to ask for it while connected"
    (a panel's put after `sink.state('connected')`; the poll fires a minute after the last read)
    and "re-reads a leader document whose own read starts to ask for it while connected" (a
    `job.changed` read that stores `running: true`, with no later events; the poll fires 60 s
    later). Neither involves a state change or a follower.
- R1-2: fixed in the same commit. The lock callback now returns `undefined` at once when
  `term.leadership !== null`.
  - When the queued request's callback ran first, the steal's lock is released, the queued
    request's rejection takes the existing `stolen` path (`leader-released { why: 'stolen' }`),
    and the tab queues again.
  - When the steal's callback ran first, the queued request's callback finds a leadership and
    returns. Its rejection is then swallowed, since `term.release` is the steal's.
  - New test: "keeps one leadership when its steal races a grant of its own queued request". It
    uses a scripted lock manager (`TmLeaderTabOptions.locks`), because the real one cannot be
    made to produce this order on demand. It grants the queued request, then the steal, then
    takes the lock from the queued one. It expects `acquired` [granted, granted], `released`
    [stolen], and one `poll` per minute.
  - Without the guard, the test fails. I checked this by removing the guard and running the test.
- R1-3: fixed in the same commit. The reason is `'session'` only when the session is not active,
  or when a partition is routed for another user (`partition !== null && partition.sub !==
  term.partition.sub`). New test: "releases on leaving for no tenant, as leaving the tenant",
  which sets the partition to `null` and expects `{ why: 'tenant' }` and no held lock.
- R1-4: fixed in the same commit, as ruled.
  - The election stamps `#returnedAt` whenever a `pageshow` or `resume` undoes a `pagehide` or
    `freeze`. It hands `TmLeadership` the value `seenSince = max(performance.timeOrigin,
    returnedAt)`.
  - The leadership refreshes `'leader'` unless `seenSince <= sync.at <= now`.
  - New tests:
    - "revalidates the tenant on a return after pagehide/pageshow" (`window`), and the same for
      `freeze`/`resume` (`document`), each with a sync from earlier in the page's life;
    - "revalidates the tenant when the last sync is stamped ahead of the clock" (one hour ahead).
  - The implementer notes' deviation 3 and open-risk bullet are amended.
- R1-5: WATCHLIST entry by the orchestrator. Point (c) is made safe in the same commit: `ended`
  passes its stop promise (with errors caught) to `#terminal`, which waits for both that promise
  and the `terminal` refresh before calling `hub.start()`. A refusal passes no stop, since the
  connection had already stopped trying.
- Bundle after these fixes: `"."` is unchanged at 38.77 KB of 39. `"./leader"` measures 3.84 KB,
  up from 3.73, so its ceiling rises from 3.8 to 3.9; the reason is noted in `package.json`.
