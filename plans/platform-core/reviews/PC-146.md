# PC-146: Inbox, preferences, delivery and retention handlers

## Implementer notes

### What was built, and where

- **Contracts** (`src/core/Tellma.Core.Abstractions/Notifications/`): `InboxSummary`,
  `InboxVerbResult`, `InboxRunningJob`, `NotificationValidationCodes` (`UnknownType`,
  `UnknownChannel`, `DuplicatePreference`, `CannotMute`).
- **Self-scope** (`Notifications/NotificationAccessCriteria.cs`): one `Read` criterion
  `UserId = me()` (reason `self`) on `core.Notification`, so the stack stops failing closed and a
  caller reads only their own rows. Registered as a singleton `IAccessCriteriaProvider` in
  `CoreFeature`.
- **Inbox** (`Notifications/InboxService.cs`, `InboxStatements.cs`), route `inbox`:
  - `summary`: one Read batch through `IGuardedBatchRunner`. The §5.3 statement returns the
    capped unread and unseen counts, `MaxUnseenSeverity` over the capped newest unseen rows,
    `Version` and `SeenAt`, `Latest` (newest first, ties by id desc), and `Running` (the caller's
    `Pending`/`Running`/`Held`/`PendingConfirmation` jobs with `ScheduledFor` null, capped, newest
    first, with their label columns). The actors of `Latest` go into a batch-local table, and a
    `Query<User>` over the users' leaf, projected to the related select, fills `Related`.
  - `seen`, `read`, `read-all`, `clear`, `clear-read`: one Persist batch each, the statement
    tail bumping `InboxVersion`, with `inbox.changed` to the caller published on commit. They are
    admitted on `ReadOnly` (`AllowWhenReadOnly`) and refused on `Frozen` through `PersistVerdict`.
  - Every action is `MemberOnly`. The statements' predicates on the caller are the authorization,
    so another user's ids are ignored.
- **Related projection** (`Crud/RelatedProjections.cs`): the related-select projection that
  `SettingsRelatedReads` computed privately, moved so the inbox's actors read uses the same rule.
  `SettingsRelatedReads` now calls it.
- **Preferences** (`Notifications/NotificationPreferencesService.cs`), route
  `notification-preferences`:
  - `get` reads the caller's stored rows and drops unregistered types and channels.
  - `save`: `NotificationPreferenceRules.Deviations` validates, then drops rows equal to the
    channel's `DefaultEnabled`. One raw-SQL persist (`ForCaller` on the preferences table) then
    replaces the caller's rows and bumps the caller's `PreferencesTag`.
- **Delivery** (`Notifications/NotificationDeliveryHandler.cs`, `NotificationDeliveryStatements.cs`):
  - The attribute is `[JobHandler("core.notification-delivery", BatchSize = 4, MaxAttempts = 12, InboxOnlyFailureNotices = true, RunsWhenReadOnly = true)]`.
  - One Read round trip loads every slice: the §4.5 users and preferences SELECTs, one pair per
    slice.
  - Each active recipient's `LocalizationContext` is negotiated once, from their stored
    preferences and the tenant's settings.
  - Per slice, the channels in registration order get the recipients enabled by their stored row,
    else by `DefaultEnabled`. `DeliverAsync` runs on the job's `IOpenWriteHost`.
  - `PersistAsync` runs once at the end. Nothing is persisted when a channel throws: the throw
    maps every item to Retry, or Failed on the last attempt.
- **Retention** (`Notifications/NotificationRetentionHandler.cs`):
  - The attribute is `[JobHandler("core.notification-retention", LeaseSeconds = 600, RunsWhenReadOnly = true)]`,
    with `BuiltInSchedule(2, …, "30 3 * * *")`.
  - Bands run read first, then unread. Each page is its own Maintenance batch with
    `Idempotent = true` and the §7 statement, using the database clock.
  - Each page records `tellma.notifications.retained{band}` and reports both totals with
    `IJobProgress.Report` (`Notifications_RetentionProgress`).
- **Metrics** (`NotificationsMetrics`): the summaries counter, read and cleared by verb, preference
  saves, and retained by band.
- **Resources**: the four validation messages, the progress message (ICU plurals in English) and
  the two handler labels, in `Strings.resx` and `Strings.ar.resx`.
- **Migrations**: the built-in schedule seeds a `core.Schedules` row and a `core.ScheduleStates`
  row. All three tenant migration sets were regenerated with dotnet-ef: the fixture entities, the
  migrator test distribution, and Acme tenant. Their diffs add only those two seed rows.
- **Tests**:
  - Unit, in `test/core/Tellma.Core.Tests/Notifications/`:
    - `NotificationDeliveryHandlerTests`: resolution, order, one round trip, negotiated context, a
      single persist, none on a throw, and the attribute policy.
    - `NotificationPreferenceRulesTests`: the rules and the self-scope criterion.
  - Integration, in `test/core/Tellma.Core.IntegrationTests/Notifications/`:
    - `InboxServiceTests`: RCSI on and off, plus the open-transaction summary under RCSI.
    - `NotificationJobsTests`: delivery end to end through `JobPollDriver`, the final failure with
      `core.job.failed` inbox only, and retention bands and paging.
    - Both use the `InboxDatabase` fixture with a change-tracking write audit.
  - No nightly-tier tests.

### Deviations

1. The inbox and preferences actions are `Mcp = Hidden`. Spec 0021 keeps `tellma_notifications`
   reserved and exposes notifications to MCP only through `tellma_query`/`tellma_get` on the
   stack.
2. `read` and `clear` refuse more ids than the Notification stack's `MaxIds` (`LimitExceeded`).
   They drop repeats and ids outside the `int` key range, which, like another user's id, name no
   row of the caller.
3. Preference validation paths:
   - `DuplicatePreference` sits at the later row's `[i].Channel`, with arguments `type` and
     `channel`.
   - A null row reports `UnknownType` at `[i].Type`.
   - `CannotMute` sits at `[i].Enabled`.
4. `save` returns the stored deviations, sorted by type then channel, rather than echoing the body.
5. The services and handlers are `internal sealed`. They are reached only through routing and the
   job registry.
6. **Channels cannot enlist rows inside the delivery job's frame.** `OpenWriteFrame.ForJob` admits
   the handler type alone. An `INotificationChannel` sender is a separate class, so its
   `EnlistSaveAsync` throws "takes no part in that frame". The spec's §13 e2e item ("the fake
   channel's enlisted rows committed with the completion") is therefore not covered. The fixture
   senders record their calls and the host kind (`Job`) instead. After Review 1, the frame stays
   as it is: `EmailOutbox`'s admission in every frame, which spec 0015 §13.3 names, and 0021 §12's
   "enlisted rows committed with the completion" test both go to PC-148. PC-148 is not blocked.
7. The retention progress is reported per page as the spec says, but the job completion statement
   does not write a buffered report's message. A short run therefore leaves `ProgressKey` as it
   was. The integration test asserts the rendered `JobProgress` debug records rather than the row.
8. The existing `JobContributionTests` and `TenantPollerTests` were narrowed to their own `acme.`
   keys and modelled handlers, because Core now contributes two handlers and a built-in schedule.
9. No Amharic strings: `Strings.am.resx` is partial and has none of the neighbouring keys.
10. (After Review 2.) A refused preferences save renders its paths as `body[i].type`,
    `body[i].channel` and `body[i].enabled` on the wire, not 0021 §4.3's `[i].type`. The body of a
    routed-service action is rooted at `body` by the 0016 §7.3 edge format, which an existing
    problem-mapping test pins (`body[1].code`), and that format wins. A web-edge test in
    `WebApiProjectionTests` pins the rendered paths. The spec wording is flagged for Ahmad.

### Open risks

- **PC-144 conflict.** PC-144 owns the scheduler and the Schedule stack and may also seed built-in
  schedules or regenerate the same three migration sets. Built-in id 2 follows spec 0021 §7. The
  regenerated `Initial` migrations will conflict textually on merge. Whoever integrates second
  regenerates them; never hand-merge.
- **Retention ordering.** Spec 0021 says retention "fires after `core.job-retention`". The cron
  here (03:30) only works if `core.job-retention` runs earlier. That schedule is not in this
  branch.
- **The delivery handler and tenant settings.** The handler reads `TenantSettings` from the
  request context, else from `ITenantSettingsCache`. A job scope with neither would fail every
  item.

### What reviewers should examine hardest

- `InboxStatements.Summary`: the count caps, `MaxUnseenSeverity` over the *capped newest unseen*
  rows, the `Running` filter and label columns, and the actors table feeding the `Query<User>`.
- The verbs' statement tails: the `InboxVersion` increment and the version returned, under RCSI on
  and off.
- `NotificationPreferencesService.SaveAsync`: the raw SQL's `DELETE`/`UPDATE`/`INSERT` against the
  stored rows, the `ForCaller` writes set, and the `PreferencesTag` bump.
- `NotificationDeliveryHandler`, in particular:
  - the slice-to-result-set mapping in the single round trip;
  - deduplication of a recipient repeated in a slice;
  - inactive users;
  - a stored row overriding `DefaultEnabled` in both directions.
- Deviation 6, the job-frame participant gap (ruled in Review 1: the frame stays, and the
  `EmailOutbox` admission goes to PC-148).

### Rulings on the implementer's points

**(1) Channels enlisting in the delivery job's frame (deviation 6).**
- The current code is correct per spec 0015 §13.3, and PC-148 is not blocked.
  - §13.3 limits enlistment to participants of the frame: the host's service, its components, the
    type the frame was opened for, or a participant of a group inside it.
  - It adds one explicit exception: `EmailOutbox` "admitted in every frame so any participant can
    enqueue mail through it".
  - Spec 0022 §4 has `EmailNotificationChannel.DeliverAsync` call `IEmailOutbox.EnqueueAsync(host,
    …)`. The writer that enlists `OutgoingEmail` is therefore `EmailOutbox`, not the channel.
  - The table in 0015 §13.3 (the `core.notification-delivery` row) says the same.
- `OpenWriteFrame.ForJob` admitting only the handler type is right. PC-148 must add the
  `EmailOutbox` admission to `EnlistmentFrame.Admit`, which does not exist yet. That checkpoint's
  brief should carry it, together with 0021 §12's e2e item ("the fake channel's enlisted rows
  committed with the completion"). That item is satisfiable there: the delivery job e2e with
  `OutgoingEmail` rows committing with the completion.
- **Cross-spec gap for Ahmad.** 0021 §4.4 says any "channel that writes rows enlists them with
  `host`", but 0015 §13.3's participant list does not admit an `INotificationChannel` sender. A
  future `push`/`sms` channel that writes its own rows would be refused.
  - Option (a): 0015 §13.3 admits the registered channel senders in the `core.notification-delivery`
    frame, for example as declared participants of `ForJob`.
  - Option (b): 0021 §4.4 restricts row-writing channels to writers admitted in every frame, as
    `EmailOutbox` is.
  - Interim: no change. The deviation stays recorded, minus its claim that PC-148 is blocked.
- **Safety now.** A refused `EnlistSaveAsync` throws in `Admit` before `Add`, so no group is
  recorded and nothing is half-committed. `PersistAsync` is never reached. The throw maps every
  item to Retry and then, at `MaxAttempts`, to Failed, with `core.job.failed` reaching
  administrators inbox only (pinned by `NotificationJobsTests.cs:93-114`). Nothing is dropped
  silently.
  - One caveat for the eventual design: an API-sending channel registered before a refused
    enlisting channel re-sends on each of the 12 attempts. This is the "can send twice" exposure of
    §4.4, but it is deterministic here.

**(2) Buffered progress not written by the completion (deviation 7).**
- This is spec intent, not a 0020 defect. 0020 §3.3 defines `Report` as buffered: "the next
  renewal carries it". §5.4 gives the completion statement as exact text. That text writes no
  progress columns besides `ProgressPercent = 100` on success.
- A short job therefore never persists its last `Report`. The retention job has no requester, so
  it never appears in anyone's `Running` and nothing reads that message.
- Asserting the rendered `JobProgress` debug records is an adequate pin that `Report` is called per
  page with the right totals.
- For Ahmad, optional: if a final message should outlive the run, 0020 would need progress columns
  in `JobOutcomeList`, or the handler would call `FlushAsync` after its last page. 0021 §7 asks for
  neither.

**(3) The built-in schedule and the regenerated migrations.**
- The three diffs are the dotnet-ef shape: renamed timestamped files, `HasData` blocks, and
  `InsertData` with `object[,]` for multiple rows. Each adds only the `core.Schedules` row 2 and its
  `core.ScheduleStates` row.
- `has-pending-model-changes` is clean for all three projects.
- Ordering: `"30 3 * * *"` follows 0020 §7.9's `core.job-retention` at `"0 3 * * *"` (row 1,
  PC-145). "Fires after" is realised by the cron offset in the same zone, which is what the specs
  intend. Nothing needs recording beyond the open risk already noted.
- Whichever of PC-144, PC-145 and PC-146 integrates second regenerates the three `Initial`
  migrations with dotnet-ef, never by hand.

**Self-scope 404.** `InboxServiceTests.cs:277-289` reads Ben's row as Ada through the
`Notification` stack's `GetByIdAsync` and asserts `NotFoundException` on `core.Notification`, while
Ada's own row is returned. The test would fail both with no criterion (fail closed, so 403) and with
an unscoped read, so it proves the 404. `query` is not covered (R1-5 b).

**Other deviations.** Deviations 1–5 and 8–10 are accepted.
