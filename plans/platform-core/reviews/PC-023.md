### What was built, and where

- **`chip`** (spec 0023 §4.3).
  - `TmChip` takes `label`, `tone` (the badge tones), `icon`, `href`, `removable` and `size`, and
    has the outputs `activate` and `remove`, plus `focus()`.
  - The label renders in a `<bdi>`, and projected content replaces it. It renders as an `<a>` when
    `href` is set, as a `<button>` while something listens to `activate`, and as text otherwise.
  - The remove button is named "Remove {label}". Delete or Backspace (no modifiers) on the chip's
    own controls emits `remove`.
  - Every control takes the pointer 24 px square through a transparent `::after` layer.
  - Host: `data-tone` and `data-size` (`ɵtmControlSize`), and `role="listitem"` inside a set.
  - `TmChipSet` takes `overflow` (`wrap`, `collapse` or `scroll`) and `ariaLabel`, and has the output
    `exit` and the method `focusLast()`. It is `role="list"` with one roving tab stop.
  - Keys: Left and Right are mapped by the live direction; Home and End move to the ends; an arrow
    past either end emits `exit`.
  - When the focused chip is destroyed, focus moves to the next chip, else the previous, else the
    set emits `exit('end')`. `internal/tm-chip-set-ref.ts` gives chips the `TM_CHIP_SET` seam.
  - Collapse mode measures the chips' natural widths inside a ResizeObserver. It runs when the set
    or a chip resizes, or when the chips change, never on input. The chips that do not fit get
    `tm-chip--collapsed`, which takes them out of the line and hides them.
  - Collapse mode then shows a "+N" list item. Its button is named
    `chip.more` with an `Intl.ListFormat` unit list, and has the same tooltip. Its digits are in
    the locale's numbering. A hidden "+{total}" probe sits in the same grid cell, so the item's
    width never changes with the count.
  - Scroll mode keeps one line with a hidden scrollbar. The focused chip is brought into view with
    a manual `nearest` `scrollBy`, focusing with `preventScroll`, so nothing outside the set
    scrolls.
- **`toast`** (§5.1).
  - `TmToasts` (root) exposes `show`, `clear` and `focus`, configured by
    `provideTellmaToasts({ maxVisible, overflow })`.
  - `TmToastRef` has `close`, `update` and `closed`, which resolves `timeout`, `dismiss`, `action`,
    `api` or `replaced`.
  - The host is `internal/tm-toast-host.ts`, a `popover="manual"` element on `<body>`, created on
    the first show and attached to the ApplicationRef.
  - It holds a `role="region"` stack, the cards newest first, the pill, and two persistent live
    regions (`status` and `alert`). Each region is emptied, then filled 100 ms later; messages that
    arrive in that window are joined.
  - Timers use `TM_NOW`. Only toasts on show run; every timer pauses on hover, on focus inside the
    stack and while the page is hidden, and keeps the time it had left.
  - Keyed `show` replaces a toast in place. `update` restarts the timer and re-announces only a
    changed text or tone.
  - An action runs inside a try/catch that reports to the ErrorHandler, then closes the toast.
  - Focus: `focus()` focuses the newest card's first button. Focus entering from outside records
    where it came from and pushes an entry on the shared Escape stack. Escape or closing the
    focused toast returns focus there, else to another toast.
  - Above modals: a MutationObserver on the CDK OverlayContainer's children re-shows the host
    (hide, then show, keeping focus), so a toast stays above a modal opened after it.
- **`banner`** (§5.2). `TmBannerBar` takes `tone`, `message`, `icon`, `actionLabel`,
  `dismissible` and `position`, and has the outputs `action`, `dismiss`, `previous` and `next`.

  - The tone glyphs are Info, TriangleAlert and CircleX. A visually hidden tone prefix reuses the

  - The pager is mirrored chevrons with "{index} of {count}" between them.
  - The buttons are 24 px. The bar has no live role.

### Deviations (also in the commit message)

1. `TmChip.activate` is an `OutputRef<void>` (from `outputFromObservable`), not an
   `OutputEmitterRef<void>`. The chip must know whether anything listens, because §4.3 renders
   the label as a button only when `activate` is bound. `OutputEmitterRef` gives no subscriber
   count, while the observable's subscribe and teardown keep one exactly.
2. The collapsed set's visible "+N" text has no key: it is "+" followed by
   `TmL10n.formatNumber(hidden)`, isolated with `dir="ltr"`. §10 gives only the accessible name a
   key (`chip.more`), and §2.2 requires locale digits.
3. A new key, `toast.more` ("{count, number} more"), is the default pill label. §5.1 says the pill
   counts the hidden toasts even without `overflow`, but §10 lists no key for it.
4. Count placeholders use the ICU `number` format (`{count, number}`, `{index, number} of
   {count, number}`), so digits follow the locale. §10 writes `{count}` and `{index} of {count}`,
   and the in-house interpreter prints a bare argument with `String()`.
5. The banner's buttons are its own 24 px icon buttons, not `tmButton`. `tmButton`'s smallest step
   is 30 px (`--field-height-sm`), and §5.2 fixes 24 px buttons in a 32 px bar. The `button` dependency in §1 is unused.
6. The banner's hidden tone prefix reuses `alert.info|warning|error` ("Info:", "Warning:", "Error:"). §5.2 gives no tone wording, and a tone carried only by colour fails WCAG 1.4.1.
7. `TmToastRef.update()` re-announces only when the title, detail or tone changes. Otherwise a
   progress toast's timer restart would re-read the same text.
8. The busy overlay blurs the focused element and holds every key at window capture while it
   blocks, on top of inert. §5.4 says only that Escape does nothing. Inert alone still lets keys
   reach the document's listeners (the shared Escape stack, the shortcut registry), and
   blurring takes focus off the trigger in every engine. Focus returns to the recorded element after the
   work.
9. The toast host re-raises itself above modals by watching the CDK OverlayContainer. §5.1 says
   the top layer keeps the host above modals. It does so only while the host is the latest
   top-layer element, and CDK 22 dialogs are top-layer popovers too.
10. Budgets above the §1 figures, each measured + ≈0.1 KB with a note: chip 5.7 (§1: 2),
    toast 5.4 (4), banner 2.6 (2), busy-overlay 2.5 (1). The primary `.` rises from 8.5 to 8.7
    for the ten new English strings (8.48 → 8.58). The measurements and savings are in the notes
    and below.
11. Tokens beyond §9's list: the remove-button inset and end gap, and a `--chip-target-size`
    (24 px), which the target rule needs. The stylesheet linter refuses literal sizes.

### Open risks

- **Busy overlay and dialogs.** The overlay re-inerts only new `<body>` children. A CDK dialog
  opened during a run inside an OverlayContainer that already exists is drawn above the scrim (it
  is a later top-layer popover) but stays inert, because its container is inert. Nothing in the
  specs opens a dialog during a busy run.
- **Toasts with a modal open.** `focus()` reaches the toast and Escape closes only the toast, as
  covered in e2e. Tab from the last toast button leaves the top layer for the document in DOM
  order. The CDK focus trap does not pull focus back, so Tab is not scoped to the toasts while a
  modal is open.
- **Collapse measurement.** Measuring briefly gives every chip `position: absolute` (the
  `tm-chip--measuring` class) inside the ResizeObserver callback, then applies the fit and runs
  `detectChanges` in the same frame. A consumer that styles chips with a different width when
  out of flow would measure wrong.
- **Chip focus tracking.** Chrome can fire `focusout` for a focused node that is being removed.
  `onFocusout` therefore waits a microtask before forgetting the focused chip. `chipDestroyed`
  reads the index from the chips as last rendered, because `contentChildren` has already dropped
  the destroyed chip.
- **Key blocking.** Keys are blocked at window capture only while a run is active. A listener
  registered at window capture before the overlay's own listener still sees the event; Angular
  and CDK listen on the document, so in practice nothing does.

### Findings

**R1-1 (major): a focused toast control can be removed without the host noticing, which loses
focus and, in Firefox and WebKit, freezes every timer and swallows Escape page-wide.**
`TmToastHost.releaseFocus` (`toast/internal/tm-toast-host.ts:169`) runs only from `ɵclose`
(`toast/tm-toasts.ts:345`). Three other paths remove the focused element:
- `show()` (`tm-toasts.ts:281`) pushes the focused card out of the `maxVisible` window.
- A keyed `show()` or an `update()` (`tm-toasts.ts:357`) drops the action button that holds focus.
- The pill (`tm-toast-host.ts:100`) disappears while focused, when another toast's `close()` or
  timeout brings the hidden count to 0.

In each case `@for` destroys the node and focus falls to `<body>`. The probe confirms that
Chromium fires `focusout` (relatedTarget `null`) for the removed node, and Firefox and WebKit
fire nothing. On Chromium focus is simply lost, not returned to its origin. On Firefox and WebKit
`onFocusout` never runs, with two results:
- `pausedBy.focus` stays `true`, so no toast times out again until focus happens to enter and leave
  the stack.
- The `tmPushEscapeDismissal` entry stays on top of the shared stack. Every later Escape is
  consumed by `dismissFocused`, which finds no card and does nothing, so a modal or menu opened
  under it no longer closes on Escape.

Scenario: the user presses the `tm.toasts.focus` shortcut and lands on a job toast's "Track"
action, then the job finishes and its keyed toast is replaced by one with no action, or three more
toasts arrive.

Fix: after each render of the stack (for example an `afterRenderEffect` in the host reading
`visible()` and `pill()`), check whether the host holds the focus state while the region no
longer contains `document.activeElement`. If so, hand focus to the newest card's first button,
else to the origin, else release the pause and the Escape entry. A unit test with
`maxVisible: 1` should show that `focus()` and a second `show()` leave focus on the new card, and
that Escape afterwards still reaches an entry pushed before.

**R1-2 (major): Tab is not scoped while a modal is open, and focus leaves the toasts for the page
behind the modal.** The region (`tm-toast-host.ts:47`) has no Tab handling, so Tab follows DOM
order out of the host, which `ensureHost` appends last to `<body>` (`tm-toasts.ts:480`).

When any CDK overlay ran before the first toast, the overlay container precedes the host. The
probe reproduces that order in Chromium: Tab from the last toast button goes to `<body>`, and the
next Tab goes to the first button of the `aria-hidden` page behind the modal. CDK dialogs do not
inert the page, and their focus trap does not pull focus back. That breaks §2.1 ("Tab and
Shift+Tab move between toast buttons") and puts focus on content hidden from assistive
technology.

The same region swallows Escape on the pill: `dismissFocused` (`tm-toast-host.ts:219`) finds no
card, does nothing, and the key is still consumed.

Fix: handle Tab on the region. Tab on the last control and Shift+Tab on the first should return
focus to the recorded origin when it is connected; that origin is inside the modal when the
modal's `focus()` shortcut brought the user here. Otherwise, wrap within the stack. Make Escape
on the pill (or anywhere without a card) return focus to the origin. Add an e2e test next to
`toast.spec.ts:226`: call `focus()` from inside the modal, Tab past the last toast button, and
expect focus back in the modal.

**R1-3 (major): destroying a chip set together with a focused chip declared in the same view
throws NG0911 and aborts the rest of that view's destroy hooks.** `TmChip`'s destroy hook calls
`set.chipDestroyed` (`chip/tm-chip.ts:187`). When that chip holds focus, `chipDestroyed`
registers `afterNextRender(…, { injector: this.injector })` (`chip/tm-chip-set.ts:237`).

`afterNextRender` registers on the injector's `DestroyRef`
(`node_modules/@angular/core/fesm2022/_debug_node-chunk.mjs:4489`, `:4435`). For the set's node
injector, that is the declaration view. `cleanUpView` flags that view destroyed before it runs
its destroy hooks (`_debug_node-chunk.mjs:4823`, `:4826`), and `storeLViewOnDestroy` throws
`RuntimeError(911)` on a destroyed view (`_pending_tasks-chunk.mjs:1761`). Nothing catches the
error, so it propagates out of the teardown, and the remaining hooks and effects of that view
are skipped.

This happens whenever the chips are written statically in the same template as the set, rather
than in a `@for` or `@if` (whose child views are cleaned up first). Example: drill-through link
chips; the user clicks one, it keeps focus, and the router destroys the page. The unit suite
only uses `@for` chips (`tm-chip-set.spec.ts:26`), so nothing catches it.

Fix: in `chipDestroyed`, return early when the set's own `DestroyRef.destroyed` is true. Add a
unit test that focuses a static `href` chip and destroys the fixture.

**R1-4 (minor): a busy run silences the toast and CDK live regions.** `block()` makes every
`<body>` child inert (`busy-overlay/tm-busy-overlay.ts:191`), including the toast host and CDK's
`cdk-live-announcer-element`. Inert subtrees leave the accessibility tree, so a toast shown
during a long run (an upload, or a background job finishing) has its live region filled while
inert. The text does not change after the unblock, so it is never announced. The same applies
to anything a component announces through `LiveAnnouncer` during the run.

Fix: in `makeInert`, skip `<body>` children that carry `aria-live` or are the toast host (for
example, every `[popover]` child, as CDK's own `aria-hidden` sweep does). The pointer stays
blocked by the raised busy host and the keys by the window hold, so nothing becomes operable.

**R1-5 (minor): the focused chip can collapse out from under focus.** `applyVisibleCount`
(`chip/tm-chip-set.ts:428`) gives a chip `tm-chip--collapsed` (`visibility: hidden`,
`tm-chip.css:109`) whatever holds focus. When the set narrows (a window resize or a sidebar
toggle) while a chip near the line's end is focused, focus falls to `<body>`. `updateTabStop` then
quietly moves the tab stop to the first chip.

Fix: when the focused control's chip is newly collapsed, move focus to the "+N" button with
`preventScroll`.

**R1-6 (minor): two of the riskiest paths are not pinned.**
- The re-raise above a modal opened *after* a toast is tested only by a `showPopover` spy
  (`tm-toasts.spec.ts:318`). The e2e "a toast shown while a modal is open" (`toast.spec.ts:207`)
  opens the modal first (`toast-story.ts:141`), so the host is created on top and the
  MutationObserver never runs. Add an e2e test that shows a toast, opens the modal, and
  hit-tests the toast with `elementFromPoint`.
- Scroll mode is tested in LTR only (`tm-chip-set.spec.ts:280`, `chip.spec.ts:142`), although
  the notes flag `scrollBy` in RTL for review. The physical arithmetic looks right, but RTL's
  negative `scrollLeft` is unpinned. Loop the e2e over `dir`, and assert that the chip is inside
  the box rather than `scrollLeft !== 0`.

**R1-7 (minor): members without TSDoc (AGENTS.md).** In `toast/tm-toasts.ts`:
- `assertiveText` (`:218`) and `announceTimers` (`:222`) sit under comments that document only
  the field above them.
- `ToastRef.closed` and `resolve` (`:116`–`:117`), and `ToastRef.close` and `update`
  (`:126`, `:132`), have none.

Give each its own comment. The injected fields follow the library's existing convention and are
not part of this finding.

### Responses

- **R1-1 — fixed in c224a4e.**
  - What changed in `toast/internal/tm-toast-host.ts`:
    - An `afterRenderEffect` reads `visible()` and `pill()`. After each render it runs
      `recoverFocus()`.
    - When the host holds focus (its Escape entry is set) but focus has fallen to `<body>`,
      focus goes to the newest toast's first button. Failing that, it goes back to the origin.
      Failing that, the hold is released: the timers resume and the Escape entry is popped.
    - `onFocusin` keeps the first origin while the hold lasts.
    - `onFocusout` releases the hold at once on a move to another element. A loss to nowhere
      (Chromium's report of a removed control) waits a microtask, by which time the render's
      check has already refocused.
    - `returnFocus()` and `leave()` are the one path out. `returnFocus()` releases the hold
      only when the origin actually took focus.
  - Unit tests:
    - With `maxVisible: 1`: `focus()`, then a second `show()`. Focus lands on the new card;
