- **Scheduler (`Crud/Validation/ValidationScheduler`).** `SettleAsync` adds a `Settling` state. A settling task no longer counts toward the quiet point. When the rounds reach a quiet point with nothing parked or requested and a task settling, the settling tasks resume together without a dispatch, and the rounds go on. The same strays a park refuses are refused here: outside every task, while another flow of the task waits, after the task left the rounds, after the task or the rounds ended. An abort cancels it. `ActionRun.Rounds` wires `Settle = scheduler.SettleAsync`.

### Deviations

1. **`ActionContext.ValidatedAsync()` (platform contract addition).**
   - The invite and issue-credentials methods must not call the identity server while a validator could still report an error, because a refused target would otherwise be invited or given a client.
   - §11.2 gives the method no way to wait for the other tasks' verdicts without reading `Persisted`, which ends its part in the rounds.
   - `ValidatedAsync` resumes the method at the quiet point where the rounds would end, at no round-trip cost.
2. **The actions' post-commit effects run in the method after `await Persisted`, not in `Batch.OnCommitted`.**
   - The effects are the membership hint and the previous client's delete. `OnCommitted` is synchronous, and these effects are async remote calls.
   - The semantics are the same: they run after the commit, outside the transaction, and once even when the persist is recomposed, because the method is not re-invoked.
3. **`PartialFailureException` is raised after the commit.** The answered prefix is written back first, so the prefix results include `SubjectMismatch` rows.
4. **An errored identity answer writes no subject, even when the server sent one.** A later invite links that account.
5. **The write-back statement declares its own `@inviteNow`** instead of the spec's `@tb{b}_now`. The TELLMA0003 analyzer reserves the `@tb` prefix and accepts only statement-own variables.
6. **`UserService`'s constructor takes a public `UserServiceDependencies` bundle** rather than eleven collaborators. A distribution leaf then derives with two arguments, and the composition can activate it.
7. **The invite's target rules are independent.** An inactive individual without an email reports both errors. A missing email is `Required` at the target.
8. **The §3.2 locale and contact checks apply only to new or changed values.** A stored value the deployment no longer offers does not block an unrelated save.
9. **The Acme end-to-end tests live in `Tellma.Distro.Acme.IntegrationTests`.** That is where Acme's host and in-proc engine are built.
   - They run the actions as the system principal in a tenant scope. Signing in an administrator over HTTP would add nothing the actions see.
   - The sandbox test runs, because §A.7 is implemented.
10. **`ScriptedIdentityClient` lives in `Tellma.Testing.Support`.** Both the unit and the integration suites use it.
11. **A duplicate subject (2627 on `UX_Users_Subject`) from the invite write-back is reported as `Unique` on the whole payload**, through the action pipeline's existing unique mapping. The spec says "at `Subject`". An action has no payload row to attach the path to, so this needs a decision on the action path's mapping.
12. **`InvitedAt` is written on every successful invite and kept on a refused one**, as the spec's write-back SQL does. The prose's "set once" is read as "set by the invite that succeeded".
13. **Labels.** Core's resources gain the three actions' labels and the two new securable actions' labels (`Core_User_Action_*`, `Action_Invite`, `Action_Credentials`, English and Arabic), so the permission editor and the action menu are not left on humanized English fallbacks.

### Open risks

- Cancelling the request after the identity server answered a prefix, but before the persist, loses the write-back for that prefix. A re-invite recovers it.
- On a persist that faults, the pipeline does not await the method. The new client's delete may complete after the exception reaches the caller.
- The write-back statement does not re-apply the caller's `Invite` filter. Round trip 1 already counted the targets under it, as for every action's persist.
- `UserAccessCriteria` turns a member without grants from `Denied` to `Filtered (Id = me())` for `core.User × Read`. The full `Tellma.Core.Tests` unit run is green (5640 passed, 2 skipped); suites outside the touched ones were not run.
- PC-F62: a users `delete` writes `core.UserPreferences`, `core.UserStamps` and `core.NotificationPreferences` through their `ON DELETE CASCADE` keys, and the delete plan declares none of them.
  - This checkpoint declares them locally, as PC-144 does for schedules: `UserService.ContributeAsync` appends `DeclareUserDelete` on the platform's channel to a delete's persist (`UserService.Delete.cs`).
  - It is pinned by a unit test and by an integration delete test.
  - PC-F62's general fix removes this workaround.
- The client fixture gained the three actions. Client specs that count actions were not run (no full client run).

### What to examine hardest

- `ValidationScheduler.SettleAsync` and its quiet-point branch: the interplay with `BeginLeave`, aborts and late registrations.
- `UserService.Invite.cs`: ordering around `Persisted`, the mismatch read, and that no secret, email or subject reaches a log.
- `UserService.Status.cs`: no existence oracle. Unreadable ids are omitted, the delivery call names only readable subjects, and a too-complex filter is an error, never a denial.
- `UserService.Credentials.cs`: the orphan-client handling on a failed persist, and the secret's single appearance (the result).
