### Deviations

1. **`ActionContext.ValidatedAsync()` (platform contract addition).**
   - The invite and issue-credentials methods must not call the identity server while a validator could still report an error, because a refused target would otherwise be invited or given a client.
   - §11.2 gives the method no way to wait for the other tasks' verdicts without reading `Persisted`, which ends its part in the rounds.
   - `ValidatedAsync` resumes the method at the quiet point where the rounds would end, at no round-trip cost.
2. **The actions' post-commit effects run in the method after `await Persisted`, not in `Batch.OnCommitted`.**
   - The effects are the membership hint and the previous client's delete. `OnCommitted` is synchronous, and these effects are async remote calls.
   - The semantics are the same: they run after the commit, outside the transaction, and once even when the persist is recomposed, because the method is not re-invoked.
3. **`PartialFailureException` is raised after the commit.** The answered prefix is written back first, so the prefix results include `SubjectMismatch` rows.
4. **An errored identity answer writes no subject, even when the server sent one.** A later invite links that account.
5. **The write-back statement declares its own `@inviteNow`** instead of the spec's `@tb{b}_now`. The TELLMA0003 analyzer reserves the `@tb` prefix and accepts only statement-own variables.
6. **`UserService`'s constructor takes a public `UserServiceDependencies` bundle** rather than eleven collaborators. A distribution leaf then derives with two arguments, and the composition can activate it.
7. **The invite's target rules are independent.** An inactive individual without an email reports both errors. A missing email is `Required` at the target.
8. **The §3.2 locale and contact checks apply only to new or changed values.** A stored value the deployment no longer offers does not block an unrelated save.
9. **The Acme end-to-end tests live in `Tellma.Distro.Acme.IntegrationTests`.** That is where Acme's host and in-proc engine are built.
   - They run the actions as the system principal in a tenant scope. Signing in an administrator over HTTP would add nothing the actions see.
   - The sandbox test runs, because §A.7 is implemented.
10. **`ScriptedIdentityClient` lives in `Tellma.Testing.Support`.** Both the unit and the integration suites use it.
11. **A duplicate subject (2627 on `UX_Users_Subject`) from the invite write-back is reported as `Unique` on the whole payload**, through the action pipeline's existing unique mapping. The spec says "at `Subject`". An action has no payload row to attach the path to, so this needs a decision on the action path's mapping.
12. **`InvitedAt` is written on every successful invite and kept on a refused one**, as the spec's write-back SQL does. The prose's "set once" is read as "set by the invite that succeeded".
13. **Labels.** Core's resources gain the three actions' labels and the two new securable actions' labels (`Core_User_Action_*`, `Action_Invite`, `Action_Credentials`, English and Arabic), so the permission editor and the action menu are not left on humanized English fallbacks.

### Open risks

- Cancelling the request after the identity server answered a prefix, but before the persist, loses the write-back for that prefix. A re-invite recovers it.
- On a persist that faults, the pipeline does not await the method. The new client's delete may complete after the exception reaches the caller.
- The write-back statement does not re-apply the caller's `Invite` filter. Round trip 1 already counted the targets under it, as for every action's persist.
- `UserAccessCriteria` turns a member without grants from `Denied` to `Filtered (Id = me())` for `core.User × Read`. The full `Tellma.Core.Tests` unit run is green (5640 passed, 2 skipped); suites outside the touched ones were not run.
- PC-F62: a users `delete` writes `core.UserPreferences`, `core.UserStamps` and `core.NotificationPreferences` through their `ON DELETE CASCADE` keys, and the delete plan declares none of them.
  - This checkpoint declares them locally, as PC-144 does for schedules: `UserService.ContributeAsync` appends `DeclareUserDelete` on the platform's channel to a delete's persist (`UserService.Delete.cs`).
  - It is pinned by a unit test and by an integration delete test.
  - PC-F62's general fix removes this workaround.
- The client fixture gained the three actions. Client specs that count actions were not run (no full client run).

### What to examine hardest

- `ValidationScheduler.SettleAsync` and its quiet-point branch: the interplay with `BeginLeave`, aborts and late registrations.
- `UserService.Invite.cs`: ordering around `Persisted`, the mismatch read, and that no secret, email or subject reaches a log.
- `UserService.Status.cs`: no existence oracle. Unreadable ids are omitted, the delivery call names only readable subjects, and a too-complex filter is an error, never a denial.
- `UserService.Credentials.cs`: the orphan-client handling on a failed persist, and the secret's single appearance (the result).

**R1-4 (minor). A faulted issue-credentials persist deletes the new client even when the commit is unknown.**
- Where: `src/core/Tellma.Core/Users/UserService.Credentials.cs:67-73` (`catch (Exception)`).

### Findings

**R2-1 (major). `ValidatedAsync` lets the identity call run for an action the pipeline then refuses: an unconfirmed warning is not an error.**
- Where:
  - `src/core/Tellma.Core/Users/UserService.Invite.cs:64-68` and `UserService.Credentials.cs:38-42`: the check is `context.Errors.HasErrors` only.
  - `src/core/Tellma.Core.Abstractions/Validation/ActionContext.cs:70`: the contract.
  - `src/core/Tellma.Core/Crud/Actions/ActionRun.Rounds.cs:136`: `ValidationOutcome.Raise` runs after the rounds, so after the method's remote call.
- Scenario: a validator of the users' stack calls `IConfirmations.Require(...)` during `invite`. It can be a distribution's `IEntityValidator<User>`, a component, or a leaf's `ValidateActionAsync` override.
  - The rounds settle with no error, so the method invites everyone at the identity server and composes the write-back.
  - The rounds end, `ValidationOutcome.Raise` throws `ConfirmationRequiredException`, and nothing is persisted.
  - The admin confirms and resends. The identity server answers `Reinvited` and emails every user a second time. The first emails went out before the admin had confirmed anything.
  - For `issue-credentials` the cost is one client created and deleted per unconfirmed attempt.
- No validator in Core raises a warning on these two actions today, so this is latent. But `ValidatedAsync` is a new public member, and what its documentation promises (the method "makes the call only while Errors holds none") is not enough to keep a refused action from calling out.
- The tenant-state verdict has the same window (`PersistVerdict.Ensure`, `ActionRun.Persist.cs:93`): it is checked only after the remote call. The web layer's mutation filter refuses such a tenant up front, so this one needs a non-web caller or a state change mid-request.
- Fix:
  - Make the settle point answer whether the action would be refused as it stands. For example, `ValidatedAsync` returns a `bool`, or the context exposes a member, that is false when the frame holds errors or unconfirmed warnings, or the tenant's state admits no persist.
  - The two methods then wait for `Persisted` to rethrow, as `FailValidatedAsync` does for errors.
  - Add a unit test with a validator that requires a confirmation: no identity call.
  - For Ahmad with deviation 1, since it shapes the public contract.

**R2-2 (minor). R1-4's fix gains nothing when the commit is unknown, and leaves one more client behind when the persist did not commit. Its spec refinement should not go to Ahmad as written.**
- Where: `src/core/Tellma.Core/Users/UserService.Credentials.cs:68-85`; `UsersLog.ServiceAccountClientKept`.
- R1-4's premise was that deleting the new client N after an unknown commit leaves the account unable to authenticate. It cannot authenticate either way: the method rethrows, the caller gets a 503, and N's secret is never delivered to anyone.
  - The commit happened. The row names N. Kept or deleted, nobody holds N's secret, and the previous client P no longer resolves to the user. An admin must issue again in both designs. That issue deletes N as its `previous`. P is never deleted by anyone, in both designs.
  - The commit did not happen. The row still names P, which works. Before the fix N was deleted and nothing lingered. After the fix N stays registered for good, unreferenced, with a secret nobody holds.
- So the fix changes nothing in the first case and leaves one more live client in the second. No lockout and no row naming a missing client results on either path. An unreferenced client resolves to no user, so this is hygiene, not a hole.
- The log text is also off. "Issue its credentials again if it does not authenticate" reads as if the kept client could work; it never can.
- Fix, either of:
  - Go back to the spec's rule: delete on any fault. It is simpler and matches 0018 §3.8 as written.
  - Resolve the unknown. On `CommitUnknown`, read the row's `Subject` once, outside any transaction. If it is N, the commit happened: record the hint, delete P, rethrow. If it is not, delete N and rethrow. A failed read keeps N and logs. This is the only variant that leaves no client behind.
- Either way, reword or withdraw the "for Ahmad" refinement in the R1-4 response.

**R2-3 (minor). A request cancelled after the identity server answered loses the whole write-back, not only a prefix's.**
- Where: `UserService.Invite.cs:82-95`; `ActionRun.Persist.cs:99` (the persist runs under the request's token).
- Scenario: the browser drops, or the request times out, while a slow identity call is in flight.
  - `InviteAsync` returns every result, since one chunk is atomic.
  - The method composes the write-back, and the persist is refused unsent because the token is already cancelled.
  - The users are invited and emailed. Their rows stay `New` with no `InvitedAt`. The admin's re-invite answers `Reinvited` and emails them again.
- The implementer's open risk names this for a prefix only. It holds for a complete answer too, which is the common case of one chunk.
- Spec 0018 §3.3 designs the prefix "so subjects that exist on the identity server are recorded". Under a cancelled token the pipeline cannot record them.
- Fix: none inside this checkpoint without a pipeline change.
  - For Ahmad: either an action may ask that its persist ignore the request's cancellation once it has called out, or the spec states that a cancelled invite emails again on retry.
  - Widen the open risk in the commit message to the complete answer.

**R2-4 (minor). The window between the two round trips is unguarded against three concurrent changes.**
- Where: `UserService.Credentials.cs:50,87-92`; `UserService.Effects.cs:45,62-68`; `UserService.Invite.cs:98-109`.
- Two concurrent `issue-credentials` on one account.
  - Both load `previous = P`, create N1 and N2, and commit in turn. The action's update overrides, so the later one wins. Both delete P.
  - The caller whose commit came first holds a `CredentialsResult` for a client the row no longer names: a 200 with credentials that never work. That client lingers.
- `deactivate` racing `issue-credentials`.
  - The deactivation loaded `Subject = P` and deletes P after its commit. The credentials action, validated as active in its own round trip 1, then links N.
  - The account is inactive with a live client N, against §3.6's "so the client obtains no token; reactivation needs `issue-credentials` again". The connect prologue still refuses the inactive user, so no access results. On reactivation N works without a new issue.
- `invite` racing a delete of a `New` user, or a deactivation.
  - For a deleted user the write-back's `UPDATE` matches no row, yet the result says `Invited` and a membership hint is recorded for the subject.
  - For a user deactivated in between, the hint says `IsActive = true`.
- None of these opens access. The first answers an admin with a success that is wrong.
- Fix:
  - Credentials: `context.Update` cannot say "where Subject is still the loaded one". Append a one-line assertion through `context.Batch.Sql` that raises when the row's `Subject` differs from `previous` or the row is inactive. The losing call then faults and deletes its own client.
  - Invite: let the second result set also return the ids the update did not touch for any reason, and answer those as an error, not their identity status. Take `IsActive` for the hint from the statement.
  - If this is judged out of scope, list the three under open risks in the commit message.

**R2-5 (minor, for Ahmad). The service-client deletes are not tenant-scoped, which a cloned sandbox would turn into a cross-tenant effect.**
- Where: `UserService.Effects.cs:62-68` (deactivate) and `UserService.Credentials.cs:89-92` (rotation). Both call `DeleteServiceAccountAsync` with the row's `Subject`.
- Client ids are distribution-wide, and the identity server's ownership check is per distribution, not per tenant.
- A sandbox made as a copy of its live tenant (spec 0011's future sandbox clone) carries the live service accounts' rows with their `Subject`. Deactivating one in the sandbox, or issuing it credentials there, deletes the live tenant's working client.
- Spec 0018 guards the sandbox for `invite` (`ExistingOnly`) and says nothing for §3.6 and §3.8.
- Not reachable today: only `issue-credentials` writes a service account's `Subject`, always with a fresh id, and cloning is not built.
- Fix: none required now. For Ahmad: either the clone clears service accounts' subjects, or §3.6 and §3.8 delete a client only when no other tenant's hint names it. Add it to WATCHLIST 0018.

**R2-6 (minor). The no-open-transaction probe has no positive control.**
- Where: `test/core/Tellma.Core.IntegrationTests/Users/UserIdentityActionTests.cs:638-645`, used at `:180`, `:229` and `:359`.
- The three assertions are `Assert.Equal([0], open)`. The probe reads `sys.dm_exec_sessions`, which shows other sessions only to a login with `VIEW SERVER STATE`. Under a login without it, or after a slip in the query, it answers 0 whatever the pipeline holds, and all three tests pass.
- It does catch the pipeline's own connection today: same database, a user session, pooled or not.
- Fix: one control. Open a second connection to the tenant database, begin a transaction, assert the probe answers 1, roll back.
