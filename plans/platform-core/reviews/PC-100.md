# PC-100: JobWorker hosting, polling, drain and the gap hold

## Implementer notes

### What was built, and where

- **Hosting** (`src/core/Tellma.Core/Jobs/`):
  - `JobWorker`: a `BackgroundService` that is also an `IHostedLifecycleService` and an `ITenantStateListener`.
    - `ExecuteAsync` starts the renewal schedule, then keeps one `TenantPoller` per served tenant (`Active` or `ReadOnly`). It does this on the catalog's `RefreshInterval` (`Synchronise`) and on every listener call.
    - A poller whose tenant leaves service is stopped and retired (`JobPollerRetired`). Its orphan gauge entry is dropped once no new poller replaced it.
    - `StoppingAsync` is the §6.7 drain:
      1. stop every poller;
      2. hand the dispatcher the polls in flight and `JobRenewalScheduler.StopAsync`;
      3. abort the pollers.
  - `JobsComposition`: registers the worker as the `IHostedService` and the `ITenantStateListener` only when `Tellma:Jobs:Enabled` is not `false`. Every other part is always registered (the parts are singletons).
  - `JobsHostValidation` (`IValidateOptions<JobsOptions>`): when the worker is enabled, refuses a `DrainTimeout` that is not shorter than `HostOptions.ShutdownTimeout`.
  - `Tellma.Core.csproj` references `Microsoft.Extensions.Hosting` for `HostOptions`, an orchestrator decision; ARCHITECTURE.md's package table now says so.
- **Polling**:
  - `TenantPoller`:
    - a bounded wake channel (capacity 1, `DropWrite`, so ten nudges make one wake);
    - a `PeriodicTimer` on `TimeProvider`;
    - the §6.4 adaptive interval (`Next`): full → 0, some → Min, nothing → doubling to Max, failure → Max, a nudge resets to Min;
    - the per-minute backlog sample and the hourly orphan sample;
    - read-only filtering: only `RunsWhenReadOnly` keys get a claim;
    - a slot reserved per key before each poll (none for a key at its limit).
  - `JobPoll` (`IJobPoll`) composes one Maintenance batch (`TransactionMode.None`), in spec order:
    1. gap check and hold;
    2. one claim per reserved key, each under a fresh token;
    3. tick step 1 (the `IScheduleTick` seam; no implementation is registered yet);
    4. backlog sample;
    5. orphan sample and retired-key cancel;
    6. heartbeat stamp, last.

    After the round trip it runs:
    - `Observe`: the instruments, plus `SchedulerGapDetected`, `JobRetiredKeysCancelled` and `JobWorkerHeartbeat`;
    - the dispatch callback;
    - the follow-ups:
      - `job.changed` to each claimed row's requester (D-128);
      - the gap notices, in Persist batches of 100 requests;
      - the withdrawals of the retired rows' `core.job.pending-confirmation` notices;
      - tick step 2.

      A failure in any follow-up but step 2 is logged (`JobPollNoticesFailed`) and leaves the poll standing.
  - `JobStatements.Poll.cs`:
    - `GapCheckAndHold`: the threshold in whole minutes, rounded up; the hold; the stamp and clock read; held counts per requester;
    - `BacklogSample`, `OrphanSample`, `RetiredKeyCancel` (with `OUTPUT` of the former status) and `HeartbeatStamp` (throttled to once a minute, with `OUTPUT` of the stamp written). Every statement is idempotent.
- **Concurrency and dispatch**:
  - `JobConcurrency`: the per-(tenant, key) `MaxConcurrency` counter, reserved at plan time, and the `MaxParallelBatches` bound, taken only when a claim that leased rows starts and only while a slot is free; a claim that finds none is released uncounted by `JobDispatcher` through `JobRunner.ReleaseAsync` (Review 2). A key refused at its limit wakes its tenant when one of its own batches ends; a tenant refused an instance slot, at its plan or at its start, is woken when a batch frees one, one per slot, first refused first (Review 1).
  - `JobDispatcher` (`IJobDispatch`): runs each claim on the thread pool, outside the poll's activity, under the host token. It counts `in_flight` and releases the slot when the batch ends; an unexpected failure is logged as `JobBatchFailed`.
  - `JobDispatcher.DrainAsync` is the §6.7 rule:
    1. cancel the host token;
    2. wait for the polls in flight and the batches (including those the polls start) up to `DrainTimeout`;
    3. stop the renewals;
    4. issue one final renewal per tenant with leases, at `ceil(ShutdownTimeout)` seconds;
    5. abandon what still runs (`JobHandlerAbandoned`, then `WorkerDrainTimedOut`).
- **Renewal** (§5.2):
  - `JobRenewalScheduler` checks once a second. Each tenant gets one renewal, sent when its earliest carried lease reaches a fifth of its length since its last renewal.
    - A failure is logged and backs the tenant off for one interval.
    - A lease whose requested cancel was ignored for one lease length is abandoned. It is no longer carried and lapses.
  - `JobRenewal` gained `IJobRenewal`, `RenewFinalAsync` (every lease set to one length), the renewal time stamp, and lost leases counted and logged once per lease.
  - `JobItemProgress`: a jump of 5 points or more from the last carried percent renews at once, at most once per job every 5 s. A failed jump renewal is logged and never thrown.
  - `JobLeases` / `JobLease` / `JobSlot` were extended to support the above.
  - `JobLeaseOwner`: `machine/pid/instance`, at most 128 characters.
- **Notices**:

### Deviations

- **D-1. The heartbeat stamp `OUTPUT`s the stamp it wrote**, and each stamp is logged (`JobWorkerHeartbeat`, Information) with the tenant scope. The stale-heartbeat alert reads that log, since a KQL alert cannot read each tenant's database.
- **D-2. The backlog sample ages each key's oldest row that is free to claim** (Pending, or Running with a lapsed lease, and due), measured on the database clock. A row a live lease holds is in flight, not backlog.
- **D-3. `lease_lost` and `JobLeaseLost` are reported once per lease,** however many paths (a renewal, a flush, a completion fence) find it lost.
- **D-4. Log events beyond the spec's list:** `JobPollFailed`, `JobRenewalFailed`, `JobPollerRetired`, `JobRetiredKeysCancelled`, `JobPollNoticesFailed`, `JobWorkerHeartbeat` and `JobBatchFailed`; Review 2 added `JobClaimReleased` (Debug) and `JobSynchroniseFailed` (Error).
- **D-5. A released slot wakes the tenants refused one** (`TenantPollerWake`), so a tenant at `MaxConcurrency` does not wait out a long interval once a slot frees. Narrowed in Review 1 (R1-1): a key refused at its own limit wakes its tenant only when one of that key's batches ends, and an empty claim's release wakes nobody.
- **D-6. `batch_fill` is not recorded for an empty claim;** it would drown the ratio in zeros at every idle poll.
- **D-7. `Microsoft.Extensions.Hosting` is referenced by `Tellma.Core`** for `HostOptions` (an orchestrator decision); ARCHITECTURE.md was updated.
- **D-8. `Notification_core_scheduler_gap`'s text was authored here;** the spec gives none (it names the arguments only).
- **D-9. `DrainTimeout < ShutdownTimeout` is validated at startup through `IValidateOptions<JobsOptions>`,** and only while the worker is enabled.
- **D-10. The drain also waits for the polls in flight** (and the batches they start), within the same `DrainTimeout`. Otherwise a claim that lands after the host token was cancelled would be dispatched past the drain.
- **D-11. The killed-connection and transient re-run tests inject the failure from a scripted tick statement** (an application-lock wait killed by the test, or a `50503` THROW), not a deadlock. A deadlock and a transient error take the same re-run path, which the data layer's fault tests already cover with a real deadlock.
- **D-12. The drain is also bounded by the host's own stop token**, beyond §6.7's `DrainTimeout` and the final renewal's bound (see the ruling below).
- **D-13. No option is read when the worker, the poll or the batch slots are constructed;** they read them when they first run (see the ruling below).
- **D-14. The shared unit-test compositions turn the worker off** (`Tellma:Jobs:Enabled=false`), since their tenants have no databases (see the ruling below).
- **D-15. `MaxParallelBatches` is a counter of started batches, not a semaphore held from plan to batch end** (§6.5 names `SemaphoreSlim`). A poll plans its keys while an instance slot is free and takes one only when a claim that leased rows starts, so an idle poll never holds one (R1-3). Since Review 2 (R2-1, the reviewer's option (b)) a claim that finds no slot left is released uncounted, as §6.7's drain releases a partition no handler started, and its tenant waits for the next freed slot: the bound always holds, at boot included.
- **D-16. Polling is staggered and jittered** (§6.4 names neither; R2-1). A poller's first poll waits a random part of `MinPollInterval`, and each wait is lengthened by a random part of a fifth of itself, so pollers started together do not stay in lock-step and the releases of D-15 stay rare.

### Rulings worth knowing

- **The links of §9 are on `process <key>`** (one per claimed row with a trace parent, as PC-098 built it); the poll's span is a plain root of its own and each `receive <key>` its child, as §9 places them.
- **Tick step 1 is a seam** (`IScheduleTick`, `IScheduleTickRound`) that nothing registers yet; the scheduler checkpoint fills it. Its `Full` and `Leased` take part in the §6.4 finding already, which a unit test pins.
- **A frozen, suspended or removed tenant's poller is stopped, not aborted.** Its current poll ends, and the batches it dispatched run on.
- **No option is read while the worker is constructed.** The host creates every hosted service before the startup gate runs, so `JobWorker`, `JobPoll` and `JobConcurrency` read their options when they first poll or reserve; an invalid option is the gate's to report with every other problem (the gate tests caught this).
- **The drain is also bounded by the host's own stop token** (`StoppingAsync`'s `cancellationToken`): once it fires, the wait ends and the final renewal is skipped, so a poll stuck behind a retry backoff cannot hold the host past its deadline.
- **Test compositions turn the worker off** (`Tellma:Jobs:Enabled=false` in Core.Tests' `TestComposition.SoundConfiguration` and AspNetCore.Tests' `TellmaTestHost.SoundSettings`): their fake tenants point at Azure SQL servers that do not exist, and a poll there logged `JobPollFailed` and, on a fake clock, never finished. The worker's own tests turn it on.
- **`JobRenewalScheduler` is `IDisposable` only.** A `ServiceProvider`'s synchronous `Dispose` throws on a singleton that is only `IAsyncDisposable`.
- **`JobConcurrency` holds no disposable.** Its bounds are counters under a lock, so a batch that outlives the host's disposal releases its slot harmlessly.
- **The abandonment clock starts once every item the lease still tracks had its cancel requested** (on the request of the last, or when the last item not cancelled leaves the lease). A handler that stopped one item and runs on with the others is not ignoring a cancel (R1-2).
- **The renewal schedule, the back-off, the jump interval and the abandonment clock run on `TimeProvider.GetTimestamp()`** (R1-5). The poller's sample cadences stay on the wall clock: a step there only shifts a sample.
- **Amharic strings** stay partial in this repo; the two new notice texts are in English and Arabic.

### Open risks

- **Hosts now run the worker.** Every composed host starts polling the tenants its registry serves unless `Tellma:Jobs:Enabled=false`. The Acme and Identity suites run with it on; a test host whose tenants have no reachable database logs `JobPollFailed` every 30 s and should turn it off.
- **The drain tests use wall-clock timeouts** (a 1 s drain, a 3 s shutdown). They assert ranges, not instants.
- **D-129 (open)** still governs a host stop after a handler observed an item on its last attempt (`Retry(0)` at `MaxAttempts`).

### What reviewers should examine hardest

1. `JobDispatcher.DrainAsync` and `JobWorker.StoppingAsync`: the order (host token, the wait, stop renewals, final renewal, abandonment); the polls in flight; and a poll that lands a claim after the host token was cancelled.
2. `TenantPoller.PollOnceAsync`: who disposes a slot reservation on every path (the round trip never returned, a claim with no rows, a dispatched claim).
3. `JobRenewalScheduler.CheckAsync`: the abandonment rule's clock (`CancelledAt + LeaseSeconds`) against a lease renewed after its cancel.
4. `JobPoll.RoundTripAsync`: the statement order against §6.2, and the re-run safety of every statement under `TransactionMode.None`.

#### Blocking

**R1-1 (blocking). A tenant with a key at its `MaxConcurrency` polls back to back for as long as
that key's batch runs.**
- *Where.*
  - `JobConcurrency.cs:92-95` adds the tenant to `_refused` when the refusal is only the
    per-(tenant, key) limit.
  - `JobConcurrency.cs:140-148` wakes every refused tenant on **any** release, including the release
    of an empty claim's reservation.
  - `TenantPoller.cs:256-268` releases empty claims inside the poll's own `onExecuted`.
  - `TenantPoller.cs:189-193` then turns that wake into an immediate poll at the minimum interval.
- *Failure scenario.* Tenant T has two keys: A (`MaxConcurrency = 1`, an export running for ten
  minutes) and B (idle).
  1. Every poll refuses A, which puts T in `_refused`.
  2. The poll reserves B, and B's claim leases nothing.
  3. `Dispatch` disposes B's reservation. `Release` wakes T, so the channel already holds a wake
     when `WaitAsync` starts.
  4. T polls again at once, and the cycle repeats.

  The result is a continuous loop of full poll round trips against T's database (gap check, claims,
  stamp) for the whole ten minutes. The §6.4 back-off is ignored. With many tenants, each one's
  releases also wake every other refused tenant. The isolated failure above shows it: poll 3
  started before the test ended the recompute batch.
- *Fix.*
  - Do not add a tenant to `_refused` when only its own key's limit refused it. That key's batch
    ending already frees the slot, and its release can wake its own tenant.
  - Wake instance-slot refusals only when a dispatched batch's slot is released, not when an empty
    claim's slot is.
  - Add a fake-clock unit test: with one key at its limit and one idle key, the poller's waits
    double (2 s, 4 s…) while the batch runs.
  - Make the existing test deterministic: it currently relies on scheduling to pass.

**R1-2 (blocking). A cancel requested for one item abandons the whole batch, so the other items
run twice concurrently.**
- *Where.*
  - `JobLeases.cs:266` sets `_cancelledAt` when the **first** item's cancel is requested.
  - `JobRenewalScheduler.cs:123-129` abandons the lease `LeaseSeconds` after that, whatever the
    batch's other items are doing.
  - Once abandoned, `JobRenewal.Carried` (`JobRenewal.cs:99`) no longer renews the lease.
- *Failure scenario.*
  1. A delivery batch holds 200 rows. A user cancels one of them.
  2. The handler correctly stops that item and keeps working through the other 199, which takes
     longer than the lease (300 s by default).
  3. The scheduler abandons the lease, and renewals stop.
  4. The database lease lapses while the handler is still running.
  5. Another instance (or this one, when `MaxConcurrency > 1`) re-claims the 198 rows not yet done
     and runs them again concurrently.
  6. This instance's completion then hits the fence and is discarded.

  External effects (emails, webhooks) happen twice. §5.2 abandons a handler that *ignores*
  cancellation, and §6.7 forbids letting a running handler's lease lapse while the process lives.
  A handler that honoured an item's cancel and carries on with the rest of the batch is neither.
- *Fix.*
  - Start the abandonment clock only when every tracked item's token has been cancelled for a
    requested cancel, which is the moment `RequestCancel` cancels `_batch`.
  - Alternatively, measure from the last item's cancel.
  - Add a `JobRenewalSchedulerTests` case with a two-row lease where one row is cancelled: it is
    still renewed after `LeaseSeconds`. The existing test only covers a one-row lease
    (`JobRenewalSchedulerTests.cs:133-151`).

#### Major

**R1-3 (major). Every poll reserves one instance batch slot per key for the length of its round
trip, so concurrent polls of other tenants claim nothing.**
- *Where.*
  - `TenantPoller.cs:207-213` reserves a `MaxParallelBatches` slot for every registered key with a
    free per-key slot before each poll, whether or not anything is due.
  - `JobConcurrency.cs:92` refuses every later key once the semaphore is empty.
- *Failure scenario.* `MaxParallelBatches` defaults to 8. Core alone will register about nine keys
  (§14), and a distribution adds more.
  1. An idle tenant's poll takes all 8 free slots for its round trip (tens of milliseconds).
  2. Any other tenant whose poll overlaps gets no claim for any key.
  3. Its "nothing" doubles its interval, and it is put in `_refused`.
  4. It is woken by the first release.
  5. Woken tenants poll at once, take the slots, and refuse the next ones.

  At the §6.4 sizing (500 tenants, about 17 polls a second) overlap is frequent. Claims serialize
  across tenants, and the wake-all release turns each refusal into extra round trips. The
  "seventeen polls per second" cost no longer holds, and a tenant with due work can wait repeated
  refusals behind idle tenants.
- *Fix.* Keep the per-(tenant, key) reservation, but do not hold instance slots for empty work.
  Either option works:
  - Check `Free > 0` at plan time, then acquire the instance slot at dispatch, accepting a brief
    overcommit bounded by the number of keys. Re-check the bound so `in_flight` stays honest.
  - Or cap a poll's instance reservations at a share of the free slots, and wake at most one
    refused tenant per freed slot, in FIFO order.

  Add a unit test with two pollers polling concurrently: both claim a due key.

#### Minor

**R1-4 (minor). `Synchronise` can apply a stale snapshot over a newer listener call, which restarts
a frozen tenant's poller.**
- *Where.* `JobWorker.cs:282` reads `_registry.Tenants` outside `_gate`.
- *Failure scenario.*
  1. The timer's `Synchronise` reads the snapshot (tenant 5 Active).
  2. The registry publishes Frozen. The listener's `Apply` takes the gate first and retires the
     poller.
  3. `Synchronise` then applies its stale Active and starts a new poller.

  The Frozen tenant (§6.2: never polled) is then polled, claiming and stamping, until the next
  refresh tick (15 s). The same race can reset a just-ReadOnly tenant's `ReadOnly` to false, so it
  claims keys that do not run when read-only.
- *Fix.* Read the snapshot inside the lock. Listeners run after the registry publishes, so the
  later of the two always sees the newer state.

**R1-5 (minor). The renewal schedule and the abandonment clock run on the wall clock.**
- *Where.* `JobLeases.cs:46`, `:215`, `:266`; `JobRenewal.cs:182`; `JobRenewalScheduler.cs:120`.
  All of these use `TimeProvider.GetUtcNow()`.
- *Failure scenario.* An NTP step backwards on the host by more than 4/5 of a lease (for example
  250 s on a 300 s lease, or about 12 s at the minimum lease) delays every renewal by the step. The
  database-clock lease lapses while the handler runs, and another instance re-claims the rows. A
  forward step merely renews early.
- *Fix.* Schedule renewals and the cancel-abandon interval on `GetTimestamp()`/`GetElapsedTime()`,
  which are monotonic. The fake clock drives both, so the tests keep working.

**R1-6 (minor). One exception in a renewal check stops lease renewal for the life of the
process.**
- *Where.* `JobRenewalScheduler.cs:166-170` calls `CheckAsync()` with no catch. The loop's handler
  catches only `OperationCanceledException`. `CheckAsync`'s synchronous part logs, builds scopes and
  enumerates leases.
- *Failure scenario.* Any throw in that synchronous part (a logging provider fault, for example)
  ends `RunAsync` silently. No lease is renewed again, every running handler's lease lapses while
  it runs, and the rows run twice. §6.1's "never lets an exception escape" intent applies here too.
- *Fix.* Wrap each `CheckAsync()` call in a catch that logs (for example `JobRenewalFailed` without
  a tenant) and continues. Add a test with a throwing lease enumeration or logger.

### Responses
