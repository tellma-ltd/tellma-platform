# PC-144: Scheduler and the Schedule stack

## Implementer notes

### What was built, and where

- **The tick** (`src/core/Tellma.Core/Jobs/`):
  - `ScheduleTick` (`IScheduleTick`, registered in `JobsComposition`): step 1 rides the poll's round trip (`JobStatements.TickStep1`, a fresh token per poll, the read-only key list only on a `ReadOnly` plan); `Round.RunStep2Async` runs step 2 in the poll's system scope after the round trip.
  - Step 2 (`FireAsync`): per leased row, `Evaluate` (parse + zone) then `ScheduleOccurrences.Compute`; job rows with `Id` = 1-based ordinal; one `ScheduleNextList` row per leased schedule; pauses grouped by reason with their `core.schedule.paused` notices; one guarded `Persist` batch: `TickStep2`, `Pause` per reason, `notifier.Notify`, a wake on commit when anything fired. `Observe` records `tellma.schedules.fired` / `.missed` (tagged by policy) / `.overlap_skipped` and logs each pause.
  - Failures: a lost lease (`Schedule.TickLeaseLost`, `THROW 50422`) logs `SchedulerTickLeaseLost`; any other failure logs `SchedulerTickFailed`; neither fails the poll. An ambiguous-but-committed batch counts nothing.
  - `IScheduleTickRound.RunStep2Async` now takes the poll scope's `IServiceProvider` (the seam's only caller is `JobPoll`; test doubles updated).
- **Occurrences** (`ScheduleOccurrences.cs`): `ScheduleCron` (Cronos `CronFormat.Standard`, zone lookup, tenant zone from the scope's settings) and `ScheduleOccurrences.Compute`, the §7.5 algorithm: owner/retired pause first; past the gap threshold `GetPreviousOccurrence` only (no enumeration); else the due occurrences and Coalesce / ReplayAll (catch-up window) / Skip (newest within 2 × max poll interval); next strictly after the database's now; none → `Exhausted`. Everything is UTC.

- **Notices, logs, metrics, strings**: `JobNotices.SchedulePaused*`, `JobsLog` (`SchedulerTickFailed`, `SchedulerTickLeaseLost`, `SchedulePaused` (§9's name), `SchedulerScheduleUnusable`; `SchedulerHandlerUnknown` reworded to cover user schedules), `JobsMetrics` (three counters), `Strings.resx` / `Strings.ar.resx` (every `Schedules_*` code, the paused notice, the two action labels).

### Deviations

1. The read-only variant of step 1 reads the key list's `[Id]` column (`StringList`'s only column); the spec says `[Value]`.
2. Step 1 also selects `Name`, `Name2`, `Name3`, for the paused notice's arguments.
3. The state upsert also clears `LeaseToken` / `LeaseExpiresAt`, so a save during a tick fails that tick's fence rather than being overwritten by a stale advance. Since review 1 every other updated row and every take-over target has its lease released too (`ReleaseTickLeases`, `NextDueAt` untouched), so a tick in flight never fires the arguments, policies or owner it read before the change.
4. `DeleteByQuery` is removed from the schedules' stack (a query delete names no ids for the built-in rule; D-132 pattern).
5. The service and the validator are generic over the leaf (`ScheduleService<TSchedule>`, `ScheduleRules<TSchedule>`); the rules live in the validator component, not the service, so a distribution's leaf keeps them.
6. The pause is one fixed statement per reason, stamped by the system user; the state needs no second update (step 2 already nulls `NextDueAt`).
7. `fixture.export` stands in for `core.export` (not yet registered) in the not-schedulable test.
8. A step-2 failure is logged and never fails the poll; the seam takes the poll scope's `IServiceProvider`.
9. A schedule the host cannot evaluate (unparseable cron, unresolvable zone) is parked: `NextDueAt = now + 1 h`, logged `SchedulerScheduleUnusable`, rather than left to its lapsing lease, which would re-lease it every 30 s and starve the 50-row tick.
10. Save recomputes `NextDueAt` only for new rows and rows whose cron or zone changed (a rename keeps a due occurrence due); activate only for targets inactive as loaded; deactivate nulls only active targets.
11. `HandlerUnknown` / `HandlerNotSchedulable` are not applied to built-ins on save or activate (their key is fixed by the feature and checked at composition).
12. `ArgumentsTooLarge` is measured in UTF-8 bytes against `JobLimits.MaxHandlerArgumentsBytes`, argument `maxBytes`.
13. `[Range(1, int.MaxValue)]` on `CatchUpWindowMinutes` (the column's check constraint, surfaced as a field error).
14. The paused notice goes to the administrators when the reason is `OwnerInactive` or the owner is the system user, else to the owner.
15. The gap path counts no missed occurrences (the gap check already reports the gap).
16. A delete appends one statement (`DeclareScheduleDelete`) that declares what the keys' delete actions wrote: a `DELETE` of the deleted schedules' states (`FK_ScheduleStates_ScheduleId`, cascade) and an `UPDATE` nulling their jobs' `ScheduleId` (`FK_Jobs_ScheduleId`, set null). Both match nothing by then; they put `core.ScheduleStates` and `core.Jobs` among the batch's writes, which the delete plan does not declare for non-sibling FK actions (the change-tracking audit caught it). It rides the platform's internal batch channel, so the jobs' stack authority does not gate it. Interim, per D-150: PC-F62 makes the delete plan declare these tables and removes this statement.
17. Two action-label strings (`Core_Schedule_Action_TakeOver`, `Core_Schedule_Action_RunNow`) were added in English and Arabic.

### Open risks

- **D-146 is not closed here.** A fired job is exposed to D-146's lease gap like any other job, through the worker; the scheduler itself adds no exposure (review 1 showed step 2's job insert and state advance are one transaction, and the fence plus `UPDLOCK`/`READPAST` block a re-fire).
- The tenant zone is read from the poll scope's negotiated settings at fire time; a zone the host cannot resolve falls back to UTC silently for schedules without their own zone (the startup check judges tenant zones).
- The step-1 plan test depends on the optimizer choosing seeks with 20 000 states; it is in the PR integration tier like the claim's plan test.

### What reviewers should examine hardest

- `ScheduleOccurrences.Compute` against §7.5, especially the Skip window, DST transitions (Cronos zone overloads) and the strict-after-now next due time.
- `TickStep2`: the fence count against the `next` rows, the sequence range and `ROW_NUMBER` ordering, `LastSkippedAt` only when rows existed but none fired, and `LastJobId` = the max fired id.
- `ScheduleService.ContributeAsync` per persist kind (the recording-batch append path) and the activate path that clears the reason and withdraws the notice in the same batch.
- `ScheduleRules.ValidateActionAsync`: the first-round loads (inactive owners, running jobs) and which codes apply to built-ins.

**Counts: blocking 0, major 2, minor 4.**

Rulings on the orchestrator's points:
- (1) **The declared `DELETE` of `core.ScheduleStates` is safe, but it is a per-service patch over
  a general delete-plan defect, and it misses the second FK action of the same delete.** It is
  appended by `ContributeAsync` after `batch.Delete(...)` in the same persist batch and transaction
  (`DeleteRun.Persist.cs:257-277`: the delete, then the participants through the recording view).
  When it runs, the FK cascade has already removed the rows, so it matches nothing and only
  declares the write. No orphan and no FK failure is possible. But `DeletePlan.Writes`
  (`DeletePlan.cs:89-130`) declares only the model's registered siblings (`SiblingConvention`),
  and `core.ScheduleStates` is a platform table outside that convention. The same gap leaves
  `FK_Jobs_ScheduleId ... ON DELETE SET NULL` undeclared. See R1-1, which asks for a 0015/0012 fix
  item.
- (2) **No double fire, and not the D-146 gap.** Step 2 inserts the jobs and advances
  `NextDueAt`/clears the lease in one transaction (`JobStatements.Schedules.cs:200-228`). If an
  ambiguous step 2 committed, the next tick reads the advanced `NextDueAt` and fires nothing more.
  If it did not commit, nothing fired, so firing again is the first fire. A re-run after a
  not-committed verdict that in fact committed late (`DataExecutor.cs:470-479`) hits the fence:
  the lease is null, `Schedule.TickLeaseLost` is thrown, and the re-run rolls back. A lapsed tick
  lease while step 2 runs is covered too. The fence takes `UPDLOCK` on the states, and step 1's
  `READPAST` skips locked rows, so a second ticker cannot lease them before the commit. The
  open-risk bullet "Double fire on an ambiguous step 2" is therefore inaccurate and should be
  removed. D-146's gap (a catalog outage lapsing a running job's lease) applies to a fired job
  like any other job, through the worker, not through the scheduler.
- (3) **The strings are correct.** `Core_Schedule_Action_TakeOver` and `Core_Schedule_Action_RunNow`
  follow `<Schema>_<Resource>_Action_<PascalName>` (`StringKeyConventions.ActionInfix`; compare
  `Acme_Customer_Action_SetCreditLimit` for `set-credit-limit`). They are present in en and ar, and
  `CoreResourceAuditTests` and `ClientEntityFixturesTests` pass with them. The Arabic reads well.
- (4) **A failing step 2 cannot hold a lease past 30 s, and it never advances silently.** Leases are
  time-bound (`LeaseExpiresAt`), and step 1 re-leases a row once `LeaseExpiresAt < now`. Every
  failure path is logged: `SchedulerTickLeaseLost` (Information) or `SchedulerTickFailed`
  (Error). The one silent path is host-stop cancellation, which is correct. The probe-confirmed
  commit is not a failure. The poll is not failed, which matches 0020 §7.6's last bullet (the
  lease lapses and the next poll recomputes). 0020 §9 names no step-2 failure event, so
  `SchedulerTickFailed`, `SchedulerTickLeaseLost` and `SchedulerScheduleUnusable` are additions.
  The pause event, which §9 does name (`SchedulePaused`), was renamed; see R1-4. Passing the poll
  scope's `IServiceProvider` is right: `JobPoll.FollowUpAsync` runs inside the poll's system
  scope (`JobPoll.cs:128-137`), which is the scope §7.6 asks for.

#### Major

**R1-1 (major). A schedule delete still writes an undeclared table: `core.Jobs`, through
`FK_Jobs_ScheduleId ON DELETE SET NULL`. The workaround for `core.ScheduleStates` patches one
symptom of a general delete-plan gap.**
- *Where.* `ScheduleService.cs:199-205` declares only `core.ScheduleStates`.
  `JobConfiguration.cs:89-93` gives `core.Jobs.ScheduleId` `DeleteBehavior.SetNull`.
  `DeletePlan.cs:89-130` adds to `Writes` only the model's registered siblings.
  `ScheduleTests.cs:417-430` deletes two schedules that have no jobs.
- *Failure scenario.* A user deletes a schedule that has fired at least once. Its job rows'
  `ScheduleId` is set to null by the FK action, a write to `core.Jobs` that the batch never
  declared.
  - Fixture tier: the change-tracking audit tracks every non-history table
    (`FixtureDatabase.cs:80-87`), so it throws "the batch wrote what it did not declare". This is
    the same mechanism the implementer saw for the cascade, per deviation 16.
  - Production: there is no audit, but `core.Jobs` misses its tag bump and its guard decision
    for that batch.
  - The same class recurs whenever retention deletes jobs, through
    `FK_ScheduleStates_LastJobId ... SET NULL` (0020 §14.4, PC-145).
- *Fix.*
  - (a) Raise a 0015/0012 fix item. `DeletePlan` should add to `Writes`, exempt from the caller's
    authority as siblings already are, the table of every model FK whose principal is a deleted
    node's table and whose delete behaviour is `Cascade` or `SetNull`. The per-service `DELETE`
    can then be dropped.
  - (b) In this checkpoint, add a delete test whose schedule has a job, which fails today. Then
    either land (a), or declare `core.Jobs` alongside `core.ScheduleStates` in the interim. If the
    interim is taken, note how the declaration passes the participant's authority check (§13.3):
    `core.Jobs` is owned by `core.Job`.

**R1-2 (major). Most of the validator's codes have no test that would fail if the rule broke,
including the one the spec and the checkpoint name explicitly (`ReplayAllRequiresAllow`).**
- *Where.* `ScheduleRules.cs:168-322`. Tested today: `CronNeverOccurs` (save), `TimeZoneUnknown`,
  `HandlerNotSchedulable`, `OwnerInactive`, `BuiltInImmutable` (delete), `BuiltInAlwaysActive`
  (deactivate) and `RunInProgress`, plus `RefuseOnRecord` through the permissions extra.
- *Untested.*
  - `ReplayAllRequiresAllow`: a 0020 §16 offline pin, and in the CHECKPOINTS unit list.
  - `CronInvalid`.
  - `ArgumentsTooLarge`, including the UTF-8 byte boundary of deviation 12.
  - `OwnedByAnotherUser`, including that a rename or a policy change by a non-owner passes.
  - `BuiltInImmutable` on an update: a built-in's policy/arguments/code change refused, and its
    name/cron/zone change allowed.
  - `BuiltInImmutable` from the `take-over` action's validation, not just the permissions extra.
  - `HandlerUnknown` on a save and on `activate`, a retired key included.
  - `CronNeverOccurs` on `activate`.
- *Failure scenario.* Inverting the `ReplayAll`/`Skip` check, or dropping the owner comparison in
  `CheckUpdate`, leaves every test green. 0020 §16 asks for "the schedule validator codes".
- *Fix.* Add a unit test of `ReplayAllRequiresAllow` (and `ArgumentsTooLarge`) over `ScheduleRules`,
  as §16 requires offline. Add integration cases to `ScheduleTests` for the save/update/action
  codes above. One save with several rows, as in
  `A_save_refuses_an_expression_that_never_occurs_...`, keeps them cheap.

#### Minor

**R1-3 (minor). A take-over, and a save that changes only the arguments or the policies, do not
release a tick's lease. A tick in flight then fires with the values it read before the change.**
- *Where.* `ScheduleService.cs:159-169` upserts the state only for new rows and cron/zone changes.
  Take-over (`ScheduleService.cs:77-85`) appends nothing to the state.
  `ScheduleTick.cs:152-163` builds the job rows from step 1's `RunAsUserId`/`ArgumentsJson`.
- *Failure scenario.*
  - A user takes over schedule S between steps 1 and 2. Step 2 still inserts S's job with the
    previous owner's `RunAsUserId` and `RequestedById`. Taking over is "the explicit way to run
    another user's schedule as oneself" (§7.8), so this job runs as the wrong user.
  - Likewise a schedule step 1 judged `OwnerInactive` is paused after the take-over made its
    owner active.
  - An arguments edit in that window fires the old arguments.
- *Fix.* Release the lease (`UPDATE ... SET LeaseToken = NULL, LeaseExpiresAt = NULL`) for every
  updated row and every take-over target, without touching `NextDueAt`. Deviation 3's fence then
  covers these changes too.

**R1-4 (minor). The pause's log event is `SchedulerSchedulePaused`; 0020 §9 names it
`SchedulePaused`, and the rename is not listed under Deviations.**
- *Where.* `JobsLog.cs:280-287`; asserted at `ScheduleTests.cs:199`.
- *Failure scenario.* A KQL alert or dashboard written against the spec's event name finds
  nothing.
- *Fix.* Rename the event to `SchedulePaused`, or list the rename as a deviation for Ahmad.

**R1-5 (minor). Deviation 9's parking and step 2's two failure paths have no test.**
- *Where.* `ScheduleTick.cs:137-141`, `ScheduleTick.cs:102-120`.
- *Failure scenario.* The parking is new behaviour, and nothing pins it. A regression could
  re-lease an unusable schedule every 30 s and starve the 50-row tick, which is the stated reason
  for the deviation. Or the lease-lost catch could stop matching `JobErrorCodes.TickLeaseLost` and
  log a lost lease as an `Error`. Neither would fail a test.
- *Fix.*
  - An integration case with a stored unparseable cron, or an unresolvable zone: it fires
    nothing, its `NextDueAt` is about `Now + 1 h`, its lease is released, and
    `SchedulerScheduleUnusable` is logged.
  - A tick whose lease is cleared between steps (the existing fence test drives the statements
    directly, not `RunStep2Async`): it asserts `SchedulerTickLeaseLost` and that the poll
    returned normally.

**R1-6 (minor). `QuietMinuteAsync` sleeps up to 16 s (`Task.Delay`), which the 0020 WATCHLIST
bans.**
- *Where.* `ScheduleTests.cs:735-745`, used by three tests.
- *Failure scenario.* Up to about 48 s of wall-clock wait per run of the class, in the PR tier,
  doubled by the RCSI on/off databases where both run.
- *Fix.* Pin each due occurrence to fixed past minutes instead of "every minute". Use an
  expression naming the minutes two, one and zero before the database's now, as the `late`
  schedule at `ScheduleTests.cs:139-141` already does. Then assert against those instants, so a
  minute boundary during the tick changes nothing.
