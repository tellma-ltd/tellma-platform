# PC-144: Scheduler and the Schedule stack

## Implementer notes

### What was built, and where

- **The tick** (`src/core/Tellma.Core/Jobs/`):
  - `ScheduleTick` (`IScheduleTick`, registered in `JobsComposition`): step 1 rides the poll's round trip (`JobStatements.TickStep1`, a fresh token per poll, the read-only key list only on a `ReadOnly` plan); `Round.RunStep2Async` runs step 2 in the poll's system scope after the round trip.
  - Step 2 (`FireAsync`): per leased row, `Evaluate` (parse + zone) then `ScheduleOccurrences.Compute`; job rows with `Id` = 1-based ordinal; one `ScheduleNextList` row per leased schedule; pauses grouped by reason with their `core.schedule.paused` notices; one guarded `Persist` batch: `TickStep2`, `Pause` per reason, `notifier.Notify`, a wake on commit when anything fired. `Observe` records `tellma.schedules.fired` / `.missed` (tagged by policy) / `.overlap_skipped` and logs each pause.
  - Failures: a lost lease (`Schedule.TickLeaseLost`, `THROW 50422`) logs `SchedulerTickLeaseLost`; any other failure logs `SchedulerTickFailed`; neither fails the poll. An ambiguous-but-committed batch counts nothing.
  - `IScheduleTickRound.RunStep2Async` now takes the poll scope's `IServiceProvider` (the seam's only caller is `JobPoll`; test doubles updated).
- **Occurrences** (`ScheduleOccurrences.cs`): `ScheduleCron` (Cronos `CronFormat.Standard`, zone lookup, tenant zone from the scope's settings) and `ScheduleOccurrences.Compute`, the §7.5 algorithm: owner/retired pause first; past the gap threshold `GetPreviousOccurrence` only (no enumeration); else the due occurrences and Coalesce / ReplayAll (catch-up window) / Skip (newest within 2 × max poll interval); next strictly after the database's now; none → `Exhausted`. Everything is UTC.
- **Statements** (`JobStatements.Schedules.cs`): `TickStep1` (§7.4 text), `TickStep2` (§7.6 text), `Pause`, `UpsertScheduleStates`, `ClearPausedReason`, `RunNow` (§7.8). Records `ScheduleTickRow`, `ScheduleFired`.
- **The stack**:
  - `ScheduleService<TSchedule>` (`[DetailsExtras("state")]`): `take-over` and `run-now` (single target, returns `JobAccepted`) actions; `PreprocessAsync` (new rows run as the caller, unpaused); `ContributeAsync` keeps `core.ScheduleStates` in step on save / activate / deactivate / delete; `AfterCommitAsync` withdraws the paused notices of deleted schedules in chunks of 100; `ContributeDetails` adds the `state` extra; `RefuseOnRecord` tells the client what a built-in refuses.
  - `ScheduleRules<TSchedule>` (`IEntityValidator`): every `Schedules.*` code (`ScheduleValidationCodes`): timing (`CronInvalid`, `CronNeverOccurs`, `TimeZoneUnknown`), handler (`HandlerUnknown`, `HandlerNotSchedulable`), policies (`ReplayAllRequiresAllow`, `ArgumentsTooLarge`), built-in and ownership rules on update (`BuiltInImmutable`, `OwnedByAnotherUser`), delete (`BuiltInImmutable`), actions (`BuiltInAlwaysActive`, `OwnerInactive`, `CronNeverOccurs`, `HandlerUnknown` on activate, `BuiltInImmutable` on take-over, `RunInProgress` on run-now).
  - `JobQueue.RunNow` wraps the statement for the action's (recording) batch and wakes on commit. `CoreFeature` registers the service, the validator and the `core.schedule.paused` notification type. `Schedule` drops `DeleteByQuery` from its stack and gains `[Range(1, int.MaxValue)]` on `CatchUpWindowMinutes`.
- **Notices, logs, metrics, strings**: `JobNotices.SchedulePaused*`, `JobsLog` (`SchedulerTickFailed`, `SchedulerTickLeaseLost`, `SchedulerSchedulePaused`, `SchedulerScheduleUnusable`; `SchedulerHandlerUnknown` reworded to cover user schedules), `JobsMetrics` (three counters), `Strings.resx` / `Strings.ar.resx` (every `Schedules_*` code, the paused notice, the two action labels).
- **Tests**:
  - Unit: `ScheduleOccurrencesTests` (policies, windows, gap degradation without enumeration, exhaustion, fixed zone, New York spring-forward and fall-back, dialect), `ScheduleStatementsTests` (goldens of every statement and both step-1 variants, writes, `MayRunTwice`, bound rows with ordinal ids); `JobChecksTests` message updated; regenerated goldens (stacks, routes, client entities chunk).
  - Integration: `ScheduleTests` over `JobRunDatabase` (real stack-table owners, recorded notices): built-in initialise-then-fire, deactivated rows never leased, overlap with Held / PendingConfirmation and the skip stamp, the OwnerInactive pause (one write, admins' notice), the HandlerRetired pause (owner's notice), two schedules plus a three-occurrence replay in one tick with ids from one range, the read-only tick, step-1 re-run plus the lease fence, the plan of step 1 (band range seek, `IX_ScheduleStates_Due` seek, no scan), the save rules (`CronNeverOccurs`, `Asia/Calcutta` → `TimeZoneUnknown`, `fixture.export` → `HandlerNotSchedulable`), save keeps the state in step, take-over → activate withdraws the notice (with `OwnerInactive` refused first), delete withdraws after commit, built-in delete/deactivate refusals, run-now as caller / as platform, `RunInProgress`, the `state` extra (empty for an invisible id) and the `permissions` extra on built-ins. The pause and run-now write `core.Schedules` / `core.Jobs` from batches whose `CallerAuthority` excludes them, under the fixture's real `StackTableOwners`.
  - `JobRunDatabase`: reset now removes user schedules and returns the seeded built-in to uninitialised; a `Schedulers` role and a filtered `Viewers` role; `UserScopeAsync` (a stepped-up user request scope, since `core.Schedule:Save` is sensitive); `CommittedNotices.Withdraw(batch, …)` records on commit; a `test.sweep` handler (`Schedulable`, `RunsWhenReadOnly`).

### Deviations

1. The read-only variant of step 1 reads the key list's `[Id]` column (`StringList`'s only column); the spec says `[Value]`.
2. Step 1 also selects `Name`, `Name2`, `Name3`, for the paused notice's arguments.
3. The state upsert also clears `LeaseToken` / `LeaseExpiresAt`, so a save during a tick fails that tick's fence rather than being overwritten by a stale advance.
4. `DeleteByQuery` is removed from the schedules' stack (a query delete names no ids for the built-in rule; D-132 pattern).
5. The service and the validator are generic over the leaf (`ScheduleService<TSchedule>`, `ScheduleRules<TSchedule>`); the rules live in the validator component, not the service, so a distribution's leaf keeps them.
6. The pause is one fixed statement per reason, stamped by the system user; the state needs no second update (step 2 already nulls `NextDueAt`).
7. `fixture.export` stands in for `core.export` (not yet registered) in the not-schedulable test.
8. A step-2 failure is logged and never fails the poll; the seam takes the poll scope's `IServiceProvider`.
9. A schedule the host cannot evaluate (unparseable cron, unresolvable zone) is parked: `NextDueAt = now + 1 h`, logged `SchedulerScheduleUnusable`, rather than left to its lapsing lease, which would re-lease it every 30 s and starve the 50-row tick.
10. Save recomputes `NextDueAt` only for new rows and rows whose cron or zone changed (a rename keeps a due occurrence due); activate only for targets inactive as loaded; deactivate nulls only active targets.
11. `HandlerUnknown` / `HandlerNotSchedulable` are not applied to built-ins on save or activate (their key is fixed by the feature and checked at composition).
12. `ArgumentsTooLarge` is measured in UTF-8 bytes against `JobLimits.MaxHandlerArgumentsBytes`, argument `maxBytes`.
13. `[Range(1, int.MaxValue)]` on `CatchUpWindowMinutes` (the column's check constraint, surfaced as a field error).
14. The paused notice goes to the administrators when the reason is `OwnerInactive` or the owner is the system user, else to the owner.
15. The gap path counts no missed occurrences (the gap check already reports the gap).
16. A delete appends an explicit `DELETE` of the deleted schedules' states, declaring `core.ScheduleStates` among the batch's writes; the rows already left by the FK cascade, which the delete plan does not declare for a non-sibling satellite (the change-tracking audit caught it).
17. Two action-label strings (`Core_Schedule_Action_TakeOver`, `Core_Schedule_Action_RunNow`) were added in English and Arabic.

### Open risks

- **Double fire on an ambiguous step 2 (relates to D-146).** If step 2's commit is ambiguous and the probe cannot confirm it, the leases lapse and the next tick recomputes from the old state. Skip schedules are protected by the overlap predicate; Allow and ReplayAll schedules could fire the same occurrence twice. The scheduler adds one more writer exposed to the D-146 gap but does not widen it; not closed here.
- The tenant zone is read from the poll scope's negotiated settings at fire time; a zone the host cannot resolve falls back to UTC silently for schedules without their own zone (the startup check judges tenant zones).
- The step-1 plan test depends on the optimizer choosing seeks with 20 000 states; it is in the PR integration tier like the claim's plan test.

### What reviewers should examine hardest

- `ScheduleOccurrences.Compute` against §7.5, especially the Skip window, DST transitions (Cronos zone overloads) and the strict-after-now next due time.
- `TickStep2`: the fence count against the `next` rows, the sequence range and `ROW_NUMBER` ordering, `LastSkippedAt` only when rows existed but none fired, and `LastJobId` = the max fired id.
- `ScheduleService.ContributeAsync` per persist kind (the recording-batch append path) and the activate path that clears the reason and withdraws the notice in the same batch.
- `ScheduleRules.ValidateActionAsync`: the first-round loads (inactive owners, running jobs) and which codes apply to built-ins.
